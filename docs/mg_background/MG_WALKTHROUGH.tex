in the RutticeQCD repository.} (``the note'') states. The note gives the method and its results in compact form; here every formula

followed with nothing more than standard graduate coursework in lattice QCD. The sections follow the
note: each heading names the section of the note it derives (Secs.~II--IV), so the two can be read
side by side. The last two sections go beyond the note: the solves of HMC, derived from the path
integral (the note leaves HMC to future work), and a map from each step to the
code. Nothing here is new with respect to the note and the code.

\section{The operator and Pauli--Villars preconditioning (note Sec.~II.B)}

\subsection{Ingredients}
The field $\psi$ lives on the four-dimensional lattice and carries a fifth index
$s=0,\dots,L_s-1$; each $\psi_s$ is a Dirac field. The chiral projectors are
\begin{equation}
P_\pm=\frac{1\pm\gamma_5}{2},\qquad P_\pm^2=P_\pm,\qquad P_+P_-=0,\qquad P_++P_-=1 .
\end{equation}
The Wilson hopping term and the Wilson operator of mass $-M_5$ are
\begin{align}
(K\psi)(x)&=\sum_{\mu=1}^{4}\Big[(1-\gamma_\mu)U_\mu(x)\psi(x+\hat\mu)
+(1+\gamma_\mu)U_\mu\dg(x-\hat\mu)\psi(x-\hat\mu)\Big],\\
W&=(4-M_5)-\tfrac12K .
\end{align}
$W$ acts on every slice $s$ alike and does not mix slices.


\subsection{The free spectrum of $W$}
\label{sec:free}
The spectrum of $W$ on the free field ($U_\mu=1$) is needed twice: for the spectra of the note
(Sec.~II.A) and for $D_-$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:dminus}). Take a plane wave $\psi(x)=e^{ip\cdot x}u$ with
a constant spinor $u$:
\begin{align}
(K\psi)(x)&=\sum_\mu\Big[(1-\gamma_\mu)e^{ip_\mu}+(1+\gamma_\mu)e^{-ip_\mu}\Big]\psi(x)\\
&=\sum_\mu\Big[\big(e^{ip_\mu}+e^{-ip_\mu}\big)-\gamma_\mu\big(e^{ip_\mu}-e^{-ip_\mu}\big)\Big]\psi(x)\\
&=\sum_\mu\Big[2\cos p_\mu-2i\gamma_\mu\sin p_\mu\Big]\psi(x),\\
W\psi&=\Big[(4-M_5)-\sum_\mu\cos p_\mu+i\sum_\mu\gamma_\mu\sin p_\mu\Big]\psi\\
&=\Big[-M_5+\sum_\mu(1-\cos p_\mu)+i\sum_\mu\gamma_\mu\sin p_\mu\Big]\psi .
\end{align}
The matrix $G=\sum_\mu\gamma_\mu\sin p_\mu$ is Hermitian, and since the $\gamma_\mu$ anticommute,
\begin{equation}
G^2=\sum_{\mu,\nu}\gamma_\mu\gamma_\nu\sin p_\mu\sin p_\nu
=\sum_\mu\gamma_\mu^2\sin^2p_\mu+\sum_{\mu<\nu}\{\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\nu\}\sin p_\mu\sin p_\nu
=\sum_\mu\sin^2p_\mu ,
\end{equation}
so its eigenvalues are $\pm(\sum_\mu\sin^2p_\mu)^{1/2}$. The eigenvalues of $W$ are therefore
\begin{equation}
w(p)=-M_5+\sum_\mu(1-\cos p_\mu)\pm i\Big(\sum_\mu\sin^2p_\mu\Big)^{1/2}.
\label{eq:wfree}
\end{equation}
Their real parts run from $-M_5$ ($p=0$) to $8-M_5$ ($p_\mu=\pi$); the zeros of the real part at
small $|p|$ are the physical domain-wall modes.

\subsection{The operator in Brower--Neff--Orginos form (note Sec.~II.B.1)}
With $b$, $c$ the M\"obius parameters, define
\begin{equation}
D_+=1+bW,\qquad D_-=cW-1 .
\end{equation}
The operator at quark mass $m$ acts slice by slice as
\begin{equation}
(D\psi)_s=D_+\psi_s+D_-\big(\Pm\psi_{s+1}+\Pp\psi_{s-1}\big),
\qquad \psi_{L_s}\equiv-m\psi_0,\qquad \psi_{-1}\equiv-m\psi_{L_s-1}.
\label{eq:Dslice}
\end{equation}
For $L_s=4$ the matrix in $s$ is
\begin{equation}
D=\begin{pmatrix}
D_+ & D_-\Pm & 0 & -mD_-\Pp\\
D_-\Pp & D_+ & D_-\Pm & 0\\
0 & D_-\Pp & D_+ & D_-\Pm\\
-mD_-\Pm & 0 & D_-\Pp & D_+
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The physical quark field is $q=\Pm\psi_0+\Pp\psi_{L_s-1}$; the runs use $b=1.5$, $c=0.5$,
$L_s=8$.

\subsection{Splitting off the fifth-dimension hop}
Define the hop in the fifth dimension, with a minus sign,
\begin{equation}
(Y\psi)_s=-\Pm\psi_{s+1}-\Pp\psi_{s-1}\qquad\text{(same wrap-around)}.
\end{equation}
Comparing with Eq.~\eqref{eq:Dslice}, the off-diagonal part of $D$ is $-D_-Y$, with $D_-$ on the
\emph{left}:
\begin{equation}
D=D_+-D_-Y .
\end{equation}
Now insert $D_\pm$ and then $W$, one substitution per line:
\begin{align}
D&=(1+bW)-(cW-1)Y\\
&=1+bW-cWY+Y\\
&=1+Y+W\,(b-cY)\\
&=1+Y+\Big[(4-M_5)-\tfrac12K\Big](b-cY)\\
&=\underbrace{\big[1+b(4-M_5)\big]}_{d_+}+\underbrace{\big[1-c(4-M_5)\big]}_{d_-}Y
-\tfrac12K\,(b-cY)\\
&=C-\tfrac12K\,(b-cY),\qquad C\equiv d_++d_-Y .
\label{eq:Dsplit}
\end{align}
$C$ contains no four-dimensional hop: it acts within one site, as an $L_s\times L_s$ matrix in
$s$. All the gauge field sits in $-\frac12K(b-cY)$. The Pauli--Villars operator is the same
operator at unit mass,
\begin{equation}
\Dpv=D\big|_{m=1},\qquad \Cpv=C\big|_{m=1}.
\end{equation}


\subsection{The $\Gfive$-transformation (note Sec.~II.B.2)}
\label{sec:g5}
For the Wilson operator $\gamma_5W\gamma_5=W\dg$. Domain-wall fermions use
$\Gfive=\gamma_5R$, $(R\psi)_s=\psi_{L_s-1-s}$. We derive how each block transforms.

\paragraph{$K$.} Read off the matrix elements of $K$ between neighbors:
\begin{equation}
K_{x,x+\hat\mu}=(1-\gamma_\mu)U_\mu(x),\qquad K_{x+\hat\mu,x}=(1+\gamma_\mu)U_\mu\dg(x).
\end{equation}
The adjoint swaps the indices and conjugates each block (spin and color commute, and the
$\gamma_\mu$ are Hermitian):
\begin{align}
(K\dg)_{x,x+\hat\mu}&=\big(K_{x+\hat\mu,x}\big)\dg=U_\mu(x)\,(1+\gamma_\mu)=(1+\gamma_\mu)U_\mu(x),\\
(K\dg)_{x+\hat\mu,x}&=\big(K_{x,x+\hat\mu}\big)\dg=(1-\gamma_\mu)U_\mu\dg(x).
\end{align}
So $K\dg$ is $K$ with $\gamma_\mu\to-\gamma_\mu$. Since $\gamma_5\gamma_\mu\gamma_5=-\gamma_\mu$,
\begin{equation}
\gamma_5K\gamma_5=K\dg,\qquad \gamma_5W\gamma_5=W\dg .
\end{equation}
$W$ acts on every slice alike, so it commutes with $R$: $\Gfive W\Gfive=W\dg$, and the same for
$D_\pm=$ functions of $W$: $\Gfive D_\pm\Gfive=D_\pm\dg$.

\paragraph{$Y$.} Write $Y$ as a matrix in $s$ ($L_s=4$), from $(Y\psi)_s=-\Pm\psi_{s+1}-\Pp\psi_{s-1}$
and the wrap-around $\psi_{L_s}=-m\psi_0$, $\psi_{-1}=-m\psi_{L_s-1}$:
\begin{equation}
Y=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\Pm&0&m\Pp\\-\Pp&0&-\Pm&0\\0&-\Pp&0&-\Pm\\m\Pm&0&-\Pp&0\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
$\gamma_5$ commutes with $P_\pm$, so $\Gfive Y\Gfive=RYR$, whose entry $(s,s')$ is
$Y_{L_s-1-s,\,L_s-1-s'}$: the matrix rotated by $180^\circ$. Compare with $Y\dg$, whose entry
$(s,s')$ is $(Y_{s',s})\dg$:
\begin{align}
(RYR)_{0,1}&=Y_{3,2}=-\Pp,& (Y\dg)_{0,1}&=(Y_{1,0})\dg=-\Pp,\\
(RYR)_{1,0}&=Y_{2,3}=-\Pm,& (Y\dg)_{1,0}&=(Y_{0,1})\dg=-\Pm,\\
(RYR)_{0,3}&=Y_{3,0}=m\Pm,& (Y\dg)_{0,3}&=(Y_{3,0})\dg=m\Pm,
\end{align}
and so on for every entry. Hence $\Gfive Y\Gfive=Y\dg$.

\paragraph{$W$ and $Y$ do not commute.} $K$ contains $\gamma_\mu$, and
$\gamma_\mu\Pp=\Pm\gamma_\mu$, so a hop of $K$ exchanges the two projectors of $Y$: $[Y,W]\neq0$.
This one fact makes the M\"obius case different from Shamir's.

\paragraph{Consequence: $\Gfive D\Gfive\neq D\dg$.}
From the three rules above and $D=D_+-D_-Y$,
\begin{align}
\Gfive D\Gfive&=\Gfive D_+\Gfive-\Gfive D_-\Gfive\,\Gfive Y\Gfive=D_+\dg-D_-\dg Y\dg,\\
D\dg&=D_+\dg-(D_-Y)\dg=D_+\dg-Y\dg D_-\dg .
\end{align}
The order of the last product differs. Subtracting,
\begin{align}
\Gfive D\Gfive-D\dg&=Y\dg D_-\dg-D_-\dg Y\dg\\
&=Y\dg(cW\dg-1)-(cW\dg-1)Y\dg\\
&=c\,\big(Y\dg W\dg-W\dg Y\dg\big)=c\,[Y\dg,W\dg],
\end{align}
which is of order $c$ (zero for Shamir, $0.15$ relative on a $12^4$ stout configuration at
$c=0.5$).


\paragraph{The exact relation.}
$D_+$ and $D_-$ are functions of $W$, so they commute. Factor $D_-$ out to the left (formally):
\begin{equation}
D=D_-K_D,\qquad K_D=D_-^{-1}D_+-Y .
\end{equation}
$K_D$ is $\Gfive$-Hermitian:
\begin{align}
\Gfive K_D\Gfive&=\Gfive D_-^{-1}\Gfive\,\Gfive D_+\Gfive-\Gfive Y\Gfive\\
&=(D_-\dg)^{-1}D_+\dg-Y\dg\\
&=D_+\dg(D_-\dg)^{-1}-Y\dg\qquad(D_\pm\dg\text{ commute})\\
&=(D_-^{-1}D_+)\dg-Y\dg=K_D\dg .
\end{align}
So
\begin{align}
D\dg&=K_D\dg D_-\dg\\
&=\Gfive K_D\Gfive\,D_-\dg\\
&=\Gfive D_-^{-1}D\,\Gfive\,D_-\dg ,
\end{align}
and multiplying by $D_-\Gfive$ from the left,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;D_-\,\Gfive\,D\dg=D\,\Gfive\,D_-\dg\;}
\end{equation}
with no inverse left. $D_-$ itself cannot be inverted in practice: $D_-=c(W-1/c)$ is $c$ times a
Wilson operator of mass $-M_5-1/c=-3$, whose spectrum surrounds the origin (GCR stalls at
$|r|^2/|b|^2\approx0.74$).


\subsection{The site-diagonal part $C$ and its inverse (note Sec.~II.B.3)}
\label{sec:Cinv}
$Y$ maps $\Pp\psi$ to $\Pp\psi$ and $\Pm\psi$ to $\Pm\psi$, so $C$ splits into a block on
$\psi^+=\Pp\psi$ and one on $\psi^-=\Pm\psi$. On $\psi^+$, $Y$ brings $\psi^+_{s-1}$ to slice $s$
with the factor $-1$, and across the wall the factor is $-(-m)=+m$:
\begin{equation}
(C\psi)^+_s=\begin{cases}
d_+\psi^+_0+m\,d_-\psi^+_{L_s-1}, & s=0,\\[2pt]
d_+\psi^+_s-d_-\psi^+_{s-1}, & s\ge1 .
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
To solve $C^+x=r$, take the rows $s\ge1$ first:
\begin{equation}
d_+x_s-d_-x_{s-1}=r_s\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x_s=\frac{r_s+d_-x_{s-1}}{d_+}.
\end{equation}
Each $x_s$ is then affine in the unknown $x_0$. With $q=d_-/d_+$ we claim
\begin{equation}
x_s=p_s+q^sx_0,\qquad p_0=0,\qquad p_s=\frac{r_s+d_-p_{s-1}}{d_+}.
\end{equation}
Check by induction:
\begin{align}
x_s&=\frac{r_s+d_-x_{s-1}}{d_+}\\
&=\frac{r_s+d_-(p_{s-1}+q^{s-1}x_0)}{d_+}\\
&=\frac{r_s+d_-p_{s-1}}{d_+}+\frac{d_-}{d_+}q^{s-1}x_0\\
&=p_s+q^sx_0 .
\end{align}
The remaining row $s=0$ fixes $x_0$:
\begin{align}
d_+x_0+m\,d_-x_{L_s-1}&=r_0\\
d_+x_0+m\,d_-\big(p_{L_s-1}+q^{L_s-1}x_0\big)&=r_0\\
\big(d_++m\,d_-q^{L_s-1}\big)x_0&=r_0-m\,d_-p_{L_s-1}\\
x_0&=\frac{r_0-m\,d_-p_{L_s-1}}{d_++m\,d_-q^{L_s-1}} .
\end{align}
The block on $\psi^-$ is the same with $s\to L_s-1-s$. So $C^{-1}$ costs $O(L_s)$ per site, is
exact, and does not depend on the gauge field. $C\dg$ has the transposed blocks and is inverted
the same way from the other end.


\subsection{The Pauli--Villars preconditioned operator (note Sec.~II.B.4)}
Kanamori, Chen and Matsufuru multiply $D\psi=\eta$ by $(\Dpv\Cpv^{-1})\dg$ from the left and set
$\psi=C^{-1}y$:
\begin{align}
D\psi&=\eta\\
DC^{-1}y&=\eta\\
(\Dpv\Cpv^{-1})\dg DC^{-1}y&=(\Dpv\Cpv^{-1})\dg\eta\\
A'y&=(\Dpv\Cpv^{-1})\dg\eta,\qquad A'\equiv(\Dpv\Cpv^{-1})\dg DC^{-1}.
\end{align}
\paragraph{At $m=1$ it is Hermitian positive.} Then $D=\Dpv$ and $C=\Cpv$, so with
$X=\Dpv\Cpv^{-1}$,
\begin{equation}
A'=X\dg X,\qquad z\dg A'z=\|Xz\|^2>0\ \ (z\neq0,\ X\ \text{invertible}).
\end{equation}
\paragraph{At $m<1$ it is a perturbation of that.} $m$ enters $D$ only in the two corners of the
matrix in $s$ ($-mD_-\Pp$ and $-mD_-\Pm$), and $C$ only in its corner $m\,d_-$:
\begin{equation}
D-\Dpv=(1-m)\,D_-\,\big(\Pp\ \text{in corner }(0,L_s-1)+\Pm\ \text{in corner }(L_s-1,0)\big),
\end{equation}
a term localized at the walls. So $A'$ is $X\dg X$ plus a wall term; its eigenvalues stay in the
right half-plane (note Figs.~2 and~7), which is what a Krylov method and a polynomial smoother need.

\subsection{Neff's $\alpha$ (note Sec.~II.B.6)}
Neff's operator is $D^{(\alpha)}=DQ$ with the site-local
\begin{equation}
Q_0=\Pm+\alpha\Pp,\qquad Q_s=\alpha\ (0<s<L_s-1),\qquad Q_{L_s-1}=\Pp+\alpha\Pm .
\end{equation}
$Q$ is Hermitian and $Q^{-1}$ is the same with $\alpha\to1/\alpha$. If $D^{(\alpha)}\psi^{(\alpha)}=\eta$, then
\begin{equation}
\eta=D^{(\alpha)}\psi^{(\alpha)}=DQ\psi^{(\alpha)}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\psi=Q\psi^{(\alpha)}
\ \text{solves}\ D\psi=\eta .
\end{equation}
$Q$ leaves $\Pm\psi_0$ and $\Pp\psi_{L_s-1}$ alone, so the quark propagator is unchanged.

\section{Even-odd forms (note Sec.~II.C)}

\subsection{Blocks}
Order the sites even first. $K$ connects nearest neighbors, which have opposite parity, so in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Dsplit} the diagonal blocks come from $C$ alone:
\begin{equation}
D=\begin{pmatrix}\Ce & D_{eo}\\ D_{oe} & \Co\end{pmatrix},\qquad
D_{pq}=-\tfrac12K_{pq}(b-cY)\quad(p\neq q).
\end{equation}
$\Ce$ and $\Co$ are the same matrix $C$ on the even and the odd sites.

\subsection{The Schur complement}
Solve $D\psi=\eta$ block by block. The odd rows:
\begin{align}
D_{oe}\psi_e+\Co\psi_o&=\eta_o\\
\psi_o&=\Co^{-1}\big(\eta_o-D_{oe}\psi_e\big).
\label{eq:psio}
\end{align}
Insert into the even rows:
\begin{align}
\Ce\psi_e+D_{eo}\psi_o&=\eta_e\\
\Ce\psi_e+D_{eo}\Co^{-1}\big(\eta_o-D_{oe}\psi_e\big)&=\eta_e\\
\big(\Ce-D_{eo}\Co^{-1}D_{oe}\big)\psi_e&=\eta_e-D_{eo}\Co^{-1}\eta_o\\
S\psi_e&=\eta'_e,\qquad S\equiv\Ce-D_{eo}\Co^{-1}D_{oe},\quad \eta'_e\equiv\eta_e-D_{eo}\Co^{-1}\eta_o .
\label{eq:schur}
\end{align}

\subsection{$S^{-1}=\pi D^{-1}\iota$}
Let $\iota$ put an even-site field on the whole lattice (zero on the odd sites) and $\pi$ take
the even part:
\begin{equation}
\iota y=\begin{pmatrix}y\\0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\pi\begin{pmatrix}\psi_e\\\psi_o\end{pmatrix}=\psi_e .
\end{equation}
For $\eta=\iota\eta_e$ we have $\eta_o=0$, so $\eta'_e=\eta_e$ and Eq.~\eqref{eq:schur} reads
$S\psi_e=\eta_e$. Hence
\begin{equation}
S^{-1}\eta_e=\psi_e=\pi D^{-1}\iota\eta_e\quad\Longrightarrow\quad S^{-1}=\pi D^{-1}\iota .
\label{eq:Sinv}
\end{equation}
The same for $D\dg$: its block form is $\begin{pmatrix}\Ce\dg & D_{oe}\dg\\ D_{eo}\dg & \Co\dg\end{pmatrix}$,
and elimination gives $\Ce\dg-D_{oe}\dg(\Co\dg)^{-1}D_{eo}\dg=S\dg$, so $S^{-\dagger}=\pi D^{-\dagger}\iota$.

\subsection{The Pauli--Villars preconditioned forms}
$S$ is not suitable for a Krylov method: its spectrum surrounds the origin. Following Kanamori,
Chen and Matsufuru we multiply from the left by the adjoint of the Pauli--Villars factor and
divide the site part out. Two placements of $\Ce$ give two forms:
\begin{align}
\AR&=(\Spv\Cpve^{-1})\dg\,(S\Ce^{-1}),\\
\AL&=(\Cpve^{-1}\Spv)\dg\,(\Ce^{-1}S).
\end{align}
For $m=1$ both become $X\dg X$, Hermitian positive; for $m<1$ they are perturbations of it, with
all eigenvalues in the right half-plane.

\paragraph{Right form, step by step.} Start from $S\psi_e=\eta'_e$ and substitute $\psi_e=\Ce^{-1}y$:
\begin{align}
S\Ce^{-1}y&=\eta'_e\\
(\Spv\Cpve^{-1})\dg S\Ce^{-1}y&=(\Spv\Cpve^{-1})\dg\eta'_e\\
\AR\,y&=\rho_R,\qquad \rho_R\equiv(\Spv\Cpve^{-1})\dg\eta'_e,\\
\psi_e&=\Ce^{-1}y,\qquad \psi_o=\Co^{-1}(\eta_o-D_{oe}\psi_e).
\end{align}

\paragraph{Left form with $\alpha$ in $D$ only.} For $DQ$ the blocks are $\Ce Q_e$, $D_{eo}Q_o$,
$D_{oe}Q_e$, $\Co Q_o$, and the Schur complement is
\begin{align}
S^{(\alpha)}&=\Ce Q_e-D_{eo}Q_o(\Co Q_o)^{-1}D_{oe}Q_e\\
&=\Ce Q_e-D_{eo}Q_oQ_o^{-1}\Co^{-1}D_{oe}Q_e\\
&=\big(\Ce-D_{eo}\Co^{-1}D_{oe}\big)Q_e\\
&=SQ_e .
\end{align}
So the left Schur factor of $DQ$ is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal K^{(\alpha)}=(\Ce Q_e)^{-1}SQ_e=Q_e^{-1}\Ce^{-1}SQ_e=Q_e^{-1}\mathcal KQ_e,\qquad \mathcal K\equiv\Ce^{-1}S,
\label{eq:Ksim}
\end{equation}
and the left form becomes $\AL^{(\alpha)}=(\Cpve^{-1}\Spv)\dg Q_e^{-1}(\Ce^{-1}S)Q_e$. In the right
form the two $Q_e$ cancel: $SQ_e(\Ce Q_e)^{-1}=S\Ce^{-1}$, so $\AR$ does not see $\alpha$.

\section{The factorized coarse-grid correction and the prolongation (note Sec.~III.A)}

\subsection{The factorized coarse correction}
\paragraph{The inverse of the right form.} Invert the product one factor at a time:
\begin{align}
\AR^{-1}&=\big[(\Spv\Cpve^{-1})\dg(S\Ce^{-1})\big]^{-1}\\
&=(S\Ce^{-1})^{-1}\,\big[(\Spv\Cpve^{-1})\dg\big]^{-1}\\
&=\Ce S^{-1}\,\big[(\Cpve^{-1})\dg\Spv\dg\big]^{-1}\\
&=\Ce S^{-1}\,(\Spv\dg)^{-1}\Cpve\dg\\
&=\Ce\;\pi D^{-1}\iota\;\;\pi(\Dpv\dg)^{-1}\iota\;\;\Cpve\dg ,
\label{eq:ARinv}
\end{align}
where Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sinv} was used twice. The inverse is a product of the inverses of two
\emph{one-hop} operators on the whole lattice, $D$ and $\Dpv\dg$, and site-local factors.

\paragraph{Galerkin replacement.} With a prolongation $\Pro$ (coarse to whole lattice), replace
each inverse by its Galerkin approximation:
\begin{equation}
X^{-1}\approx\Pro\big(\Pro\dg X\Pro\big)^{-1}\Pro\dg .
\end{equation}
Both coarse operators $\Dc=\Pro\dg D\Pro$ and $\Pro\dg\Dpv\dg\Pro$ are nearest-neighbor on the
coarse lattice, because $D$ is one hop and every aggregate contains the whole fifth dimension.

\paragraph{Why the Galerkin approximation is exact on $\operatorname{range}\Pro$.} For any $X$
with $\Pro\dg X\Pro$ invertible and any coarse vector $c$,
\begin{equation}
\Pro\big(\Pro\dg X\Pro\big)^{-1}\Pro\dg X\,(\Pro c)=\Pro\big(\Pro\dg X\Pro\big)^{-1}\big(\Pro\dg X\Pro\big)c=\Pro c .
\end{equation}
So the approximation of $X^{-1}$ inverts $X$ exactly on vectors $X\Pro c$. This is used in the
exactness proof below.

\paragraph{The size of the coarse stencil.} An operator that reaches $k$ hops on the fine lattice
connects a fine site to sites at most $k$ hops away. With blocks at least two sites wide, two coarse
sites can then be coupled only if their coordinates differ by at most $1$ in at most $k$ of the four
directions. Counting the coarse neighbors that differ in exactly $j$ directions ($\binom4j$ choices
of directions, $2^j$ signs):
\begin{align}
k=1:&\quad 1+\tbinom41 2=1+8=9,\\
k=2:&\quad 1+8+\tbinom42 2^2=1+8+24=33,\\
k\ge4:&\quad \sum_{j=0}^{4}\tbinom4j2^j=3^4=81 .
\end{align}
$D$ and $\Dpv$ are one hop: 9 points each. $\AR$ reaches four hops ($S$ is two hops, and $\AR$
contains $S$ and $\Spv\dg$): 81 points. That is why the correction factorizes instead of
coarsening $\AR$ directly.

The correction of a residual $r$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:ARinv} read from the right) is
\begin{equation}
e=O_L\,\pi\Pro\,\Dc^{-1}\Pro\dg\iota\;O_M\;\pi\Pro\big(\Pro\dg\Dpv\dg\Pro\big)^{-1}\Pro\dg\iota\;O_R\,r
\end{equation}
with the site-local factors
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule

\subsection{Aggregation and the chirality split}
The fine lattice is divided into $4^4$ blocks, each with the whole fifth dimension; each block is
one coarse site. Every test vector $v$ is split by $\Gfive=\gamma_5R$, $(R\psi)_s=\psi_{L_s-1-s}$:
\begin{equation}
v_\pm=\tfrac12(1\pm\Gfive)v,\qquad \Gfive v_\pm=\pm v_\pm .
\end{equation}
With $n_v$ test vectors a coarse site has $2n_v$ components, the $+$ parts first. Each column of
$\Pro$ is then an eigenvector of $\Gfive$, so with $\Sigma=\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf 1_{n_v},-\mathbf
1_{n_v})$ per coarse site
\begin{equation}
\Gfive\Pro=\Pro\Sigma .
\end{equation}
\paragraph{Half the storage.} Write the condition $\Gfive v_\pm=\pm v_\pm$ out slice by slice:
\begin{align}
(\Gfive v_\pm)_s&=\gamma_5\,(Rv_\pm)_s=\gamma_5\,v_\pm(L_s-1-s)\\
\pm v_\pm(s)&=\gamma_5\,v_\pm(L_s-1-s)\\
v_\pm(L_s-1-s)&=\pm\gamma_5\,v_\pm(s) .
\end{align}
So each part is fixed by its slices $s<L_s/2$; $\Pro$ is stored only there (``folded''), in half
precision. Restriction and prolongation unfold on the fly: for the restriction of a fine field $f$,
\begin{equation}
\big(\Pro\dg f\big)_{X,k}=\sum_{x\in X}\sum_{s<L_s/2}\Pro_{x,s;k}^{\,*}\,
\big(f_s\pm\gamma_5f_{L_s-1-s}\big)
\end{equation}
(the sign that of the chirality of column $k$), which is what \code{fold\_restrict\_tiled\_kernel}
computes.

\subsection{Orthonormalization per block: CholeskyQR2}
In each block and chirality the columns $w_1,\dots,w_n$ ($n=2\times$ the number of filtered
vectors, see below) are made orthonormal. Collect them as the columns of $W$ and write the QR
decomposition with a positive diagonal of $R$:
\begin{align}
W&=QR\\
W\dg W&=R\dg Q\dg QR=R\dg R\\
G\equiv W\dg W&=R\dg R\quad\text{(Cholesky)}\\
Q&=WR^{-1} .
\end{align}
So one Gram matrix ($n\times n$), one Cholesky factor and one triangular solve give $Q$; the
factorization is unique, so this $Q$ equals what modified Gram--Schmidt produces. In floating
point one pass loses orthogonality like the square of the condition number of $W$, so the pass
is done twice (CholeskyQR2): the second $G$ is close to the identity and repairs the first. Each
pass reads $W$ twice, against one sweep per pair of columns for Gram--Schmidt (800 sweeps for
$n=40$). Where a pivot of the first pass falls below $10^{-12}$ of its diagonal (nearly dependent
columns), the code falls back to Gram--Schmidt for those blocks.

\subsection{The basis $\{s,\hat t\}$ and why the correction is exact}
\label{sec:exact}
The setup produces vectors $y_j$ on the even sites (next section). Each is lifted to the whole
lattice in two ways, one for each coarse solve:
\begin{align}
s&=\big(\Ce^{-1}y,\;-\Co^{-1}D_{oe}\Ce^{-1}y\big),\\
\hat t&=\big(a,\;-(\Cpvo\dg)^{-1}D_{\mathrm{PV},eo}\dg a\big),\qquad a=\pi Ds .
\end{align}
\paragraph{$Ds$ vanishes on the odd sites.}
\begin{align}
Ds&=\begin{pmatrix}\Ce&D_{eo}\\D_{oe}&\Co\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}\Ce^{-1}y\\-\Co^{-1}D_{oe}\Ce^{-1}y\end{pmatrix}\\
&=\begin{pmatrix}\Ce\Ce^{-1}y-D_{eo}\Co^{-1}D_{oe}\Ce^{-1}y\\
D_{oe}\Ce^{-1}y-\Co\Co^{-1}D_{oe}\Ce^{-1}y\end{pmatrix}\\
&=\begin{pmatrix}\big(\Ce-D_{eo}\Co^{-1}D_{oe}\big)\Ce^{-1}y\\0\end{pmatrix}\\
&=\iota\,S\Ce^{-1}y .
\end{align}
In the same way $\Dpv\dg\hat t=\iota\Spv\dg a$.
\paragraph{Exactness of the right form.} Let $s,\hat t\in\operatorname{range}\Pro$ and solve both
coarse systems exactly. The Galerkin map $\Pro(\Pro\dg X\Pro)^{-1}\Pro\dg X$ is the identity on
$\operatorname{range}\Pro$. Follow $r=\AR y$ through the correction ($O_R=\Cpve\dg$, $O_M=1$,
$O_L=\Ce$):
\begin{align}
O_Rr&=\Cpve\dg(\Spv\Cpve^{-1})\dg S\Ce^{-1}y\\
&=\Cpve\dg(\Cpve^{-1})\dg\Spv\dg S\Ce^{-1}y\\
&=\Spv\dg\,a,\qquad (a=S\Ce^{-1}y=\pi Ds)\\
\iota O_Rr&=\iota\Spv\dg a=\Dpv\dg\hat t\\
\Pro\big(\Pro\dg\Dpv\dg\Pro\big)^{-1}\Pro\dg\,\Dpv\dg\hat t&=\hat t
\qquad(\hat t\in\operatorname{range}\Pro)\\
\pi\hat t&=a=\pi Ds\\
\iota O_M\pi\hat t&=\iota\pi Ds=Ds\qquad(Ds\ \text{is zero on the odd sites})\\
\Pro\Dc^{-1}\Pro\dg Ds&=s\qquad(s\in\operatorname{range}\Pro)\\
e=O_L\pi s&=\Ce\Ce^{-1}y=y .
\end{align}
So the correction inverts $\AR$ exactly on the span of the $y_j$. In practice $\Pro$ is in half
precision and the coarse solves are inexact (relative residual $10^{-1}$), and the left form is
not exact at all; the cycle converges nonetheless.


\paragraph{Why the left form is not exact.} Repeat the argument for $\AL$ with $\alpha=1$ and
$r=\AL y'$, $y'=\Ce^{-1}y$ ($O_R=1$, $O_M=\Ce\Cpve\dg$, $O_L=1$):
\begin{align}
r&=(\Cpve^{-1}\Spv)\dg\Ce^{-1}S\,\Ce^{-1}y=\Spv\dg(\Cpve^{-1})\dg\Ce^{-1}a,\qquad a=S\Ce^{-1}y,\\
\iota O_Rr&=\iota\Spv\dg u,\qquad u\equiv(\Cpve\dg)^{-1}\Ce^{-1}a .
\end{align}
The first solve returns the Pauli--Villars lift of $u$, not of $a$. $u$ differs from $a$ by the
site-local matrix $(\Cpve\dg)^{-1}\Ce^{-1}$, which mixes the slices $s$ and the spin, so its lift
is in general not in the span of the $\hat t_j$ made from $a$. The correction is then only
approximate. In practice the left form converges within a few iterations of the right form (note
Sec.~V), so the cycle does not need the exactness.

\subsection{The coarse operators by probing}
$\Dc=\Pro\dg D\Pro$ has on each coarse site $X$ a $2n_v\times2n_v$ diagonal block $A(X)$ and one link
block $B_\mu^\pm(X)$ per direction: nine blocks. They are found column by column. For component $k$,
put a unit vector $\epsilon_k$ on \emph{every} coarse site and prolong:
$\phi=\Pro\epsilon_k$. Split $D=C+H$ ($H$ the hops) and $H$ by leg (direction and sign), and split
each leg into the part that stays inside its block and the part that crosses to the neighbor
block:
\begin{align}
D\phi&=C\phi+\sum_{\text{legs }l}H_l\phi\\
&=\underbrace{C\phi+\sum_lH_l^{\rm in}\phi}_{\text{stays in }X}
+\sum_l\underbrace{H_l^{\rm cross}\phi}_{\text{comes from }X\pm\hat\mu} .
\end{align}
Restricting, $\big(\Pro\dg(C\phi+\sum_lH^{\rm in}_l\phi)\big)_{X}$ is column $k$ of $A(X)$, and
$\big(\Pro\dg H^{\rm cross}_l\phi\big)_X$ is column $k$ of the link block of leg $l$, since every
coarse site carries the same unit vector and a crossing hop brings exactly the neighbor's
contribution. Doing this for all $2n_v$ components fills all nine blocks (\code{coarse\_blocks},
\code{hop\_split}, \code{restrict\_leg}). Several components are probed at a time, as many as a
quarter of the free device memory allows (four on $24^4$), so that $\Pro$ is read once for them.

\section{The coarse solvers (note Sec.~III.B)}
\paragraph{Quark factor.} $\Dc c=g$ by BiCGStab on its even-odd Schur complement, preconditioned by
the inverse diagonal blocks. With $\Dc=\begin{pmatrix}A_{ee}&B_{eo}\\B_{oe}&A_{oo}\end{pmatrix}$ on
the coarse lattice, exactly as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:schur},
\begin{align}
\big(A_{ee}-B_{eo}A_{oo}^{-1}B_{oe}\big)c_e&=g_e-B_{eo}A_{oo}^{-1}g_o,\\
c_o&=A_{oo}^{-1}\big(g_o-B_{oe}c_e\big).
\end{align}
\paragraph{Pauli--Villars factor.} $(\Pro\dg\Dpv\dg\Pro)w=g$. With $\Gfive\Pro=\Pro\Sigma$,
\begin{align}
\Pro\dg(\Gfive\Dpv\Gfive)\Pro&=\Pro\dg\Gfive\,\Dpv\,\Gfive\Pro\\
&=(\Gfive\Pro)\dg\,\Dpv\,(\Gfive\Pro)\\
&=\Sigma\Pro\dg\Dpv\Pro\Sigma=\Sigma\Dcpv\Sigma .
\end{align}
If $\Gfive\Dpv\Gfive=\Dpv\dg$ (true for $c=0$, off by $O(c)$ otherwise, Sec.~\ref{sec:dagger}),
the adjoint coarse factor is $\Sigma\Dcpv\Sigma$, and
\begin{align}
\Sigma\Dcpv\Sigma\,w&=g\\
\Dcpv\Sigma\,w&=\Sigma g\qquad(\Sigma^2=1)\\
\mathcal H_c\,w&=\Sigma g,\qquad \mathcal H_c\equiv\Dcpv\Sigma ,
\end{align}
$\mathcal H_c$ Hermitian (indefinite) for $c=0$, solved by MINRES with the absolute value of the
diagonal blocks as a positive preconditioner. Both factors stop at $|r|^2/|b|^2<10^{-2}$.


\paragraph{Why $\mathcal H_c$ is Hermitian when $\Gfive\Dpv\Gfive=\Dpv\dg$.} Then
$(\Dcpv)\dg=\Sigma\Dcpv\Sigma$, and with $\Sigma\dg=\Sigma$, $\Sigma^2=1$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal H_c\dg=(\Dcpv\Sigma)\dg=\Sigma(\Dcpv)\dg=\Sigma\Sigma\Dcpv\Sigma=\Dcpv\Sigma=\mathcal H_c .
\end{equation}
For $c\neq0$ this holds only approximately (the non-Hermitian part is a few percent, note
Sec.~V.C.3), and MINRES is used anyway.

\paragraph{The even-odd form of $\mathcal H_c$.} $\Sigma$ is site-local, so with the coarse blocks of
$\Dcpv$ the blocks of $\mathcal H_c=\Dcpv\Sigma$ are $A_{ee}\Sigma_e$, $B_{eo}\Sigma_o$,
$B_{oe}\Sigma_e$, $A_{oo}\Sigma_o$. Its Schur complement:
\begin{align}
A_{ee}\Sigma_e-B_{eo}\Sigma_o(A_{oo}\Sigma_o)^{-1}B_{oe}\Sigma_e
&=A_{ee}\Sigma_e-B_{eo}\Sigma_o\Sigma_o^{-1}A_{oo}^{-1}B_{oe}\Sigma_e\\
&=\big(A_{ee}-B_{eo}A_{oo}^{-1}B_{oe}\big)\Sigma_e=S_c\Sigma_e ,
\end{align}
the Schur complement $S_c$ of $\Dcpv$ times $\Sigma_e$. So MINRES runs on the even coarse sites
with $u_e=\Sigma w_e$, and the odd sites follow as for the quark factor.

\paragraph{The preconditioner of MINRES.} MINRES needs a Hermitian positive preconditioner. On
each coarse site take the Hermitian part of the diagonal block,
$\tfrac12(\mathcal H_{xx}+\mathcal H_{xx}\dg)=V\Lambda V\dg$, and use
\begin{equation}
|\mathcal H_{xx}|^{-1}=V|\Lambda|^{-1}V\dg,\qquad
z\dg|\mathcal H_{xx}|^{-1}z=\sum_k\frac{|(V\dg z)_k|^2}{|\lambda_k|}>0 ,
\end{equation}
Hermitian and positive however indefinite $\mathcal H_{xx}$ is.

\paragraph{Levels below.} At small masses the coarse solves themselves become expensive (on
$24^4$ at $m=0.001$ about 2000 applications of $\Dc$ per correction). The coarse sites are then
grouped into super-blocks (2, then 3), local vectors are formed from $\Pro\dg s_j$, $\Pro\dg\hat
t_j$, and a V-cycle preconditions the coarse Krylov solve: with $Q_\ell$ the prolongation into
level $\ell-1$ and $B_\ell$ its diagonal blocks,
\begin{equation}
z=Q_{\ell+1}M_{\ell+1}Q_{\ell+1}\dg r,\qquad M_\ell r=z+B_\ell^{-1}\big(r-\mathcal D_\ell z\big),
\end{equation}
the last level inverted densely. This brings the coarse cost down to about 50 applications per
correction.

\section{Setup (note Sec.~III.C)}
The setup produces the vectors $y_j$, then $\{s_j,\hat t_j\}$, $\Pro$, the coarse operators and the
levels. It always uses the right form.

\subsection{Chebyshev-filtered subspace iteration}
The $y_j$ should approximate the low right singular vectors of $\AR$, i.e.\ the low eigenvectors of
$H_n=\AR\dg\AR$. With $\ell(x)=\big(2x-(\lambda_{\rm hi}+\lambda_{\rm lo})\big)/(\lambda_{\rm
hi}-\lambda_{\rm lo})$, the polynomial $T_d(\ell(x))$ is bounded by $1$ on
$[\lambda_{\rm lo},\lambda_{\rm hi}]$ and grows fast below $\lambda_{\rm lo}$. One round on a block
$X$ of $m$ vectors:
\begin{align}
X&\leftarrow T_d\big(\ell(H_n)\big)X &&\text{(three-term recurrence, $d$ applications of $H_n$)}\\
X&\leftarrow\text{orthonormalize }X\\
G&=X\dg H_nX=V\Theta V\dg,\quad X\leftarrow XV &&\text{(Rayleigh--Ritz)}\\
\lambda_{\rm lo}&\leftarrow\theta_{\max} .
\end{align}

\paragraph{How much the filter amplifies, and the degree.} For $|t|\ge1$,
$|T_d(t)|=\cosh\!\big(d\operatorname{arccosh}|t|\big)$. A mode with $x$ just below $\lambda_{\rm lo}$
has $\ell(x)=-(1+\delta)$ with small $\delta>0$, and
\begin{align}
\operatorname{arccosh}(1+\delta)&=\ln\!\Big(1+\delta+\sqrt{2\delta+\delta^2}\Big)\approx\sqrt{2\delta},\\
|T_d(\ell(x))|&\approx\cosh\!\big(d\sqrt{2\delta}\big).
\end{align}
The lowest modes have $x\approx0$, where
\begin{equation}
\ell(0)=-\frac{\lambda_{\rm hi}+\lambda_{\rm lo}}{\lambda_{\rm hi}-\lambda_{\rm lo}}
\approx-\Big(1+2\frac{\lambda_{\rm lo}}{\lambda_{\rm hi}}\Big),\qquad
\delta\approx2\frac{\lambda_{\rm lo}}{\lambda_{\rm hi}},\qquad
|T_d(\ell(0))|\approx\cosh\!\Big(2d\sqrt{\lambda_{\rm lo}/\lambda_{\rm hi}}\Big),
\end{equation}
while every mode inside $[\lambda_{\rm lo},\lambda_{\rm hi}]$ stays below $1$. With the code's
degree $d=1.5\sqrt{\lambda_{\rm hi}/\lambda_{\rm lo}}$ the lowest modes gain a factor
$\cosh3\approx10$ per round over the damped ones: the degree is chosen for a fixed amplification
per round.

In the code the degree is $d=1.5\sqrt{\lambda_{\rm hi}/\lambda_{\rm lo}}$, between 40 and 1000; the first round
uses $\lambda_{\rm lo}=\lambda_{\rm hi}/1000$. \emph{Observation} ($24^4$, $m=0.001$, 20 vectors): after
the first round (degree 48) the largest Ritz value is still $0.34\,\lambda_{\rm hi}$, so the
second round has degree 40 and damps only the top two thirds of the spectrum. The filter is weak;
the bootstrap does most of the work.

\subsection{Bootstrap}
Each round builds $\Pro$ and the cycle $\mathcal M$ from the current $y$, replaces every $y_j$ by four
steps of FGMRES on $\AR x=y_j$ from $x=0$ preconditioned by $\mathcal M$, and orthonormalizes $y$
through its Gram matrix. To see why this works, write $y_j$ in eigenvectors of the preconditioned operator. The
cycle already handles the components inside its coarse space, and FGMRES with it removes them
first; what remains, and is therefore amplified relative to the rest, is what the coarse space
misses. In HMC the levels below are used from the second round on (in the first, on the filtered
vectors, the coarse solves were as fast without them).

\subsection{Cost and memory}
On $24^4$ ($L_s=8$, 20 filtered vectors, $m=0.001$; the $24^3\times48$ runs of the note use
16, $n=32$): the filter about 35\,s and the bootstrap
about 77\,s, the setup in all about 113\,s. A bootstrap round makes its correction ($\Pro$ 1.8\,s,
the two coarse operators about 4.4\,s each, the inverse blocks 0.8\,s, the top of the smoother
1.3\,s; the levels 7\,s more in the second round) and then improves the 20 vectors (about 20\,s in
the first round, 26\,s in the second). $\Pro$ ($40$ columns, half precision, whole lattice) is the largest
object, about 4.7\,GB.

\section{The Chebyshev smoother and the cycle (note Sec.~III.D)}
One cycle $y=\mathcal Mb$ is
\begin{align}
y&\leftarrow p(\tilde A)\,b &&\text{(pre-smoothing)}\\
r&\leftarrow b-\tilde Ay\\
y&\leftarrow y+e(r) &&\text{(coarse correction)}\\
r&\leftarrow b-\tilde Ay\\
y&\leftarrow y+p(\tilde A)\,r &&\text{(post-smoothing)}.
\end{align}


\paragraph{Cost of one cycle.} Count the applications of $\tilde A$: the pre-smoothing ($\nu$
Chebyshev steps from $x=0$) costs $\nu-1$, the residual after it $1$, the residual after the
correction $1$, the post-smoothing $\nu-1$:
\begin{equation}
(\nu-1)+1+1+(\nu-1)=2\nu ,
\end{equation}
plus the two coarse solves. One application of $\tilde A$ is two Schur factors, each two hops:
$\nu=8$ means 16 applications, $64$ applications of $D_{eo}$ or $D_{oe}$, per cycle. This is why one multigrid iteration costs as
much as about 24 iterations of CG on $\mathcal K\dg \mathcal K$ (two hops each with $\mathcal K$ and $\mathcal K\dg$).

\subsection{The Chebyshev smoother, derived}
The smoother is $\nu$ steps of Chebyshev iteration on $\tilde Ax=b$ from $x=0$. Choose the interval
$[\lambda_{\rm lo},\lambda_{\rm hi}]$ that it damps ($\lambda_{\rm hi}$ slightly above the spectral
radius from a power iteration, $\lambda_{\rm lo}=\lambda_{\rm hi}/\kappa_s$) and set
\begin{equation}
\theta=\tfrac12(\lambda_{\rm hi}+\lambda_{\rm lo}),\qquad
\delta=\tfrac12(\lambda_{\rm hi}-\lambda_{\rm lo}),\qquad \sigma=\theta/\delta .
\end{equation}
The residual after $k$ steps is $r_k=R_k(\tilde A)r_0$ with the scaled Chebyshev polynomial
\begin{equation}
R_k(\lambda)=\frac{T_k\big((\theta-\lambda)/\delta\big)}{T_k(\sigma)},\qquad R_k(0)=1,
\end{equation}
which is at most $1/T_k(\sigma)$ in modulus on the interval. Write $t=(\theta-\lambda)/\delta$ and
use $T_{k+1}(t)=2tT_k(t)-T_{k-1}(t)$:
\begin{align}
T_{k+1}(\sigma)R_{k+1}&=2t\,T_k(\sigma)R_k-T_{k-1}(\sigma)R_{k-1}\\
R_{k+1}&=\rho_k\big[2t\,R_k-\rho_{k-1}R_{k-1}\big],\qquad \rho_k\equiv\frac{T_k(\sigma)}{T_{k+1}(\sigma)} .
\end{align}
The same recurrence at $t=\sigma$ gives the $\rho$'s:
\begin{align}
T_{k+1}(\sigma)&=2\sigma T_k(\sigma)-T_{k-1}(\sigma)\\
\frac1{\rho_k}&=2\sigma-\rho_{k-1},\qquad \rho_0=\frac{T_0(\sigma)}{T_1(\sigma)}=\frac1\sigma .
\end{align}
Now split $2t=2\sigma-2\lambda/\delta$ and use $2\sigma=1/\rho_k+\rho_{k-1}$:
\begin{align}
R_{k+1}&=\rho_k\Big[\big(2\sigma-\tfrac{2\lambda}{\delta}\big)R_k-\rho_{k-1}R_{k-1}\Big]\\
&=\rho_k\Big[\big(\tfrac1{\rho_k}+\rho_{k-1}\big)R_k-\tfrac{2\lambda}{\delta}R_k-\rho_{k-1}R_{k-1}\Big]\\
&=R_k+\rho_k\rho_{k-1}\big(R_k-R_{k-1}\big)-\frac{2\rho_k}{\delta}\lambda R_k .
\end{align}
Apply to $r_0$, with $\lambda\to\tilde A$:
\begin{equation}
r_{k+1}-r_k=\rho_k\rho_{k-1}(r_k-r_{k-1})-\frac{2\rho_k}{\delta}\tilde Ar_k .
\end{equation}
Since $r=b-\tilde Ax$, a step $x_{k+1}=x_k+d_k$ changes the residual by $-\tilde Ad_k$. Matching
the two sides,
\begin{equation}
d_k=\rho_k\rho_{k-1}\,d_{k-1}+\frac{2\rho_k}{\delta}\,r_k .
\end{equation}
The first step: $R_1=T_1(t)/T_1(\sigma)=t/\sigma=1-\lambda/\theta$, so $x_1=b/\theta$ and
$d_0=r_0/\theta$. These are the updates of the code (\code{BlasChebyshevStep}): $\nu$ steps cost
$\nu-1$ applications of $\tilde A$, and the polynomial is fixed, so the smoother is a linear map.
The runs use $\nu=8$, $\kappa_s=10$ with stout links ($M_5=1$) and $\nu=12$, $\kappa_s=30$ without
($M_5=1.8$).

\section{The outer solver: flexible GCR (note Sec.~III.E)}
The outer iteration solves $\tilde Ay=\rho$ ($\tilde A=\AR$ or $\AL$) with a right preconditioner
$\mathcal M$, the multigrid cycle. Because $\mathcal M$ contains Krylov solves stopped by a
residual test, it changes from one application to the next, so the method must be flexible: it
keeps the preconditioned directions $z_i$ themselves.

One step of GCR($k$), from the residual $r_i=\rho-\tilde Ay_i$:
\begin{align}
z_i&=\mathcal M r_i &&\text{(one cycle)}\\
q_i&=\tilde Az_i &&\text{(one application of }\tilde A)\\
q_i&\leftarrow q_i-\sum_{j}\frac{(q_j,q_i)}{(q_j,q_j)}q_j,\quad
z_i\leftarrow z_i-\sum_{j}\frac{(q_j,q_i)}{(q_j,q_j)}z_j &&\text{(the last $k$ kept pairs)}\\
\alpha_i&=\frac{(q_i,r_i)}{(q_i,q_i)}\\
y_{i+1}&=y_i+\alpha_iz_i,\qquad r_{i+1}=r_i-\alpha_iq_i .
\end{align}
The coefficient $\alpha_i$ minimizes $\|r_i-\alpha q_i\|$, and the orthogonalization keeps
$\tilde Az_i=q_i$ true after the update, so $r_{i+1}=\rho-\tilde Ay_{i+1}$ exactly. With $k=2$
(GCR(2)) the code keeps $k+1=3$ pairs $(z,q)$ and the residual, seven fields on the even sites, at
the price of no optimality over the whole Krylov space.

\section{Solving with $D\dg$ (note Sec.~IV)}
\label{sec:dagger}
HMC needs $(D\dg D)^{-1}$, so it solves with $D\dg$ as often as with $D$. A second setup for $D\dg$
would double the cost. The tool that avoids it is an exact relation between $D$ and $D\dg$.

The $\Gfive$-transformation and the exact relation it rests on were derived in Sec.~\ref{sec:g5}.

\subsection{$\Gfive D^{-1}\Gfive$ as a preconditioner of $D\dg$ (note Sec.~IV.A)}
\label{sec:c4}
Multiply $D\dg$ from the right by $\Gfive D^{-1}\Gfive$ and use the exact relation of Sec.~\ref{sec:g5} in the form
$D\dg=\Gfive D_-^{-1}D\,\Gfive D_-\dg$, together with $\Gfive D_-\dg\Gfive=D_-$:
\begin{align}
D\dg\,\Gfive D^{-1}\Gfive&=\Gfive D_-^{-1}D\,\Gfive D_-\dg\,\Gfive\,D^{-1}\Gfive\\
&=\Gfive D_-^{-1}D\,D_-\,D^{-1}\,\Gfive\\
&=\Gfive D_-^{-1}\big(D_-D+[D,D_-]\big)D^{-1}\Gfive\\
&=\Gfive\big(1+D_-^{-1}[D,D_-]D^{-1}\big)\Gfive .
\end{align}
The commutator, with $D=D_+-D_-Y$ and $[D_+,D_-]=0$:
\begin{align}
[D,D_-]&=[D_+,D_-]-[D_-Y,D_-]\\
&=-\big(D_-YD_--D_-D_-Y\big)=-D_-[Y,D_-]\\
&=-D_-[Y,cW-1]=-c\,D_-[Y,W] .
\end{align}
The factor $D_-$ cancels against $D_-^{-1}$:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;D\dg\,\Gfive D^{-1}\Gfive=\Gfive\,(1-N)\,\Gfive,\qquad N=c\,[Y,W]\,D^{-1}\;}
\end{equation}
$[Y,W]=-\frac12[Y,K]$ is bounded (one hop in four dimensions, one in the fifth), so $N$ is large
only on the low modes of $D$. There the commutator flips chirality and moves the mode to the other
wall, out of the low-mode span, so $N^2$ is small and $(1-N)^{-1}\approx1+N$: GCR needs only a few
more steps than for the identity. On $16^4$ it needs 2--3 iterations more than the solve with $D$; on $12^4$ in setup~S, GCR(2) stalls on the residual right-hand side of HMC (note, Sec.~V.B.2).

\subsection{On the even sites, step by step (note Secs.~IV.B--C)}
The solves of HMC are on the even sites. Since $\Gfive$ acts within a site it commutes with $\pi$
and $\iota$, and with Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sinv}
\begin{equation}
S^{-\dagger}=\pi D^{-\dagger}\iota\approx\pi\Gfive D^{-1}\Gfive\iota=\Gfive\,\pi D^{-1}\iota\,\Gfive
=\Gfive S^{-1}\Gfive .
\end{equation}
The solve of $D\dg w=r$:
\begin{align}
r'_e&=r_e-D_{oe}\dg(\Co\dg)^{-1}r_o &&\text{so that }S\dg w_e=r'_e,\\
w_e&=(\Ce\dg)^{-1}z &&\Longrightarrow\ S\dg(\Ce\dg)^{-1}z=r'_e\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \mathcal K\dg z=r'_e,\\
z&=\text{GCR on }\mathcal K\dg\text{ with preconditioner }\Ce\dg\,\Gfive S^{-1}\Gfive,\\
S^{-1}t&\approx\Ce^{-1}\,\mathcal M\,(\Spv\Cpve^{-1})\dg t &&\text{(one cycle of }\AR),\\
w_e&=(\Ce\dg)^{-1}z,\qquad w_o=(\Co\dg)^{-1}\big(r_o-D_{eo}\dg w_e\big).
\end{align}
The line $\mathcal K\dg z=r'_e$ uses $\mathcal K=\Ce^{-1}S$: $\mathcal K\dg=S\dg(\Ce\dg)^{-1}$. The approximation for $S^{-1}$
comes from Eq.~\eqref{eq:ARinv}: $S^{-1}=\Ce^{-1}\AR^{-1}(\Spv\Cpve^{-1})\dg$. In the
preconditioner, $(\Spv\Cpve^{-1})\dg t$ is the right-hand side of $\AR$ for the source $(t,0)$ (no
odd part), so it is applied directly as one adjoint Schur factor of $\Dpv$. Each GCR step costs
one cycle, as for $D$.

\subsection{Why $D_-$ cannot be inverted (note Sec.~IV.D)}
\label{sec:dminus}
The exact relation of Sec.~\ref{sec:g5} contains $D_-$, and one could try to solve with $D\dg$
through $D$ exactly by inverting $D_-$. Rewrite it:
\begin{equation}
D_-=cW-1=c\Big(W-\frac1c\Big),
\end{equation}
$c$ times a Wilson operator of mass $-M_5-1/c$. By Eq.~\eqref{eq:wfree} with $M_5\to M_5+1/c$, its
free eigenvalues are
\begin{equation}
c\Big[-M_5-\frac1c+\sum_\mu(1-\cos p_\mu)\pm i\Big(\sum_\mu\sin^2p_\mu\Big)^{1/2}\Big].
\end{equation}
For $M_5=1$, $c=0.5$ the real part of the bracket runs from $-3$ to $5$; near real part $0$ the
imaginary parts have both signs. The spectrum surrounds the origin. A Krylov method needs a
polynomial $p$ with $p(0)=1$ that is small on the spectrum, and on a set around the origin no
low-degree polynomial does that (by the maximum principle $|p|$ on a curve around $0$ is at least
$|p(0)|=1$). GCR(8) on $D_-$ indeed stops at $|r|^2/|b|^2\approx0.74$ after 20000 iterations.
The preconditioner of Sec.~\ref{sec:c4} never inverts $D_-$.

\section{HMC}
The note does not derive HMC and leaves its measurements to future work. This section goes
through HMC from the path integral to the solves of the force, and then shows where the multigrid
enters.

\subsection{What we want to compute}
Lattice QCD computes expectation values in the path integral,
\begin{equation}
\langle\mathcal O\rangle=\frac1Z\int\mathcal DU\,\mathcal D\bar\psi\,\mathcal D\psi\;
\mathcal O\,e^{-S_g[U]-\bar\psi D[U]\psi},\qquad
Z=\int\mathcal DU\,\mathcal D\bar\psi\,\mathcal D\psi\;e^{-S_g[U]-\bar\psi D[U]\psi},
\end{equation}
with $U$ the gauge links, $S_g$ the gauge action (here the plaquette action at $\beta=6.0$) and
$\psi$ the quark field. The quark fields are Grassmann numbers, and their integral can be done
exactly:
\begin{equation}
\int\mathcal D\bar\psi\,\mathcal D\psi\;e^{-\bar\psi D\psi}=\det D .
\end{equation}
For two degenerate flavors (up and down) each flavor gives one $\det D$:
\begin{align}
Z&=\int\mathcal DU\;e^{-S_g[U]}\,\det D[U]\,\det D[U]\\
&=\int\mathcal DU\;e^{-S_g[U]}\,\det\!\big(D\dg D\big)[U],
\end{align}
where the second line uses that $\det D$ is real, so $(\det D)^2=\det D^*\det D=\det(D\dg D)$.
That $\det D$ is real follows from the exact relation $D_-\Gfive D\dg=D\Gfive D_-\dg$ of
Sec.~\ref{sec:dagger}, taking determinants ($\det\Gfive=\pm1$ cancels on both sides):
\begin{align}
\det D_-\,\det D\dg&=\det D\,\det D_-\dg\\
\det D_-\,(\det D)^*&=\det D\,(\det D_-)^*\\
(\det D)^*&=\det D\qquad\big(\det D_-\ \text{is real: }D_-=cW-1\ \text{and}\ \gamma_5W\gamma_5=W\dg\big).
\end{align}
So the problem is to generate gauge fields $U$ with the probability
\begin{equation}
P[U]\;\propto\;e^{-S_g[U]}\det\!\big(D\dg D\big)[U] .
\end{equation}
$\det(D\dg D)$ is the hard part: it couples all sites at once, and computing it directly costs
$O(V^3)$.

\paragraph{The Pauli--Villars ratio.} The domain-wall operator has $L_s$ slices. Only the modes bound
to the walls are the physical quark; the modes in the bulk of the fifth dimension are heavy,
unphysical, and their determinant diverges as $L_s\to\infty$. They are removed by dividing by the
determinant of the same operator at unit mass, the Pauli--Villars operator, whose wall modes are
heavy too:
\begin{equation}
\det\!\big(D\dg D\big)\;\longrightarrow\;\frac{\det\!\big(D\dg D\big)}{\det\!\big(\Dpv\dg\Dpv\big)} .
\end{equation}
This is the ``Ratio'' action.

\subsection{Pseudofermions: a determinant as a Gaussian integral}
\paragraph{One complex variable.} For a number $a>0$ and a complex variable $\phi=x+iy$,
\begin{align}
\int\frac{dx\,dy}{\pi}\;e^{-\phi^*a^{-1}\phi}
&=\int\frac{dx\,dy}{\pi}\;e^{-(x^2+y^2)/a}\\
&=\frac1\pi\Big(\int dx\,e^{-x^2/a}\Big)\Big(\int dy\,e^{-y^2/a}\Big)\\
&=\frac1\pi\,\sqrt{\pi a}\,\sqrt{\pi a}=a .
\end{align}
\paragraph{Many variables.} For a Hermitian positive matrix $A=V\Lambda V\dg$ (eigenvalues
$a_k>0$), change variables $\phi=V\chi$; the Jacobian of a unitary change is $1$:
\begin{align}
\int\mathcal D\phi\;e^{-\phi\dg A^{-1}\phi}
&=\int\mathcal D\chi\;e^{-\chi\dg V\dg V\Lambda^{-1}V\dg V\chi}\\
&=\int\mathcal D\chi\;e^{-\sum_k|\chi_k|^2/a_k}\\
&=\prod_k a_k=\det A .
\end{align}
So a determinant is a Gaussian integral over \emph{bosonic} fields $\phi$, the pseudofermions:
\begin{equation}
\det A=\int\mathcal D\phi\;e^{-\phi\dg A^{-1}\phi} .
\end{equation}
\paragraph{The ratio.} Take $A$ such that $\det A$ is our ratio. With $H=\Gfive D$ (whose
determinant has the modulus of $\det D$),
\begin{align}
A&=H_{\rm PV}\,(H\dg H)^{-1}\,H_{\rm PV}\dg,\\
\det A&=\det H_{\rm PV}\,\det(H\dg H)^{-1}\,\det H_{\rm PV}\dg
=\frac{\det(H_{\rm PV}\dg H_{\rm PV})}{\det(H\dg H)}.
\end{align}
Note this is the \emph{inverse} of the ratio; the action uses $A^{-1}$ in the exponent, so
\begin{equation}
\int\mathcal D\phi\;e^{-\phi\dg A\phi}=\det A^{-1}=\frac{\det(H\dg H)}{\det(H_{\rm PV}\dg H_{\rm PV})}
=\frac{\det(D\dg D)}{\det(\Dpv\dg\Dpv)},
\end{equation}
and the pseudofermion action is
\begin{equation}
S_F[U,\phi]=\phi\dg A\phi=\phi\dg H_{\rm PV}(H\dg H)^{-1}H_{\rm PV}\dg\phi .
\end{equation}
The joint distribution of $(U,\phi)$ is $e^{-S_g-S_F}$; integrating out $\phi$ gives back $P[U]$.

\subsection{Even-odd: only $\mathcal K$ depends on the links}
Factor $D$ into block triangular matrices (LDU):
\begin{equation}
\begin{pmatrix}\Ce&D_{eo}\\D_{oe}&\Co\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}1&D_{eo}\Co^{-1}\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}S&0\\0&\Co\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\\Co^{-1}D_{oe}&1\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Check by multiplying out the last two factors and then the first:
\begin{align}
\begin{pmatrix}S&0\\0&\Co\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\\Co^{-1}D_{oe}&1\end{pmatrix}
&=\begin{pmatrix}S&0\\D_{oe}&\Co\end{pmatrix},\\
\begin{pmatrix}1&D_{eo}\Co^{-1}\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}S&0\\D_{oe}&\Co\end{pmatrix}
&=\begin{pmatrix}S+D_{eo}\Co^{-1}D_{oe}&D_{eo}\\D_{oe}&\Co\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}\Ce&D_{eo}\\D_{oe}&\Co\end{pmatrix}.
\end{align}
The triangular factors have determinant $1$, so
\begin{align}
\det D&=\det S\,\det\Co\\
&=\det\big(\Ce \mathcal K\big)\det\Co\qquad(\mathcal K=\Ce^{-1}S)\\
&=\det\Ce\,\det\Co\,\det \mathcal K .
\end{align}
$\Ce$ and $\Co$ do not depend on the links (Sec.~\ref{sec:Cinv}), so they are constants and drop out of
$P[U]$. Only $\det \mathcal K$ is needed, on the even sites alone, half the lattice. The action becomes
\begin{equation}
S_F=\phi_e\dg H_{\rm PV}(H\dg H)^{-1}H_{\rm PV}\dg\phi_e,\qquad H=\Gfive \mathcal K,\ \ H_{\rm PV}=\Gfive \mathcal K_{\rm PV},
\end{equation}
and since $\Gfive$ is Hermitian and unitary, $H\dg H=\mathcal K\dg\Gfive\Gfive \mathcal K=\mathcal K\dg \mathcal K$, so with
$v_1=H_{\rm PV}\dg\phi_e=\mathcal K_{\rm PV}\dg\Gfive\phi_e$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;S_F=v_1\dg\,(\mathcal K\dg \mathcal K)^{-1}\,v_1\;}
\end{equation}
This is the action of the code (Bridge++'s \code{Action\_F\_alt\_Ratio\_eo}).

\subsection{The idea of HMC}
Sampling $U$ with $P[U]\propto e^{-S[U]}$, $S=S_g+S_F$, by small random changes is hopeless: each
accepted change moves one link a little. HMC moves all links at once along a long path, and still
samples $P[U]$ exactly.

\paragraph{Fictitious momenta.} For every link introduce a momentum $\pi_\mu(x)$, a traceless
Hermitian $3\times3$ matrix, and the Hamiltonian
\begin{equation}
\mathcal H[U,\pi]=\tfrac12\sum_{x,\mu}\operatorname{tr}\pi_\mu(x)^2+S[U] .
\end{equation}
The joint distribution $e^{-\mathcal H}=e^{-\frac12\sum\operatorname{tr}\pi^2}\,e^{-S[U]}$ is a
product: the momenta are Gaussian and independent of $U$. Sampling $(U,\pi)$ from $e^{-\mathcal
H}$ and forgetting $\pi$ samples $U$ from $e^{-S}$.

\paragraph{One trajectory.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Draw $\pi$ from its Gaussian and $\phi_e$ by the heat bath (below).
\item Evolve $(U,\pi)$ for a fictitious time $\tau$ (here $\tau=1$) by Hamilton's equations,
\begin{equation}
\dot U_\mu(x)=i\,\pi_\mu(x)\,U_\mu(x),\qquad \dot\pi_\mu(x)=-F_\mu(x),
\end{equation}
with the force $F$ the derivative of $S$ with respect to $U$ (below). Exactly solved, these
conserve $\mathcal H$.
\item The numerical integration does not conserve $\mathcal H$ exactly. Accept the new $U$ with
probability $\min(1,e^{-\Delta\mathcal H})$, $\Delta\mathcal H=\mathcal H_{\rm end}-\mathcal
H_{\rm start}$; otherwise keep the old $U$ (the Metropolis test).
\end{enumerate}
\paragraph{Why the result is exact.} The test makes the method exact for any step size, provided
the integrator has two properties: it is \emph{reversible} (running it with the momenta reversed
brings $(U,-\pi)$ back) and it \emph{preserves volume} in $(U,\pi)$ space. Then the proposal
$(U,\pi)\to(U',\pi')$ is as likely as its reverse, and the acceptance $\min(1,e^{-\Delta\mathcal H})$
gives detailed balance with $e^{-\mathcal H}$:
\begin{align}
e^{-\mathcal H(U,\pi)}\min\big(1,e^{-[\mathcal H(U',\pi')-\mathcal H(U,\pi)]}\big)
&=\min\big(e^{-\mathcal H(U,\pi)},e^{-\mathcal H(U',\pi')}\big)\\
&=e^{-\mathcal H(U',\pi')}\min\big(1,e^{-[\mathcal H(U,\pi)-\mathcal H(U',\pi')]}\big),
\end{align}
which is symmetric in the two states. A small step size only raises the acceptance.

\subsection{The integrator}
\paragraph{Leapfrog.} Split a step of size $\epsilon$ into half a momentum update, a full link
update, and half a momentum update:
\begin{align}
\pi&\leftarrow\pi-\tfrac\epsilon2F(U),\\
U&\leftarrow e^{i\epsilon\pi}U,\\
\pi&\leftarrow\pi-\tfrac\epsilon2F(U).
\end{align}
Each of the three updates changes only $U$ or only $\pi$ by an amount that depends on the other
one, so each is volume preserving (a shear), and the product of shears is too. Reversing $\pi$
and running the step again undoes it, update by update. The energy error after a trajectory is
$O(\epsilon^2)$.

\paragraph{Omelyan.} With a parameter $\lambda\approx0.193$,
\begin{equation}
\pi\leftarrow\pi-\lambda\epsilon F,\quad U\leftarrow e^{i\frac\epsilon2\pi}U,\quad
\pi\leftarrow\pi-(1-2\lambda)\epsilon F,\quad U\leftarrow e^{i\frac\epsilon2\pi}U,\quad
\pi\leftarrow\pi-\lambda\epsilon F .
\end{equation}
It is still reversible and volume preserving, and $\lambda$ is chosen to make the leading error
term about ten times smaller than that of leapfrog, at two force evaluations per step instead of
one. $e^{i\epsilon\pi}$ is evaluated by its Taylor series (order 8 in the runs).

\paragraph{Nested steps.} The gauge force is cheap and the fermion force expensive. The runs use
two levels: the fermion force with $n$ Omelyan steps, and within each of its link updates the gauge
force with 2 steps of its own (``\code{number\_of\_steps: [n, 2]}'').

\paragraph{How many solves.} At the fermion level, consecutive momentum updates of neighboring
steps merge into one ($\lambda\epsilon+\lambda\epsilon=2\lambda\epsilon$). Count the force
evaluations of $n$ steps:
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{1}_{\lambda\epsilon\text{ at the start}}+\underbrace{n}_{(1-2\lambda)\epsilon}
+\underbrace{n-1}_{2\lambda\epsilon\text{ between steps}}+\underbrace{1}_{\lambda\epsilon\text{ at the end}}
=2n+1 .
\end{equation}
Each needs one solve with $\mathcal K\dg \mathcal K$, and the energy at the start and the end one more each:
\begin{equation}
\text{solves per trajectory}=2n+3 .
\end{equation}
For $n=20$ this is 43 and for $n=40$ it is 83, the numbers the runs report.

\paragraph{Step size and stability.} The force grows as the quark mass falls (the low modes of $\mathcal K$
come closer to zero). If $\epsilon$ times the largest force frequency exceeds a threshold, the
integrator becomes unstable and $\Delta\mathcal H$ explodes rather than growing smoothly. On the
dynamical $16^4$ configuration at $m=0.01$, $n=10$ gave $\Delta\mathcal H\approx10^3$ (every
trajectory rejected), $n=20$ gave $|\Delta\mathcal H|\approx0.05$ (acceptance $0.98$), and
$n=40$ gave $0.005$.

\subsection{The heat bath for $\phi_e$}
$\phi_e$ must be drawn from $e^{-S_F}$ at fixed $U$. Draw $\xi_e$ Gaussian, $P(\xi_e)\propto
e^{-|\xi_e|^2}$, and set
\begin{equation}
\phi_e=H_{\rm PV}\,(H_{\rm PV}\dg H_{\rm PV})^{-1}H\dg\,\xi_e .
\end{equation}
Then $S_F=|\xi_e|^2$, so $\phi_e$ has the right distribution:
\begin{align}
H_{\rm PV}\dg\phi_e&=H_{\rm PV}\dg H_{\rm PV}(H_{\rm PV}\dg H_{\rm PV})^{-1}H\dg\xi_e=H\dg\xi_e,\\
S_F&=(H_{\rm PV}\dg\phi_e)\dg(H\dg H)^{-1}(H_{\rm PV}\dg\phi_e)\\
&=(H\dg\xi_e)\dg(H\dg H)^{-1}(H\dg\xi_e)\\
&=\xi_e\dg H\,H^{-1}H^{-\dagger}\,H\dg\xi_e\\
&=\xi_e\dg\xi_e .
\end{align}
This costs one solve with the Pauli--Villars operator ($H_{\rm PV}\dg H_{\rm PV}$, well conditioned:
CG converges fast).

\subsection{The force}
The force is the change of $S_F$ when the links change. Vary $U$; $\phi_e$ is fixed. With
$\chi=\Gfive\phi_e$, $v_1=\mathcal K_{\rm PV}\dg\chi$ and $v_2=(\mathcal K\dg \mathcal K)^{-1}v_1$, and using
$\delta(M^{-1})=-M^{-1}(\delta M)M^{-1}$:
\begin{align}
\delta S_F&=\delta\big[v_1\dg(\mathcal K\dg \mathcal K)^{-1}v_1\big]\\
&=(\delta v_1)\dg(\mathcal K\dg \mathcal K)^{-1}v_1+v_1\dg(\mathcal K\dg \mathcal K)^{-1}\delta v_1+v_1\dg\,\delta\big[(\mathcal K\dg \mathcal K)^{-1}\big]\,v_1\\
&=(\delta v_1)\dg v_2+v_2\dg\,\delta v_1-v_1\dg(\mathcal K\dg \mathcal K)^{-1}\,\delta(\mathcal K\dg \mathcal K)\,(\mathcal K\dg \mathcal K)^{-1}v_1\\
&=2\,\Re\,(\delta v_1)\dg v_2-v_2\dg\,\delta(\mathcal K\dg \mathcal K)\,v_2\\
&=2\,\Re\,(\delta \mathcal K_{\rm PV}\dg\chi)\dg v_2-v_2\dg\big(\delta \mathcal K\dg\,\mathcal K+\mathcal K\dg\,\delta \mathcal K\big)v_2\\
&=2\,\Re\,\chi\dg\,\delta \mathcal K_{\rm PV}\,v_2-2\,\Re\,(\mathcal Kv_2)\dg\,\delta \mathcal K\,v_2 .
\end{align}
So one force evaluation needs exactly one solve, $v_2=(\mathcal K\dg \mathcal K)^{-1}v_1$; the rest are
multiplications. $\delta \mathcal K$ comes from the hops in $S$ alone ($\Ce$ does not depend on the links):
for a variation of one link, only the hops through that link contribute, and the two terms above
become a local outer product of $v_2$, $\mathcal Kv_2$ and $\chi$ at the two ends of the link. With stout
smearing the operator sees the smeared links; the force is taken with respect to them and carried
back to the thin links by the chain rule through the smearing steps.

\subsection{How precisely to solve}
\paragraph{Energy.} $\mathcal H$ at the start and the end enters $\Delta\mathcal H$ directly, and an
error there biases the Metropolis test. These solves are done to $|r|^2/|b|^2<10^{-24}$.
\paragraph{Force.} An error in the force only changes the trajectory, which the test corrects,
\emph{as long as the integrator stays reversible}. A solve from a zero initial guess is a fixed
function of the links, so reversibility holds to its tolerance; $10^{-16}$ is ample. (Starting the
force solves from the previous solutions, \code{number\_of\_initial\_guess}, saves iterations but
breaks reversibility at the level of the tolerance; it is off by default.)

\subsection{Solving $(\mathcal K\dg \mathcal K)^{-1}$}
\paragraph{With CG.} $\mathcal K\dg \mathcal K$ is Hermitian positive, so CG solves it directly; each iteration costs
two applications of $\mathcal K$. Mixed precision runs CG in single precision with corrections in double.
With Neff's $\alpha$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Ksim} gives $\mathcal K=Q_e\mathcal K^{(\alpha)}Q_e^{-1}$ and
\begin{align}
\mathcal K\dg \mathcal K&=Q_e^{-1}\mathcal K^{(\alpha)\dagger}Q_e\,Q_e\mathcal K^{(\alpha)}Q_e^{-1}\\
&=Q_e^{-1}\,(Q_e\mathcal K^{(\alpha)})\dg(Q_e\mathcal K^{(\alpha)})\,Q_e^{-1}\\
&=Q_e^{-1}\,(\mathcal KQ_e)\dg(\mathcal KQ_e)\,Q_e^{-1}\qquad(Q_e\mathcal K^{(\alpha)}=\mathcal KQ_e),\\
(\mathcal K\dg \mathcal K)^{-1}&=Q_e\big[(\mathcal KQ_e)\dg(\mathcal KQ_e)\big]^{-1}Q_e ,
\end{align}
so CG runs on the normal equation of $\mathcal KQ_e$, better conditioned, while the action and the force stay
those of $\mathcal K$ (on $24^4$ at $m=0.001$: 22\% fewer iterations).

\paragraph{With the multigrid: two solves.} The multigrid solves with $D$ and $D\dg$, not with
$\mathcal K\dg \mathcal K$. Invert the product, with $\mathcal K=\Ce^{-1}S$:
\begin{align}
(\mathcal K\dg \mathcal K)^{-1}&=\mathcal K^{-1}\mathcal K^{-\dagger}\\
&=(\Ce^{-1}S)^{-1}\big[(\Ce^{-1}S)\dg\big]^{-1}\\
&=S^{-1}\Ce\;\Ce\dg S^{-\dagger} .
\end{align}
So $z=(\mathcal K\dg \mathcal K)^{-1}r$ is two solves:
\begin{align}
w&=\Ce\dg\,S^{-\dagger}r=\Ce\dg\,\pi D^{-\dagger}\iota\,r &&\text{(with $D\dg$, Sec.~\ref{sec:dagger})},\\
z&=S^{-1}\Ce\,w=\pi D^{-1}\iota\,\Ce w &&\text{(with $D$)}.
\end{align}
Each solve stops at $10^{-10}$, while the normal equation needs $10^{-16}$ or $10^{-24}$. So the two
solves are repeated on the residual of the normal equation (iterative refinement):
\begin{align}
r^{(k)}&=b-\mathcal K\dg\mathcal Kz^{(k)},\\
z^{(k+1)}&=z^{(k)}+\mathcal K^{-1}\mathcal K^{-\dagger}r^{(k)} ,
\end{align}
each sweep gaining about ten digits, at most eleven sweeps. With $\alpha$ the solve with $D$ uses
$D^{-1}=QD^{(\alpha)-1}$ (the left form $\AL^{(\alpha)}$); the solve with $D\dg$ is unchanged, since
$D^{(\alpha)\dagger}=QD\dg$ only rescales its right-hand side.

\paragraph{When the sweeps stall.} If the sweeps stop gaining (the coarse space no longer fits the
links, as after large link changes), the solve is repeated from the current $z$ with $\Pro$ and the
coarse operators rebuilt for the current links, then also after a bootstrap; if it still stalls, CG
finishes it from the current $z$, with a warning. A trajectory is never lost to the multigrid.

\subsection{What the multigrid keeps along a trajectory}
The links change a little at every step of the integrator, and the setup is expensive, so the
multigrid is not rebuilt for every solve.
\begin{itemize}
\item The test vectors $y$ are kept from solve to solve (on the host between solves) and improved by
a bootstrap, from the old $y$, when the pseudofermions are drawn; the filter runs only for the
first trajectory.
\item $\Pro$, the coarse operators and the top of the smoother's interval are kept while the links
move and rebuilt for the current links when the GCR iterations per solve have grown by half
(\code{rebuild\_iteration\_growth}~$=1.5$) or after a bootstrap.
\item The cycle is made for each solve from these parts and dropped after it.
\end{itemize}
A kept coarse space is the coarse space of slightly different links. It is still a good
preconditioner; GCR on the operator of the current links keeps the solve exact.

\subsection{What the measurements say so far}
\begin{itemize}
\item On a thermalized dynamical $16^4$ configuration (60 trajectories at $m=0.05$, then 40 at
$m=0.01$ from the quenched one) the multigrid reproduces the trajectories of CG: the plaquette to
$10^{-11}$, $\Delta\mathcal H$ to three digits. This checks that it solves the right equation.
\item There CG needs about 300 iterations per solve at $m=0.01$ and at $m=0.001$ alike: on a lattice
this small the lowest eigenvalues are held up by the finite volume, not by the mass. The multigrid
needs about 55 iterations, but one of them (a whole cycle) costs about 24 CG iterations, so it is
3--4 times slower per trajectory. Tuning gains at most 6\%.
\item On the quenched $24^4$ configuration CG needs about 6400 iterations per solve and the
multigrid wins, but a trajectory of length 1 there blows up ($\Delta\mathcal H\approx4\times10^9$),
so it is no test of HMC. There are no data yet that show the multigrid faster in HMC; that needs a
volume where CG takes more than about 1300 iterations per solve.
\end{itemize}
