\title{A factorized Pauli--Villars multigrid method\\
for the M\"obius domain-wall operator}
\author{Wei-Lun Chen\\[0.3ex]{\small\itshape draft}}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The M\"obius domain-wall operator has a spectrum that surrounds the origin,
which defeats non-Hermitian Krylov solvers and most multigrid constructions.
Kanamori, Chen and Matsufuru showed that preconditioning with the
Pauli--Villars operator together with the site-diagonal part of the operator,
$A'=(\Dpv\Cpv^{-1})\dg DC^{-1}$, moves the spectrum into the right half-plane
and gives the smallest condition number among the operators they examined. This note
describes a two-grid method for $A'$ on the even sites. Its coarse-grid correction is
factorized into one Galerkin solve for the quark operator and one for the adjoint
Pauli--Villars operator, each with a nearest-neighbor coarse stencil, so that the
outer system can be the even-odd one while the coarse operators stay nearest-neighbor.
The prolongation is built from
approximate low modes lifted to the whole lattice, and the correction is exact on
the vectors it is made from. The smoother is a fixed Chebyshev polynomial.\ifresults{}
Why the method converges is not obvious: the field of values of the preconditioned
operator reaches far into the left half-plane, so the usual convergence bound of GCR
does not apply. Its eigenvalues nevertheless stay at $\Re\lambda\ge0.78$, and its
pseudospectra on $12^4$ and $16^4$ bound the convergence of full GMRES; the truncated
GCR used in practice converges best when the coarse solves are inexact, and with
accurate ones it can stagnate. On a quenched
$24^3\times48$ configuration the multigrid iterations grow by a factor of $2$--$4$
from $m=0.1$ to $m=0.001$, against $20$ for even-odd CGNE, and at $m=0.001$ the solve
is $7.5$--$9.0$ times faster than CGNE in double precision and $4.5$--$4.8$ times
faster than in mixed precision, the setup being paid back after $2$--$5$ right-hand
sides; a whole point-source propagator at $m=0.001$ takes half the time of the
mixed-precision CGNE, setup included. The results are from one configuration per
volume; Sec.~\ref{sec:reproduce} describes how to extend them.\else{} We state the
operator and its forms (with Neff's $\alpha$), the correction, the coarse solvers, the
setup, the smoother, the outer solver, and the questions that are open.\fi
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Lattice computations of flavor physics and of the hadronic contributions to
precision observables need a fermion discretization whose chiral symmetry is
broken only mildly at finite lattice spacing. The overlap
operator~\cite{NarayananNeuberger,Neuberger} has an exact lattice chiral
symmetry. Domain-wall fermions~\cite{Kaplan,Shamir,FurmanShamir} approximate it
at a lower cost by adding a fifth dimension of extent $L_s$, in which modes of
opposite chirality are bound to the two boundaries by a large negative Wilson
mass $-M_5$; the residual chiral symmetry breaking is suppressed as $L_s$
grows, and the effective four-dimensional operator is a rational approximation
of the overlap operator. The M\"obius generalization~\cite{BNO0,BNO1,BNO}
introduces two coefficients $(b,c)$ and improves this approximation at fixed
$L_s$; $c=0$ is Shamir's operator.

The price is paid in the linear solver. The operator is neither Hermitian nor
positive, one application costs $L_s$ four-dimensional Wilson hops, and the
standard solver is still the conjugate gradient method on the normal equation
of the even-odd preconditioned operator, whose condition number is the square
of that of the operator itself. For Wilson-type fermions this problem has been
solved by multigrid: the low modes that slow down Krylov solvers are locally
coherent~\cite{Luscher}, so a few of them, cut into blocks, span the low-mode
space, and adaptive aggregation multigrid makes the cost nearly independent of
the quark mass~\cite{Brannick,DDalphaAMG,BridgeMG}.

\paragraph{Multigrid for domain-wall fermions.}
The domain-wall operator has a spectrum that encloses the origin, because of
the large negative Wilson mass. This violates the half-plane condition of
non-Hermitian Krylov methods, whose convergence is then of the order of the
system size~\cite{NRT,BoyleMRHS}. Boyle~\cite{BoyleMRHS} lists four multigrid
approaches that have had a measure of success, and they differ in the operator
that is coarsened. Cohen \textit{et al.}~\cite{Cohen} coarsened the squared
unpreconditioned operator, with blocks covering the whole fifth dimension, and
noted that its coarse operator reaches two hops. HDCG~\cite{HDCG} accelerates
the best standard solver, CG on the normal equation of the red-black (even-odd)
operator, and coarsens that squared Schur complement; its coarse stencil is
next-to-next-to-next-to-nearest neighbor, $81$ points for blocks of at least
$4^4$. HDCR~\cite{HDCR} works instead directly on the nearest-neighbor
Hermitian indefinite operator $\gamma_5R_5D$ at $c=0$, so that the coarse
operator stays nearest neighbor, explicitly to make re-evaluation along an HMC
trajectory affordable. Brower, Clark, Howarth and Weinberg~\cite{BCHW} pointed
out that the Pauli--Villars preconditioned operator $\Dpv^{-1}D$, not $D$,
carries the physical four-dimensional degrees of freedom, solved the
system $\Dpv\dg Dx=\Dpv\dg b$ with coarse operators built from the
projected Wilson kernel, and demonstrated it in the two-dimensional Schwinger
model. Boyle and Yamaguchi~\cite{BY} compared these approaches for Shamir
fermions; HDCR could be set up and solved faster than a single red-black CG
solve on large volumes. Two of their observations bear on the present method:
among red-black solvers, CG on the Schur complement
$M_{ee}-M_{eo}M_{oo}^{-1}M_{oe}$ needed far fewer iterations than on the
left-preconditioned $1-M_{ee}^{-1}M_{eo}M_{oo}^{-1}M_{oe}$; and combining a
red-black smoother with the HDCR coarse correction was not effective, which
they attributed to the eigenvectors of the red-black operator and of
$\gamma_5R_5D$ not being aligned.

\paragraph{Recent work by Boyle and collaborators.}
The multiple right-hand-side HDCG of Ref.~\cite{BoyleMRHS} works on the
red-black Hermitian operator
$(M_{ee}-M_{eo}M_{oo}^{-1}M_{oe})\dg(M_{ee}-M_{eo}M_{oo}^{-1}M_{oe})$ with a
flexible ADEF2 two-level conjugate gradient, a coarse space spanned by blocked
low modes obtained with Chebyshev filters, and a stationary coarse solver
(deflation plus a Chebyshev polynomial). Treating several right-hand sides at
once with block CG and batched matrix products makes the coarse space
sub-dominant, and gains of up to a factor of twenty over red-black CG are
reported at physical quark mass. In a Lattice~2026 talk, Boyle and
Oare~\cite{BoyleOare} named two limitations of this approach---the cost of
forming the $81$-point coarse operator, and that a single solve on a new gauge
field, as in HMC, does not profit from many right-hand sides---and presented
a multigrid that directly coarsens the two-hop operator
$\Dpv\dg D$ with blocks that contain the whole fifth dimension, which gives a
$33$-point coarse stencil. They emphasized that $\Dpv\dg D$ is non-normal: its field of values
can extend into the left half-plane although its spectrum does not, the
bound of Eisenstat, Elman and Schultz~\cite{ESS} for GCR is then lost, and they
introduced a shift in the smoothers. The coarse solve dominated the cost; a
three-level multiple right-hand-side version performs similarly to
multiple right-hand-side HDCG, and an exact dense inverse on a further coarse
level is planned.

\paragraph{Setup cost and ensemble generation.}
The setup of a multigrid, and of deflation, is paid once per gauge field, which is
affordable for propagators but not for the solves of Hybrid Monte Carlo, where the field
changes at every step. Eick and Mawhinney~\cite{EickMawhinney} avoid the setup by
renormalization-group blocking: a fine M\"obius ensemble ($a^{-1}=2$\,GeV) is blocked to a
coarse lattice ($a^{-1}=1$\,GeV) whose native domain-wall operator approximates the low
modes of the fine one, and the coarse solve corrects a CG on the normal equations. They
find that the interpolated correction carries high modes that the fine operator amplifies
by four orders of magnitude, and that a Chebyshev low-pass filter of degree $150$--$300$ is
needed to make the correction useful; they also find the fifth-dimensional profiles of the
fine and coarse low modes to differ.

\paragraph{Site-diagonal and Pauli--Villars preconditioning.}
Kanamori, Chen and Matsufuru~\cite{KCM2024} examined the spectra of several
preconditioned M\"obius operators. Combining the Pauli--Villars factor with the
site-diagonal part $C$ of the operator, they found that
$A'=(\Dpv\Cpv^{-1})\dg DC^{-1}$ has eigenvalues with positive real part and
the smallest condition number among the candidates, while replacing $\Dpv$ by
a block-diagonal truncation brings back eigenvalues with negative real part.
Independently, Neff's modification of the domain-wall
matrix~\cite{Neff,CKMN} rescales the fifth dimension by a site-local factor
$Q$ and changes the conditioning of the even-odd system.

\paragraph{This note.}
We describe a two-grid method for $A'$. It is an adaptive aggregation-based
multigrid in the sense of Refs.~\cite{Brannick,DDalphaAMG,BridgeMG}: the coarse space
is built from computed near-null vectors, cut into aggregates, and the coarse
operators are Galerkin projections of fine operators, not a rediscretization; it
differs from these methods in that the coarse-grid correction is factorized
rather than the Galerkin correction of the operator that is solved. The outer system is the usual even-odd
reduced one, as in HDCG, but the coarse-grid correction is \emph{factorized}: the inverses of the quark
operator and of the adjoint Pauli--Villars operator are replaced by two
separate Galerkin inverses, each with a nearest-neighbor coarse stencil and an
aggregation that is compatible with the five-dimensional chirality. The
prolongation is built from approximate low modes lifted to the whole lattice in
two ways, which makes the correction exact on these vectors for the right form
of the operator. The smoother is a fixed Chebyshev polynomial and the setup
combines Chebyshev filtering with a bootstrap.\ifresults{} The note states the
method and gives first numerical results: the field of values, spectrum and
pseudospectra of the preconditioned operator, the behavior of truncated GCR, a mass
scan and point-source propagators against even-odd CGNE, and the sensitivity to the
parameters.\else{} The note
states the method; it does not assess its performance.\fi

Section~\ref{sec:overview} describes the method in words.
Section~\ref{sec:spectra} recalls the spectra on which the construction rests.
Sections~\ref{sec:operator}--\ref{sec:outer} describe the method: the operator

\paragraph{Conventions.}
Five-dimensional fields are $\psi_s(x)$, $s=0,\dots,L_s-1$; $\Pp,\Pm=\tfrac12(1\pm\gamma_5)$.
$R$ reverses the fifth dimension, $(R\psi)_s=\psi_{L_s-1-s}$, and $\Gfive=\gamma_5R$.
Subscripts $e$ and $o$ denote blocks on the even and odd four-dimensional sites;
$X_{pq}$ maps parity $q$ to parity $p$. The injection $\iota$ puts a field on the
even sites onto the whole lattice (zero on the odd sites), and $\pi=\iota\dg$
takes the even sites. The operator and its parts $C$ and $Q$ are defined in
Sec.~\ref{sec:operator}; Appendix~\ref{sec:notation} relates them to the notation of
Refs.~\cite{BNO,KCM2024} and of Bridge++~\cite{Bridge}.

\section{Overview of the method}
\label{sec:overview}

This section describes the method in words, for readers who know domain-wall fermions
but not necessarily multigrid; the sections after it give the equations.

\paragraph{Multigrid in one paragraph.} A multigrid preconditioner removes the error of
an iterate in two parts. The part made of high modes is damped by a few steps of a cheap
iteration on the fine lattice, the \emph{smoother}. The part made of low modes, the
near-zero modes that make CG slow, is removed by solving a much smaller problem on a
\emph{coarse lattice}. The coarse lattice comes from \emph{aggregation}: the fine lattice
is cut into blocks (here $4^4$ sites with the whole fifth dimension), each block becomes
one coarse site, and on each block a few approximate low modes, the \emph{test vectors},
are kept as its degrees of freedom. The \emph{prolongation} $\Pro$ maps a coarse vector to
the fine lattice by these vectors and $\Pro\dg$ maps back; the coarse operator is the
\emph{Galerkin} projection $\Pro\dg X\Pro$ of a fine operator $X$, which couples only
neighboring coarse sites when $X$ couples only neighboring fine sites. A few vectors a
block suffice because low modes are locally coherent~\cite{Luscher}. Finding the test
vectors is the \emph{setup}, paid once per gauge field and shared by all right-hand sides.

\paragraph{What is solved.} As for even-odd CGNE, $D\psi=\eta$ is reduced to the even
sites, here with the Pauli--Villars and site-diagonal preconditioning of $A'$: a system
$\tilde Ay=\rho$ in the left form $\tilde A=\AL$, which can carry Neff's $\alpha$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:eo}); $\psi$ is reconstructed from $y$ afterwards. The outer solver is
flexible GCR keeping two directions, GCR(2), with one two-grid cycle as the preconditioner
in each iteration (Sec.~\ref{sec:outer}).

\paragraph{One cycle.} A cycle is a Chebyshev polynomial in $\tilde A$ (the smoother), a
coarse correction, and the smoother again (Algorithm~\ref{alg:cycle}). The coarse
correction is the new ingredient. Coarsening $\tilde A$ itself, a four-hop operator on the
even sites, would give a coarse stencil of $81$ points; instead its two factors are
inverted separately, first the adjoint Pauli--Villars operator and then the quark
operator, each through the Galerkin operator of $\Dpv$ or $D$, whose coarse stencil has
$9$ points (Fig.~\ref{fig:correction}, Sec.~\ref{sec:correction}). Both coarse systems are
solved only roughly, to a relative residual of about $0.1$, in single precision and on the
even coarse sites (Sec.~\ref{sec:coarse}).

\paragraph{The setup.} (i) A Chebyshev filter applied to random vectors gives approximate
low modes $y$ of $\AR$, the vectors of its smallest singular values; (ii) a bootstrap
improves them: the cycle is built from the current vectors, and a few FGMRES steps
preconditioned by it, applied to each vector, enrich it in what the cycle still misses;
(iii) each $y$ is lifted to the whole lattice in two ways, $s$ and $\hat t$, one for each
coarse solve, which makes the correction exact on these vectors
(Proposition~\ref{prop:exact}); (iv) the lifts are split by their $\Gfive$-chirality and
orthonormalized on each block, which gives $\Pro$, and the coarse operators of $D$ and $\Dpv$
are built from it (Algorithm~\ref{alg:setup}, Sec.~\ref{sec:setup}). One setup serves both
forms and every $\alpha$.

\paragraph{Where to read on.} The construction is in Secs.~\ref{sec:eo} and
\ref{sec:correction}; Table~\ref{tab:approaches} compares it with other multigrid methods
for domain-wall fermions.\ifresults{} The parameters used in practice are in
Table~\ref{tab:setups}, and the comparison with CGNE in Sec.~\ref{sec:massscan}.\fi

\section{Spectra of preconditioned M\"obius operators}
\label{sec:spectra}

The location of the spectrum decides which iterative methods can work. A Krylov
residual polynomial $p$ with $p(0)=1$ that is small on the spectrum exists at
low degree only if the spectrum can be enclosed by a region, such as an
ellipse, that excludes the origin. The same holds for the Chebyshev smoother
of Sec.~\ref{sec:smoother}, which is defined by an ellipse in the right
half-plane. In short, Krylov solvers and polynomial smoothers work when the
eigenvalues lie on one side of the origin, and those of the domain-wall operator
do not; the preconditioners below move them there. The spectra of Ref.~\cite{KCM2024}, reproduced in
Figs.~\ref{fig:free}--\ref{fig:A}, show what the preconditioners do. The
lattices are given in the captions.

\begin{figure}[tbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.6\linewidth]{spectrum_fig1.png}
\caption{Spectrum of a free M\"obius domain-wall operator on $16^4\times8$ with
quark mass $0.01$, $b=1.5$, $c=0.5$ and domain-wall height $M_5=1$.
Reproduced from Ref.~\cite{KCM2024}, Fig.~1.}
\label{fig:free}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The unpreconditioned operator.}
The free spectrum (Fig.~\ref{fig:free}) is a band that reaches into
$\Re\lambda<0$. On a $2+1$-flavor configuration at the physical point
(Fig.~\ref{fig:pv}, top left) the real parts lie in $[-1.29,13.2]$. Conjugate
gradients and BiCGStab therefore do not apply to $D$ itself, and the practical
choice has been CG on a positive definite squared operator.

\paragraph{Site-diagonal preconditioning.}
With the site-diagonal part $C$, whose inverse is a constant matrix in the fifth
dimension and spin (Sec.~\ref{sec:C}), $DC^{-1}$ has real parts in
$[-0.22,2.22]$ (middle left): the extent shrinks, the sign problem remains.

\paragraph{Pauli--Villars preconditioning.}
The operator $\Dpv^{-1}D$, with $\Dpv=D(m=1)$, is a rational approximation of
the overlap operator~\cite{BCHW}. Because $\Dpv^{-1}$ is as expensive as a solve
with $D$, Ref.~\cite{BCHW} used $\Dpv\dg$ instead. $\Dpv\dg D$ has positive real
parts (top right), and its low modes lie close to the imaginary axis with a
positive shift of the order of the quark mass, as expected of an approximate
overlap operator; its high end, however, reaches about $187$, which an overlap
operator would not show. Replacing $\Dpv$ by its block-diagonal approximation
$\Bpv$, which drops the hops between $4^4$ blocks, brings back negative real
parts (middle and bottom right).

\paragraph{Both together.}
The operator $A'=(\Dpv\Cpv^{-1})\dg DC^{-1}$ has real parts in
$[2.67\times10^{-4},\,5.22]$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:A}, left); its condition number,
$1.96\times10^{4}$, is smaller than the $2.34\times10^{4}$ of $\Dpv\dg D$, and
the smallest among the operators examined in Ref.~\cite{KCM2024}. With $\Dpv$
replaced by $\Bpv$ the real parts become negative again (right). A
preconditioner that truncates the Pauli--Villars factor to blocks would
therefore inherit eigenvalues with negative real part; in the method below
the factor is kept exact on the fine lattice and is approximated only through
its Galerkin coarse operator (Sec.~\ref{sec:correction}).

\paragraph{Two caveats.}
First, the figures show the full-lattice operator $A'$; the even-odd operators
of Sec.~\ref{sec:eo} are related to it but their spectra are not part of these
figures. Second, positivity of the real parts of the eigenvalues is not
positivity of the field of values. For a non-normal operator the convergence
bound of GCR~\cite{ESS} requires the Hermitian part $(A+A\dg)/2$ to be positive
definite, which can fail while every eigenvalue lies in the right half-plane;
this is the mechanism discussed for $\Dpv\dg D$ in Ref.~\cite{BoyleOare}.
\ifresults{}It occurs for the even-odd form: for $\AR$ itself the field of values reaches just
below zero on $12^4$ ($\Re W\ge-4.6\times10^{-3}$ with stout smearing,
$-5.0\times10^{-2}$ without), and for the multigrid-preconditioned $\AR\mathcal M$ far
below (Sec.~\ref{sec:fov}). The bound of Ref.~\cite{ESS} is then lost, but the
spectrum of $\AR\mathcal M$ stays at $\Re\lambda\ge0.78$ and its pseudospectra bound
full GMRES (Secs.~\ref{sec:spectrum}, \ref{sec:pseudo}); truncated GCR is not covered
(Sec.~\ref{sec:gcrk}). For the full-lattice $A'$ it has not been measured.\else{}Whether
it occurs for $A'$ and its even-odd forms is open (Sec.~\ref{sec:open}).\fi

\section{The operator and Pauli--Villars preconditioning}
\label{sec:operator}

This section defines the M\"obius operator in the form of Brower, Neff and
Orginos~\cite{BNO} and derives from it the quantities the method is built on: the
part $C$ of the operator that acts within a four-dimensional site and its inverse, the
$\Gfive$-transformation of $D$, the Pauli--Villars preconditioned operator $A'$ of
Ref.~\cite{KCM2024}, and Neff's $\alpha$. Every step is written out. The notation
follows Ref.~\cite{BNO} except where stated; Appendix~\ref{sec:notation} relates it to
Ref.~\cite{KCM2024} and to Bridge++ symbol by symbol.

\subsection{The M\"obius operator}
\paragraph{Ingredients.} $\psi$ is a field on the four-dimensional lattice with an extra
index $s=0,\dots,L_s-1$, the fifth dimension; $\psi_s$ is an ordinary Dirac field for each
$s$. $P_\pm=\tfrac12(1\pm\gamma_5)$ are the chiral projectors. The Wilson hopping term is
\begin{equation}
(K\psi)(x)=\sum_{\mu=1}^{4}\Big[(1-\gamma_\mu)U_\mu(x)\psi(x+\hat\mu)
+(1+\gamma_\mu)U_\mu\dg(x-\hat\mu)\psi(x-\hat\mu)\Big],
\end{equation}
and the Wilson operator with mass $-M_5$ (the domain-wall height $M_5>0$) is
\begin{equation}
W=(4-M_5)-\tfrac12K .
\label{eq:W}
\end{equation}
This is the usual $D_W(-M_5)$ with hopping coefficient $\tfrac12$; in the $\kappa$
normalization $W=(4-M_5)(1-\kappa K)$ with $\kappa=1/(8-2M_5)$. $W$ acts on each slice
$s$ in the same way and does not mix slices. Its free spectrum, needed in
Sec.~\ref{sec:dagger}, is $\{-M_5+\sum_\mu(1-\cos p_\mu)\pm i(\sum_\mu\sin^2p_\mu)^{1/2}\}$.

\paragraph{The operator.} With
\begin{equation}
D_+=1+bW,\qquad D_-=cW-1
\label{eq:Dpm}
\end{equation}
(Ref.~\cite{BNO}, Eq.~(2.18)), the M\"obius operator at quark mass $m$ acts slice by slice as
\begin{equation}
(D\psi)_s=D_+\psi_s+D_-\big(\Pm\psi_{s+1}+\Pp\psi_{s-1}\big),\qquad
\psi_{L_s}\equiv-m\,\psi_0,\quad \psi_{-1}\equiv-m\,\psi_{L_s-1}
\label{eq:Dslice}
\end{equation}
(Ref.~\cite{BNO}, Eq.~(2.20), with $s$ counted from $0$). For $L_s=4$, as a matrix in $s$,
\begin{equation}
D=\begin{pmatrix}
D_+ & D_-\Pm & 0 & -mD_-\Pp\\
D_-\Pp & D_+ & D_-\Pm & 0\\
0 & D_-\Pp & D_+ & D_-\Pm\\
-mD_-\Pm & 0 & D_-\Pp & D_+
\end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:Dmatrix}
\end{equation}
Shamir's operator is $b=1$, $c=0$; the results of this note use $b=1.5$, $c=0.5$
($b-c=1$, $b+c=2$). The quark mass enters only through the two corners. The physical
four-dimensional quark field is $q=\Pm\psi_0+\Pp\psi_{L_s-1}$.

\paragraph{The hop in the fifth dimension.} Eq.~\eqref{eq:Dslice} separates into a part
that hops in the fifth dimension and one that does not. Define
\begin{equation}
(Y\psi)_s=-\Pm\psi_{s+1}-\Pp\psi_{s-1}\qquad\text{(with the same wrap-around)},
\label{eq:Y}
\end{equation}
the off-diagonal part of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Dmatrix} without $D_-$ and with a minus sign. Then
$D=D_+-D_-Y$. The order matters: $D_-$ stands to the \emph{left} of $Y$, since in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Dslice} $D_-$ acts on the shifted and projected field. Inserting
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:W} and \eqref{eq:Dpm},
\begin{equation}
D=1+bW-(cW-1)Y=1+Y+W\,(b-cY)=C-\tfrac12K\,(b-cY),\qquad C=d_++d_-Y,
\label{eq:D}
\end{equation}
with $d_+=1+b(4-M_5)$ and $d_-=1-c(4-M_5)$. In the last step $W=(4-M_5)-\frac12K$ was split
into its site-local constant and its hop; the constant parts form $C$. Note
$d_-=-(c(4-M_5)-1)$ is \emph{minus} the site-local part of $D_-$, because $Y$ carries the
sign. The Pauli--Villars operator is the same operator at unit mass, $\Dpv=D|_{m=1}$,
$\Cpv=C|_{m=1}$; $D_\pm$ and $K$ do not depend on $m$.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$\Gfive$}{Gamma5}-transformation and the exact adjoint}
\label{sec:g5}
For the Wilson operator $\gamma_5W\gamma_5=W\dg$, so a solve with $W\dg$ is a solve with $W$
between two multiplications by $\gamma_5$. The analogue for domain-wall fermions uses
\begin{equation}
\Gfive=\gamma_5R_5,\qquad (R_5\psi)_s=\psi_{L_s-1-s} .
\end{equation}
Each building block transforms as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $W$, $D_+$, $D_-$: they act on every slice alike, so they commute with $R_5$, and
$\gamma_5W\gamma_5=W\dg$. Hence $\Gfive W\Gfive=W\dg$, $\Gfive D_\pm\Gfive=D_\pm\dg$.
\item $Y$: it has no hop in the four dimensions, so $\Gfive Y\Gfive$ can be read off
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Y}. $\gamma_5$ commutes with $P_\pm$; $R_5$ turns the hop $s\to s+1$ into
$s\to s-1$. So $\Gfive Y\Gfive$ has $-\Pm$ on the hop down and $-\Pp$ on the hop up,
and the corner $-m$ moves to the other corner. This is the transpose of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Y} in $s$ with $P_\pm$ in place, which is $Y\dg$ since $P_\pm\dg=P_\pm$ and $m$
is real: $\Gfive Y\Gfive=Y\dg$.
\item $W$ and $Y$ do not commute: $\gamma_\mu$ in $K$ anticommutes with $\gamma_5$, so
$\gamma_\mu P_\pm=P_\mp\gamma_\mu$, and the hop of $K$ exchanges the two projectors of $Y$.
\end{enumerate}
From $D=D_+-D_-Y$ it follows that
\begin{equation}
\Gfive D\Gfive=D_+\dg-D_-\dg Y\dg ,\qquad\text{whereas}\qquad D\dg=D_+\dg-Y\dg D_-\dg ,
\label{eq:g5D}
\end{equation}
the adjoint reversing the order of the product. The difference is a commutator,
\begin{equation}
\Gfive D\Gfive-D\dg=Y\dg D_-\dg-D_-\dg Y\dg=c\,[Y\dg,W\dg]=\tfrac{c}{2}\big[K\dg,Y\dg\big],
\label{eq:g5herm}
\end{equation}
where $D_-\dg=cW\dg-1$ and $W\dg=(4-M_5)-\frac12K\dg$ were used. It vanishes for Shamir's
operator ($c=0$) and is of order $c$ otherwise. Thus $\Gfive D\Gfive\neq D\dg$ for M\"obius
fermions: a multigrid built for $D$ cannot be used for $D\dg$ by conjugating with $\Gfive$.
\ifresults{} On a $12^4$ configuration with stout links ($M_5=1$, $m=0.001$),
$\|\Gfive D\Gfive b-D\dg b\|/\|D\dg b\|=0.15$ for $(b,c)=(1.5,0.5)$, $0.22$ for $(2,1)$,
and $0$ for $(1,0)$, for a random $b$.\fi

\paragraph{The exact relation.} $D_+$ and $D_-$ are functions of the same $W$ and commute.
Where $D_-$ is invertible, factor it out to the left:
\begin{equation}
D=D_-\,K_{D},\qquad K_{D}=D_-^{-1}D_+-Y .
\end{equation}
$K_D$ is $\Gfive$-Hermitian: by item 1, $\Gfive D_-^{-1}D_+\Gfive=(D_-\dg)^{-1}D_+\dg$, and
this equals $(D_-^{-1}D_+)\dg=D_+\dg(D_-\dg)^{-1}$ because $D_+\dg$ and $D_-\dg$ commute;
with item 2, $\Gfive K_D\Gfive=K_D\dg$. Then
\begin{equation}
D\dg=K_D\dg D_-\dg=\Gfive K_D\Gfive\,D_-\dg=\Gfive D_-^{-1}D\,\Gfive D_-\dg ,
\end{equation}
and multiplying by $D_-\Gfive$ from the left,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;D_-\,\Gfive\,D\dg=D\,\Gfive\,D_-\dg\;}
\label{eq:g5exact}
\end{equation}
This form has no inverse, holds for any $m$ (so also for $\Dpv$, with the same $D_-$), and can
be checked by applying both sides to a vector.\ifresults{} On the configuration above both sides
agree to $3\times10^{-16}$ for $(b,c)=(1.5,0.5)$ and $(2,1)$.\fi{} The Hermitian operator
of the M\"obius case is $\Gfive K_D=\Gfive D_-^{-1}D$; Ref.~\cite{BNO}, Eq.~(2.22), defines
its $\Gamma_5$ as $\gamma_5R_5D_-^{-1}$ for that reason. It contains $D_-^{-1}$, whose cost
is discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:dagger}.

\subsection{The site-diagonal part and its inverse}
\label{sec:C}
$C=d_++d_-Y$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:D} is what remains of $D$ when the four-dimensional hops are
dropped. It acts within one four-dimensional site, as an $L_s\times L_s$ matrix in $s$ times
the identity in color, and it commutes with $P_\pm$ (only $Y$ is involved, and $Y$ maps
$\Pp\psi$ to $\Pp\psi$ and $\Pm\psi$ to $\Pm\psi$). So it splits into a block on
$\psi^+=\Pp\psi$ and one on $\psi^-=\Pm\psi$. From Eq.~\eqref{eq:Y}, $Y$ moves $\psi^+$ from
$s-1$ to $s$ and $\psi^-$ from $s+1$ to $s$, with the factor $-1$, and the wrap-around gives
$+m$:
\begin{equation}
(C\psi)^+_s=\begin{cases}d_+\psi^+_0+m\,d_-\psi^+_{L_s-1}, & s=0,\\
d_+\psi^+_s-d_-\psi^+_{s-1}, & s\ge1,\end{cases}
\label{eq:Cblocks}
\end{equation}
a lower bidiagonal matrix with one corner element; for $\psi^-$ the same with
$s\to L_s-1-s$ (upper bidiagonal). For $L_s=4$ the block on $\psi^+$ is
\begin{equation}
C^+=\begin{pmatrix}d_+&0&0&m\,d_-\\-d_-&d_+&0&0\\0&-d_-&d_+&0\\0&0&-d_-&d_+\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{The inverse.} To solve $C^+x=r$, write the rows $s\ge1$ as
$x_s=(r_s+d_-x_{s-1})/d_+$. Starting from an unknown $x_0$, each $x_s$ is affine in $x_0$:
with $q=d_-/d_+$,
\begin{equation}
x_s=p_s+q^{s}x_0,\qquad p_0=0,\quad p_s=(r_s+d_-p_{s-1})/d_+ ,
\end{equation}
which is checked by induction: $(r_s+d_-(p_{s-1}+q^{s-1}x_0))/d_+=p_s+q^sx_0$. The row $s=0$,
$d_+x_0+m\,d_-x_{L_s-1}=r_0$, with $x_{L_s-1}=p_{L_s-1}+q^{L_s-1}x_0$, fixes $x_0$:
\begin{equation}
x_0=\frac{r_0-m\,d_-\,p_{L_s-1}}{d_++m\,d_-\,q^{L_s-1}},\qquad x_s=p_s+q^{s}x_0 .
\label{eq:Cinv}
\end{equation}
For $C^-$ the substitution runs from $s=L_s-1$ downwards. $C\dg$ has the transposed blocks:
on $\psi^+$ an upper bidiagonal matrix, solved like $C^-$, and on $\psi^-$ a lower one. $C^{-1}$
is thus exact, costs $O(L_s)$ per site, and does not depend on the gauge field~\cite{KCM2024}.
It exists unless $d_++m\,d_-q^{L_s-1}=0$.

\subsection{The preconditioned operator}
The spectrum of $D$ surrounds the origin (Sec.~\ref{sec:spectra}), so Krylov methods and
polynomial smoothers cannot work on it directly. Ref.~\cite{KCM2024} multiplies $D\psi=\eta$
from the left by $(\Dpv\Cpv^{-1})\dg$ and substitutes $\psi=C^{-1}y$:
\begin{equation}
A'y=(\Dpv\Cpv^{-1})\dg\,\eta,\qquad A'=(\Dpv\Cpv^{-1})\dg\,D\,C^{-1},\qquad \psi=C^{-1}y .
\label{eq:Aprime}
\end{equation}
Why these two factors:
\begin{itemize}
\item $DC^{-1}$ is $D$ with its site-local part divided out; its eigenvalues are much more
compact than those of $D$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:pv}: real parts $[-0.22,2.22]$ against
$[-1.29,13.2]$), but still on both sides of the origin.
\item For $m=1$, $DC^{-1}=\Dpv\Cpv^{-1}$ and $A'=(\Dpv\Cpv^{-1})\dg(\Dpv\Cpv^{-1})$, which is
Hermitian and positive definite. For $m<1$ the two factors differ only in the corners of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Dmatrix}, and $A'$ is a perturbation of a positive operator.
\item $\Dpv^{-1}D$ approximates the overlap operator~\cite{BCHW}; $\Dpv\dg$ in place of
$\Dpv^{-1}$ keeps the cost of one application of $A'$ at two applications of $D$ plus the
site-local $C^{-1}$ and $\Cpv^{-1}$.
\end{itemize}
The result is an operator whose eigenvalues all have positive real part
(Fig.~\ref{fig:A}), with the smallest condition number among those of
Ref.~\cite{KCM2024}.

\subsection{Neff's \texorpdfstring{$\alpha$}{alpha}}
Neff's form of the domain-wall matrix~\cite{Neff,CKMN} is $D^{(\alpha)}=DQ$ with a
site-local $Q$, diagonal in the fifth dimension,
\begin{equation}
Q_0=\Pm+\alpha\Pp,\qquad Q_s=\alpha\ \ (0<s<L_s-1),\qquad Q_{L_s-1}=\Pp+\alpha\Pm .
\label{eq:Q}
\end{equation}
Properties used below, each read off Eq.~\eqref{eq:Q}:
\begin{itemize}
\item $Q$ is Hermitian, and $Q^{-1}$ is Eq.~\eqref{eq:Q} with $\alpha\to1/\alpha$.
\item $Q$ leaves the components $\Pm\psi_0$ and $\Pp\psi_{L_s-1}$ unchanged, which make up the
quark field $q$; so $D^{(\alpha)}$ and $D$ have the same four-dimensional propagator.
\item The solution of $D\psi=\eta$ is $\psi=Q\psi^{(\alpha)}$ with
$D^{(\alpha)}\psi^{(\alpha)}=\eta$, since $D^{(\alpha)}\psi^{(\alpha)}=DQ\psi^{(\alpha)}=D\psi$.
\item $Q$ is site-local, so the site-diagonal part of $DQ$ is $CQ$, with
$(CQ)^{-1}=Q^{-1}C^{-1}$ and $(CQ)\dg=QC\dg$.
\end{itemize}
The factor may be applied to the quark operator alone or to the Pauli--Villars operator as
well; we write $\Dpv^{(\alpha)}=\Dpv Q$ in the latter case. For $\alpha<1$ it improves the
conditioning of the even-odd operator~\cite{Neff,CKMN}.

\section{Even-odd forms}
\label{sec:eo}

With $D=\begin{pmatrix}\Ce & D_{eo}\\ D_{oe} & \Co\end{pmatrix}$,
$D_{pq}=-\tfrac12K_{pq}(b-cY)$, the Schur complement on the even sites is
$S=\Ce-D_{eo}\Co^{-1}D_{oe}$, and $\Spv$ is that of $\Dpv$; note
$S^{-1}=\pi D^{-1}\iota$ and $(\Spv\dg)^{-1}=\pi(\Dpv\dg)^{-1}\iota$. The
site-diagonal factor can stand on either side of $S$, which gives the right and
the left form,
\begin{equation}
\AR=(\Spv\Cpve^{-1})\dg\,(S\,\Ce^{-1}),\qquad
\AL=(\Cpve^{-1}\Spv)\dg\,(\Ce^{-1}S).
\label{eq:AR}
\end{equation}
$\AR$ is the even-odd counterpart of $A'$; $\Ce^{-1}S$ is the usual left
preconditioned even-odd operator, which Boyle and Yamaguchi found inferior to $S$
itself for CG on the normal equation~\cite{BY}. With $\alpha$, $DQ$ has Schur
complement $SQ_e$ and diagonal block $\Ce Q_e$, so $\AR$ is independent of
$\alpha$, whereas
\begin{equation}
\AL^{(\alpha)}=
\begin{cases}
Q_e\,(\Cpve^{-1}\Spv)\dg\,Q_e^{-2}\,(\Ce^{-1}S)\,Q_e, & \alpha \text{ in } D \text{ and } \Dpv,\\[2pt]
(\Cpve^{-1}\Spv)\dg\,Q_e^{-1}\,(\Ce^{-1}S)\,Q_e, & \alpha \text{ in } D \text{ only}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:ALalpha}
\end{equation}
For $D\psi=\eta$, with $\eta'_e=\eta_e-D_{eo}\Co^{-1}\eta_o$, the right-hand side
is $\rho_R=(\Spv\Cpve^{-1})\dg\eta'_e$ or $\rho_L=(\Cpve^{-1}\Spv)\dg\Ce^{-1}\eta'_e$
(operators with $Q$ when $\alpha\neq1$); the solution is
$\psi^{(\alpha)}_e=\Ce^{-1}y$ (right) or $y$ (left),
$\psi^{(\alpha)}_o=\Co^{-1}(\eta_o-D_{oe}\psi^{(\alpha)}_e)$, $\psi=Q\psi^{(\alpha)}$.

\paragraph{Why both forms.} The two forms serve different purposes. The right
form is the even-odd counterpart of $A'$, and the factorized correction of
Sec.~\ref{sec:correction} is exact for it on the lifted basis
(Proposition~\ref{prop:exact}); but $Q$ cancels in it, so it cannot use Neff's
$\alpha$. The left form keeps $\alpha$ and is the one the outer solver uses in
practice: the basis is not exact for it, yet it converges within a few iterations
of the right form, and with $\alpha$ it can save iterations.\ifresults{} The measurements of
Sec.~\ref{sec:results} are on the left form unless stated.\fi

\section{The factorized coarse-grid correction and the prolongation}
\label{sec:correction}

\subsection{Why factorize}
Solving on the even sites halves the problem and improves the conditioning. The
Schur complement is a two-hop operator, so the methods in the literature make
different choices: HDCG coarsens the squared Schur complement, with a larger coarse
stencil~\cite{HDCG}, and the methods with a nearest-neighbor or two-hop coarse operator
coarsen an operator on the whole lattice: $\gamma_5R_5D$ (HDCR~\cite{HDCR}) or
$\Dpv\dg D$~\cite{BCHW,BoyleOare}. The factorized correction combines the two: the
outer operator is the even-odd one,
while the coarse operators are those of $D$ and $\Dpv$ themselves
(Table~\ref{tab:approaches}).

The point counts follow from the hops: an operator that reaches $k$ hops on the fine
lattice couples coarse sites whose coordinates differ by at most $1$ in each direction

and for $k\ge4$ all $3^4=81$.
By $S^{-1}=\pi D^{-1}\iota$, the inverse of the right form is
\begin{equation}
\AR^{-1}=\Ce\,S^{-1}\,(\Spv\dg)^{-1}\,\Cpve\dg
=\Ce\,\pi D^{-1}\iota\;\pi(\Dpv\dg)^{-1}\iota\;\Cpve\dg ,
\label{eq:ARinv}
\end{equation}
a product of two inverses of one-hop operators with site-local factors. The
Galerkin operator of $\AR$ itself, a four-hop operator on the fine lattice, would
have a much larger coarse stencil; the Galerkin operators of $D$ and $\Dpv$ are
nearest-neighbor, because $D$ is
nearest-neighbor in four dimensions and every aggregate contains the whole
fifth dimension. We therefore replace each inverse by its Galerkin
approximation $\Pro(\Pro\dg X\Pro)^{-1}\Pro\dg$. For both forms and any
$\alpha$, the correction of a residual $r$ on the even sites is
\begin{equation}
e=O_L\;\pi\Pro\,\Dc^{-1}\Pro\dg\iota\;\;O_M\;\;
  \pi\Pro\,\big(\Pro\dg\Dpv\dg\Pro\big)^{-1}\Pro\dg\iota\;\;O_R\,r,
\qquad \Dc=\Pro\dg D\Pro ,
\label{eq:corr}
\end{equation}
with the site-local factors of Table~\ref{tab:factors}. The coarse operators are
always those of the plain operators $D$ and $\Dpv$ (no $\alpha$), so one setup
serves both forms and every $\alpha$. Figure~\ref{fig:correction} follows a residual
through Eq.~\eqref{eq:corr}: it moves between the even sites, the whole lattice and
the coarse lattice twice, and the result of the first coarse solve, taken on the even
sites, is the right-hand side of the second.

\subsection{Aggregation and prolongation}
The fine lattice is divided into rectangular four-dimensional blocks; each block
contains the whole fifth dimension and becomes one coarse site. Each test vector
$v$ is split into its $\Gfive$ eigencomponents $v_\pm=\frac12(1\pm\Gfive)v$, and
within each block the $+$ parts and the $-$ parts are orthonormalized separately.
With $n_v$ test vectors a coarse site carries $2n_v$ components, the first $n_v$
of positive and the last $n_v$ of negative $\Gfive$-chirality. Writing
$\Sigma=\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf 1_{n_v},-\mathbf 1_{n_v})$ on each coarse site,
\begin{equation}
\Gfive\Pro=\Pro\Sigma ,
\label{eq:GP}
\end{equation}
so that $\Pro\dg(\Gfive X\Gfive)\Pro=\Sigma\,\Pro\dg X\Pro\,\Sigma$ for any fine
operator $X$; in particular
$\Pro\dg\Dpv\dg\Pro=\Sigma\Dcpv\Sigma$ with $\Dcpv=\Pro\dg\Dpv\Pro$ whenever
$\Gfive\Dpv\Gfive=\Dpv\dg$, i.e.\ for $c=0$. Because
$v_\pm(L_s-1-s)=\pm\gamma_5v_\pm(s)$, each part is fixed by half of the fifth
dimension, so $\Pro$ need only be stored for $s<L_s/2$. On the even sites the
transfer operators are $\Pro\dg\iota$ and $\pi\Pro$.

\subsection{The basis $\{s,\hat t\}$}
\label{sec:basis}
The prolongation is built from vectors $y_j$ on the even sites that approximate
low modes of $\AR$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:setup}). Since $\Pro$ and the coarse operators act on
the whole lattice, each $y$ is extended, or lifted, to the whole lattice, the odd sites
being filled so that the fine operator maps the lifted vector to the even-odd quantity,
as $\psi_o$ is reconstructed from $\psi_e$ after an even-odd solve; and since the
correction~\eqref{eq:corr} contains two Galerkin solves, with $\Dpv\dg$ and with $D$, each
exact only on vectors in $\operatorname{range}\Pro$, each $y$ is lifted in two ways, one
for each solve:
\begin{align}
s&=\big(\Ce^{-1}y,\;-\Co^{-1}D_{oe}\Ce^{-1}y\big),
&& Ds=\iota\,S\Ce^{-1}y,
\label{eq:s}\\
\hat t&=\big(a,\;-(\Cpvo\dg)^{-1}(D_{\mathrm{PV},eo})\dg a\big),\quad a=\pi Ds,
&& \Dpv\dg\hat t=\iota\,\Spv\dg a ,
\label{eq:that}
\end{align}
where the right-hand columns follow from the block form of $D$ and of
$\Dpv\dg$, whose odd-even block is $(D_{\mathrm{PV},eo})\dg$ and whose odd
diagonal block is $\Cpvo\dg$. The images of $s$ under $D$ and of $\hat t$ under
$\Dpv\dg$ thus vanish on the odd sites. The first coarse solve of the correction
returns $\hat t$ and the second $s$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:exact}); $\hat t$ is made from
$Ds$, so the two lifts follow the order of the solves. With the $y$ alone in the basis,
each factor of the correction would be an approximation and the errors of the two
would multiply. The structure is familiar from the block preconditioners of the
incompressible Navier--Stokes equations~\cite{ESW}: the velocity is eliminated, as the odd sites are
here, and the pressure Schur complement is approximated by a product of simpler
operators, each solved by its own multigrid; in the least-squares commutator
preconditioner, $(BB^{\mathsf T})^{-1}(BFB^{\mathsf T})(BB^{\mathsf T})^{-1}$, two
Poisson-like inverses. The product is only as good as each factor on the vectors it is
applied to, which here are $\hat t$ and $s$. The $2n$ vectors $\{s_j,\hat t_j\}$ are
the test vectors, $n_v=2n$.

\begin{proposition}[Exactness, right form]
\label{prop:exact}
Let $s,\hat t\in\operatorname{range}\Pro$, let $\Dc$ and
$\Pro\dg\Dpv\dg\Pro$ be invertible, and let both coarse systems in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:corr} be solved exactly with the exact adjoint
$\Pro\dg\Dpv\dg\Pro=(\Dcpv)\dg$. Then the right-form correction of
$r=\AR y$ is $e=y$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For any invertible $\Pro\dg X\Pro$, $\Pro(\Pro\dg X\Pro)^{-1}\Pro\dg X$ is the
identity on $\operatorname{range}\Pro$. Now
$O_Rr=\Cpve\dg\AR y=\Spv\dg S\Ce^{-1}y=\Spv\dg a$, and
$\iota\Spv\dg a=\Dpv\dg\hat t$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:that}. The first coarse solve
returns $\pi\hat t=a=\pi Ds$. With $O_M=1$ the second has
$\Pro\dg\iota\pi Ds=\Pro\dg Ds$ on the right, since $Ds$ vanishes on the odd sites,
and returns $\pi s=\Ce^{-1}y$; $O_L=\Ce$ gives $y$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Conditions in practice]
The proposition needs (i) $s,\hat t$ in the range of the stored $\Pro$, which
holds only up to the rounding of the reduced-precision storage of $\Pro$;
(ii) the exact adjoint of the Pauli--Villars coarse factor---the $\Sigma$-adjoint
$\Sigma\Dcpv\Sigma$ of Sec.~\ref{sec:coarse} equals it only if
$\Gfive\Dpv\Gfive=\Dpv\dg$, which by Eq.~\eqref{eq:g5herm} fails for $c\neq0$;
(iii) exact coarse solves, whereas they are stopped at a loose tolerance.\ifresults{}
Numerically, with both coarse factors solved on their exact adjoints to
$|r|^2/|b|^2<10^{-24}$ in double precision and $\Pro$ in half precision as the solver
stores it, the right-form correction of $r=\AR y_j$ returns $y_j$ to
$\|e-y_j\|/\|y_j\|=3\times10^{-4}$--$5\times10^{-4}$ on $4^3\times8$ ($2^4$ aggregates,
$M_5=1.8$, $m=0.05$) and $3\times10^{-4}$--$2.5\times10^{-3}$ on $12^4$ (setup~S,
$m=0.001$), against $3$--$37$ for random vectors: the proposition holds to the rounding
of the stored $\Pro$, so (i) costs about $10^{-3}$. (ii) costs nothing measurable
(Sec.~\ref{sec:herm}), and (iii) helps: with GCR(2), accurate coarse solves need more
iterations or stagnate (Secs.~\ref{sec:gcrk}, \ref{sec:sensitivity}).\else{} How
much each departure costs has not been analyzed.\fi
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Left form]
For the left form with $\alpha$ in both operators, the same argument requires
the quark lift $s'$ of $Q_ey'$ and the Pauli--Villars lift $\hat t'$ of
$u=\Cpve^{\dagger\,-1}Q_e^{-2}\Ce^{-1}SQ_ey'$ to lie in
$\operatorname{range}\Pro$ (Table~\ref{tab:factors} gives $O_Mu=SQ_ey'$). For
$\alpha=1$ and $y'=\Ce^{-1}y$ one has $s'=s$, but
$u=\Cpve^{\dagger\,-1}\Ce^{-1}a$ differs from $a$ by a site-local matrix that
mixes $s$ and spin, so $\hat t'$ is in general not in the span of the
right-form vectors, and the correction is not exact for the left form with the basis
above.\ifresults{} Numerically (as in the previous remark, $\alpha=1$), for
$r=\AL y'$ with $y'=\Ce^{-1}y_j$ the correction misses $y'$ by
$\|e-y'\|/\|y'\|=0.05$--$0.18$ on $4^3\times8$ and $1.6$--$24$ on $12^4$, the latter
not much better than random vectors ($8$--$35$). The left form converges nonetheless:
it has the spectrum of the right form and needs one to five iterations more or fewer
(Secs.~\ref{sec:spectrum}, \ref{sec:gcrk}, \ref{sec:sensitivity}), so the exactness of
Proposition~\ref{prop:exact} is not needed for the cycle to work; whether a basis exact for
both forms at once exists is left open.\fi
\end{remark}

\subsection{The coarse operators}
$\Dc=\Pro\dg D\Pro$ and $\Dcpv=\Pro\dg\Dpv\Pro$ are assembled from the hops of
the fine operator between neighboring blocks and from its block-internal part:
on each coarse site a $2n_v\times2n_v$ diagonal block and one link block per
direction, nine blocks in all. The adjoint factor $\Pro\dg\Dpv\dg\Pro$ is not
assembled; it is applied either exactly, as the conjugate transpose $(\Dcpv)\dg$
of the stored blocks, or approximately as $\Sigma\Dcpv\Sigma$. When all extents
of the coarse lattice are even, the coarse hops connect coarse sites of opposite
parity and both coarse operators admit an even-odd decomposition of their own.

% ============================================================
\section{The coarse solvers}
\label{sec:coarse}

Both coarse systems are solved approximately, to a loose relative residual or a
maximum number of iterations, in reduced precision; reaching the tolerance is not
required. They are solved in sequence: first the Pauli--Villars factor, whose
solution enters the right-hand side of the quark factor. This section lists the
options; the default, used for all results unless stated, is BiCGStab on the even-odd
form of the quark factor and MINRES on the $\Sigma$-Hermitian form of the
Pauli--Villars factor, both to $|r|^2/|b|^2<10^{-2}$.

\subsection{The quark factor}
Write $\Dc=\begin{pmatrix}A_{ee}&B_{eo}\\ B_{oe}&A_{oo}\end{pmatrix}$ on the
coarse lattice. The system $\Dc c=g$ is solved through its Schur complement,
\begin{equation}
\big(A_{ee}-B_{eo}A_{oo}^{-1}B_{oe}\big)\,c_e=g_e-B_{eo}A_{oo}^{-1}g_o,\qquad
c_o=A_{oo}^{-1}(g_o-B_{oe}c_e),
\label{eq:coarseeo}
\end{equation}
with BiCGStab or truncated flexible GCR, preconditioned by the exact inverses of
the diagonal blocks $A_{ee}^{-1}$. $\Dc$ is not Hermitian and $\Sigma\Dc$ is
Hermitian only for $c=0$, so a non-Hermitian Krylov method is used.

\subsection{The Pauli--Villars factor}
The system is $(\Pro\dg\Dpv\dg\Pro)\,w=g$. Two treatments are available.

\paragraph{Exact adjoint.} Solve $(\Dcpv)\dg w=g$ with a non-Hermitian Krylov
method, preconditioned by the inverses of the adjoint diagonal blocks,
$(A_{xx}\dg)^{-1}=(A_{xx}^{-1})\dg$.

\paragraph{$\Sigma$-Hermitian form.} Replace the adjoint by $\Sigma\Dcpv\Sigma$
and solve the equivalent system
\begin{equation}
\Hc\,w=\Sigma g,\qquad \Hc=\Dcpv\Sigma ,
\label{eq:Hc}
\end{equation}
with MINRES. $\Hc$ is Hermitian (indefinite) exactly when
$(\Dcpv)\dg=\Sigma\Dcpv\Sigma$, i.e.\ for $c=0$ by Eqs.~\eqref{eq:g5herm} and
\eqref{eq:GP}; for $c\neq0$ MINRES is applied to a non-Hermitian matrix and has
no guarantee.\ifresults{} On the coarse space the non-Hermitian part is a few
percent, up to $14\%$ in the directions of the smallest singular values, and linear in
$c$; MINRES on $\Hc$ nevertheless needs the same outer iterations at $c=0.5$ as for
the Shamir operator and is better than the exact adjoint with BiCGStab
(Sec.~\ref{sec:herm}). The absolute-value block preconditioner and the additive level
preconditioner below, also exact only for $c=0$, are used as they are; their losses
were not measured separately.\fi The preconditioner must be Hermitian positive definite; we use the
absolute value of the diagonal blocks~\cite{VK}, $|\mathcal H_{xx}|^{-1}=
V|\Lambda|^{-1}V\dg$ with $\frac12(\mathcal H_{xx}+\mathcal H_{xx}\dg)=V\Lambda V\dg$
on each coarse site. On a coarse lattice with even extents, the Schur
complement of $\Hc$ on the even sites is $S_{\mathrm c}\Sigma_e$, with
$S_{\mathrm c}$ the Schur complement of $\Dcpv$; one solves for
$u_e=\Sigma w_e$ on the even sites, completes $u$ on the odd sites as in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:coarseeo}, and sets $w=\Sigma u$.

\subsection{Levels below the coarse lattice}
Both factors may be preconditioned by further levels. The coarse sites are
grouped into super-blocks; on each super-block, local vectors are formed from
the coarse test vectors $\Pro\dg s_j$ and $\Pro\dg\hat t_j$, split by $\Sigma$
and orthonormalized (Gram--Schmidt applied twice); the level operators are
obtained by probing the nearest-neighbor stencil; the last level is inverted
densely. With $Q_{\ell}$ the prolongation into level $\ell-1$ and $B_\ell$ the
diagonal blocks of level $\ell$:

\emph{Quark factor.} A V-cycle with the coarse correction followed by one
block-Jacobi sweep on the updated residual, without pre-smoothing,
\begin{equation}
z=Q_{\ell+1}M_{\ell+1}Q_{\ell+1}\dg r,\qquad
M_\ell r=z+B_\ell^{-1}(r-\mathcal D_\ell z);
\end{equation}
on the coarse lattice itself the same multiplicative combination is used, and
with the even-odd coarse form its even part preconditions the Schur system. The
level test vectors can optionally be refined by a few GCR steps preconditioned
by the levels already built.

\emph{Pauli--Villars factor ($\Sigma$-Hermitian form only).} The additive
Hermitian positive preconditioner
\begin{equation}
M_\ell=|B_\ell|^{-1}+Q_{\ell+1}M_{\ell+1}Q_{\ell+1}\dg,\qquad
M_{\ell_{\max}}=|\mathcal H_{\ell_{\max}}|^{-1},
\end{equation}
in the spirit of absolute-value preconditioning~\cite{VK}.

% ============================================================
\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

The setup produces the vectors $y_1,\dots,y_n$ on the even sites, then the basis
$\{s_j,\hat t_j\}$, $\Pro$, and the coarse operators. It always uses the right
form; the same setup serves the left forms and every $\alpha$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:correction}). It has two stages: a Chebyshev filter finds approximate
low modes without any multigrid, and a bootstrap then improves them with the cycle
built from them, so that they also cover what the first coarse space misses.

\subsection{Chebyshev-filtered subspace iteration}
The vectors approximate the right singular vectors of the smallest singular
values of $\AR$, i.e.\ eigenvectors of the Hermitian positive operator
$H_n=\AR\dg\AR$. With the Chebyshev polynomial $T_d$ and
$\ell(x)=\big(2x-(\lambda_{\rm hi}+\lambda_{\rm lo})\big)/(\lambda_{\rm hi}-\lambda_{\rm lo})$,
$|T_d(\ell(x))|\le1$ on $[\lambda_{\rm lo},\lambda_{\rm hi}]$ while components
below $\lambda_{\rm lo}$ grow; $T_d(\ell(H_n))$ is applied by the three-term
recurrence.

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{\textsc{LowModes}: Chebyshev-filtered subspace iteration on $H_n=\AR\dg\AR$.}
\label{alg:lowmodes}
\begin{algorithmic}
\State $X\gets$ $n$ random vectors on the even sites
\State $\lambda_{\rm hi}\gets$ a bound slightly above $\lambda_{\max}(H_n)$ (power iteration);\quad $\lambda_{\rm lo}\gets$ a small fraction of $\lambda_{\rm hi}$
\For{each round}
  \State $d\gets$ a degree proportional to $\sqrt{\lambda_{\rm hi}/\lambda_{\rm lo}}$, within fixed bounds
  \State $X\gets T_d(\ell(H_n))\,X$;\quad orthonormalize $X$ through its Gram matrix
  \State $G\gets X\dg H_nX=V\Theta V\dg$;\quad $X\gets XV$ \Comment{Rayleigh--Ritz}
  \State $\lambda_{\rm lo}\gets\theta_{\max}$ \Comment{largest Ritz value}
\EndFor
\State \Return $X$, $\Theta$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Bootstrap}
Each bootstrap round builds the basis and the two-grid cycle of
Sec.~\ref{sec:smoother} from the current $y$, replaces every $y_j$ by the result
of a fixed small number of FGMRES steps on $\AR x=y_j$ from $x=0$ preconditioned
by that cycle, and orthonormalizes the new $y$ through their Gram matrix
($y\leftarrow yV\Lambda^{-1/2}$ with $y\dg y=V\Lambda V\dg$). The cycle damps the
components it already handles, so the new vectors are enriched in what it does not.

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Setup of the two-grid method.}
\label{alg:setup}
\begin{algorithmic}
\State $y\gets\textsc{LowModes}(H_n)$
\For{each bootstrap round}
  \State build $\Pro$ from $\{s_j,\hat t_j\}$ of $y$ and the cycle $\mathcal M$ on it
  \For{$j=1,\dots,n$}
    \State $y_j\gets$ a few FGMRES steps on $\AR x=y_j$, preconditioned by $\mathcal M$
  \EndFor
  \State orthonormalize $y$
\EndFor
\State compute $s_j,\hat t_j$ by Eqs.~\eqref{eq:s}--\eqref{eq:that}; split by $\Gfive$; orthonormalize per block $\to\Pro$
\State assemble $\Dc$, $\Dcpv$, the inverses of their diagonal blocks, their even-odd forms
\State optionally, build the levels below from $\Pro\dg s_j$, $\Pro\dg\hat t_j$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

% ============================================================
\section{The Chebyshev smoother and the cycle}
\label{sec:smoother}

\subsection{Smoother}
The smoother is $\nu$ steps of Chebyshev iteration~\cite{Manteuffel,Saad} for
$\tilde Ax=b$ from $x=0$, i.e.\ a fixed polynomial $x=p(\tilde A)b$ of degree
$\nu-1$. It is defined by an ellipse with real foci
$[\lambda_{\rm lo},\lambda_{\rm hi}]$: $\lambda_{\rm hi}$ slightly above an
estimate of the spectral radius of $\tilde A$ from a power iteration, and
$\lambda_{\rm lo}=\lambda_{\rm hi}/\kappa_s$ for a chosen ratio $\kappa_s$. With
$\theta=\frac12(\lambda_{\rm hi}+\lambda_{\rm lo})$,
$\delta=\frac12(\lambda_{\rm hi}-\lambda_{\rm lo})$, $\sigma=\theta/\delta$,
$\varrho_0=1/\sigma$, $r_0=b$ and $d_0=r_0/\theta$, step $k$ is
\begin{equation}
x\leftarrow x+d_k,\quad r_{k+1}=r_k-\tilde Ad_k,\quad
\varrho_{k+1}=\frac{1}{2\sigma-\varrho_k},\quad
d_{k+1}=\varrho_{k+1}\varrho_k\,d_k+\frac{2\varrho_{k+1}}{\delta}\,r_{k+1},
\end{equation}
the last step only updating $x$; $\nu$ steps cost $\nu-1$ applications of
$\tilde A$. Only a real focal interval is used. The ratio $\kappa_s$ is not derived
from the operator and has to be chosen for it: the polynomial damps the part of the
spectrum above $\lambda_{\rm lo}$ and leaves the rest to the coarse correction, and
too wide or too narrow an interval can make the outer solve stagnate.\ifresults{}
The two setups of Sec.~\ref{sec:results} need $\kappa_s\approx10$ ($M_5=1$, stout)
and $\kappa_s\approx30$ ($M_5=1.8$); the first stagnates with the second's interval,
the second needs nearly three times the iterations with the first's
(Sec.~\ref{sec:sensitivity}).\fi

\subsection{Cycle}
\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Two-grid cycle $y=\mathcal Mb$ for $\tilde A$.}
\label{alg:cycle}
\begin{algorithmic}
\State $y\gets p(\tilde A)\,b$ \Comment{pre-smoothing}
\State $r\gets b-\tilde Ay$
\State $y\gets y+e(r)$ \Comment{correction~\eqref{eq:corr}}
\State $r\gets b-\tilde Ay$
\State $y\gets y+p(\tilde A)\,r$ \Comment{post-smoothing}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

One cycle costs $2\nu$ applications of $\tilde A$ (two smoothings and two
residuals) plus the two coarse solves. Because the coarse solves are Krylov
iterations stopped by a residual test, $\mathcal M$ is not a fixed linear map.

% ============================================================
\section{The outer solver}
\label{sec:outer}

The outer iteration solves $\tilde Ay=\rho$ with the cycle as right
preconditioner, and $\psi$ is reconstructed as in Sec.~\ref{sec:eo}. Since
$\mathcal M$ varies from one application to the next, the outer method has to be
flexible: FGMRES or flexible GCR, which keep the preconditioned directions;
BiCGStab with the cycle as preconditioner is also available but has no flexible
counterpart of its convergence theory. Flexibility alone gives no convergence
guarantee. For a fixed $\mathcal M$, restarted or truncated methods converge for
every restart length if the field of values of $\tilde A\mathcal M$ lies in the right
half-plane~\cite{ESS}; otherwise only the full method is covered, by the spectrum
and the pseudospectra of $\tilde A\mathcal M$~\cite{NRT,TrefethenEmbree}, and a
truncated one can stagnate~\cite{Embree}.\ifresults{} For the present cycle the
field of values is not in the right half-plane, the pseudospectra do bound the
full method, and GCR(2) needs the coarse solves inexact: with accurate ones it needs
more iterations or stagnates (Secs.~\ref{sec:fov}--\ref{sec:gcrk}).\fi{} Block variants treat several
right-hand sides together.
Convergence is monitored on the even-odd system; the residual of the original
equation, $\|D\psi-\eta\|/\|\eta\|$, is evaluated with the plain $D$ after
reconstruction. The reference solver is CG on the normal equation of the
even-odd operator $\Ce^{-1}S$ (CGNE), which is robust but squares the condition
number; it runs in double precision or in mixed precision, CG in single precision
with reliable updates in double precision.\ifresults{} For the systems with $\AR\dg$ (the normal
equations of HMC) RutticeQCD uses the cycle with $D$ and $\Dpv$ exchanged on the same
$\Pro$ and the same smoother interval; with exact coarse solves it is the adjoint of
$\mathcal M$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:fov}). How well it converges is an open problem
(Sec.~\ref{sec:open}).\fi

% ============================================================
\section{Solving with \texorpdfstring{$D\dg$}{D-dagger}}
\label{sec:dagger}

HMC with the ratio action $\det(D\dg D)/\det(\Dpv\dg\Dpv)$ needs $x=(D\dg D)^{-1}b$ at every
force evaluation, that is one solve with $D\dg$ and one with $D$. This section derives the
system that the solve with $D\dg$ becomes on the even sites, shows why the exact relation
of Sec.~\ref{sec:g5}, which replaces the shortcut $D\dg=\Gfive D\Gfive$ of Wilson fermions,
cannot be used directly, and asks which prolongation the solve with $D\dg$ needs. Everything here is at $\alpha=1$.

\subsection{The system on the even sites}
The block form of $D\dg$ is
\begin{equation}
D\dg=\begin{pmatrix}\Ce\dg & D_{oe}\dg\\ D_{eo}\dg & \Co\dg\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
since the $(e,o)$ block of $D\dg$ is the adjoint of the $(o,e)$ block of $D$. Eliminating
the odd sites of $D\dg w=r$ in the same way as for $D$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:eo}) gives
\begin{equation}
\big(\Ce\dg-D_{oe}\dg(\Co\dg)^{-1}D_{eo}\dg\big)\,w_e=r_e-D_{oe}\dg(\Co\dg)^{-1}r_o ,
\end{equation}
and the matrix on the left is $S\dg$, the adjoint of the Schur complement of $D$:
$S\dg=\Ce\dg-D_{oe}\dg(\Co\dg)^{-1}D_{eo}\dg$ term by term. Write
\begin{equation}
X=S\Ce^{-1},\qquad X_{\rm PV}=\Spv\Cpve^{-1},\qquad\text{so}\qquad \AR=X_{\rm PV}\dg X
\label{eq:XXpv}
\end{equation}
by Eq.~\eqref{eq:AR}. With $S\dg=\Ce\dg X\dg$, the even-site equation is
$X\dg w_e=(\Ce\dg)^{-1}r'_e$, $r'_e=r_e-D_{oe}\dg(\Co\dg)^{-1}r_o$. Putting
$w_e=X_{\rm PV}z$,
\begin{equation}
X\dg X_{\rm PV}\,z=(\Ce\dg)^{-1}r'_e,\qquad X\dg X_{\rm PV}=\big(X_{\rm PV}\dg X\big)\dg=\AR\dg ,
\label{eq:ARdag}
\end{equation}
and $w_o=(\Co\dg)^{-1}(r_o-D_{eo}\dg w_e)$. Thus the solve with $D\dg$ is a solve with
$\AR\dg$, and $\AR\dg$ is the right form~\eqref{eq:AR} with the roles of $D$ and $\Dpv$
exchanged: the same code with its two operators swapped. Its inverse, as
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ARinv},
\begin{equation}
(\AR\dg)^{-1}=X_{\rm PV}^{-1}(X\dg)^{-1}=\Cpve\,\Spv^{-1}\,(S\dg)^{-1}\,\Ce\dg
=\Cpve\,\pi\Dpv^{-1}\iota\;\pi(D\dg)^{-1}\iota\;\Ce\dg ,
\label{eq:ARdaginv}
\end{equation}
is again a product of two inverses of one-hop operators, now first $D\dg$ and then $\Dpv$,
and the factorized correction~\eqref{eq:corr} carries over with
$O_R=\Ce\dg$, $O_M=1$, $O_L=\Cpve$ and the coarse factors $\Pro\dg D\dg\Pro$ (first) and
$\Pro\dg\Dpv\Pro$ (second).

\subsection{The exact adjoint and why it cannot be used directly}
\label{sec:g5fail}
By Sec.~\ref{sec:g5}, $\Gfive D\Gfive\neq D\dg$ for $c\neq0$, and the exact relation
Eq.~\eqref{eq:g5exact} gives $D\dg=\Gfive D_-^{-1}D\,\Gfive D_-\dg$. Two consequences would
make one multigrid serve both $D$ and $D\dg$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Null vectors.} If $Dv\approx0$, then $D\dg w\approx0$ for
\begin{equation}
w=(D_-\dg)^{-1}\Gfive v ,
\label{eq:dualnull}
\end{equation}
because $D\dg w=\Gfive D_-^{-1}D\,\Gfive D_-\dg w=\Gfive D_-^{-1}Dv$. For $c=0$, $D_-=-1$ and
$w\propto\Gfive v$, the Wilson case.
\item \emph{Solves.} $D\dg w=r$ is equivalent to $Dz=D_-\Gfive r$, $w=(D_-\dg)^{-1}\Gfive z$:
a solve with $D$, by the multigrid of $D$, followed by a solve with $D_-\dg$.
\end{itemize}
Both need an inverse of $D_-$.

\paragraph{Why $D_-$ cannot be inverted.} $D_-=cW-1=c\,(W-1/c)$, and $W$ is the Wilson
operator of mass $-M_5$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:W}). So $D_-$ is, up to the factor $c$, the Wilson
operator of mass $m_0=-M_5-1/c$: $-3$ in setup~S ($M_5=1$, $c=0.5$) and $-3.8$ in setup~B.
Its free eigenvalues (Sec.~\ref{sec:operator})
\begin{equation}
m_0+\sum_\mu(1-\cos p_\mu)\pm i\Big(\sum_\mu\sin^2p_\mu\Big)^{1/2}
\end{equation}
have real parts filling $[m_0,m_0+8]$, here $[-3,5]$. The eigenvalues with real part near $0$
are those with $\sum_\mu(1-\cos p_\mu)\approx3$, between the doubler corners with two and with
four momenta at $\pi$, and they have imaginary parts of both signs: the spectrum surrounds
the origin. A Krylov method builds polynomials $p$ with $p(0)=1$ that should be small on the
spectrum; when the spectrum surrounds $0$ no such polynomial of low degree exists
(Sec.~\ref{sec:spectra}), and GCR or BiCGStab make no progress. The gauge field does not
change this picture.\ifresults{} On the A30 ($12^4$, as above), GCR(8) on $D_-$ and on
$D_-\dg$ stops at $|r|^2/|b|^2=0.74$ (S) and $0.96$ (B) after $20000$ iterations, and
BiCGStab diverges.\fi{} The inverse of $D_-$ is thus harder than the solve with $D$ it would
serve, and neither route above is practical.

What remains is the structure of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dualnull}. On smooth fields ($p\approx0$) the
free $D_-$ is the number $-(1+cM_5)$, so for the smooth part of a low mode $v$,
$w\approx-\Gfive v/(1+cM_5)$: $\Gfive v$ points along a null vector of $D\dg$ up to its rough
components.

\subsection{Which prolongation the solve with \texorpdfstring{$D\dg$}{D-dagger} needs}
\label{sec:dualbasis}
The correction for $\AR\dg$ is exact on the analogue of Proposition~\ref{prop:exact}, with
the lifts of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:s} and \eqref{eq:that} made with $D$ and $\Dpv$ exchanged: for a
vector $y'$ on the even sites,
\begin{equation}
s'=\big(\Cpve^{-1}y',\;-\Cpvo^{-1}D_{\mathrm{PV},oe}\Cpve^{-1}y'\big),\qquad
\hat t'=\big(a',\;-(\Co\dg)^{-1}D_{eo}\dg a'\big),\quad a'=\pi\Dpv s' ,
\end{equation}
so that $\Dpv s'=\iota\Spv\Cpve^{-1}y'$ and $D\dg\hat t'=\iota S\dg a'$. If $r=\AR\dg y'$ and
$s',\hat t'\in\operatorname{range}\Pro$, the correction returns $y'$, by the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:exact} with the roles exchanged. The question is which $y'$.

The test vectors $y_j$ of the setup approximate the right singular vectors of $\AR$ with small
singular values: the filter works on $\AR\dg\AR$. The right singular vectors of $\AR\dg$ are
the \emph{left} singular vectors of $\AR$: if $\AR v=\sigma u$ and $\AR\dg u=\sigma v$, then
$u$ is a right singular vector of $\AR\dg$ with the same $\sigma$. So the $y'$ needed are the
$u_j=\AR y_j/\sigma_j$, which the setup does not provide; computing them as $\AR y_j$
multiplies the error of $y_j$ in the high modes by $\sigma_{\rm high}/\sigma_j$, which is why
that basis is worse.\ifresults{} (Measured: worse than the $y_j$ themselves.)\fi{} For Wilson
fermions, or here for $c=0$ on the whole lattice, $\Gfive$ maps right singular vectors of $D$
to left ones, and a basis closed under $\Gfive$ contains both. With $c\neq0$
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dualnull} replaces $\Gfive$ by $(D_-\dg)^{-1}\Gfive$, which is close to
$\Gfive$ on smooth vectors; on the even sites the factors $\Ce$ and $X_{\rm PV}$ of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ARdag} enter as well. This suggests, without proving it, that the coarse space
of $\AR\dg$ needs the vectors $\Gfive y_j$ in addition to the $y_j$.

\paragraph{The chirality split does not provide it.} The prolongation is already closed under
$\Gfive$, $\Gfive\Pro=\Pro\Sigma$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:GP}): each lifted vector is split into its
$\Gfive$ components before orthonormalization. This does not put the lifts of $\Gfive y_j$
into $\operatorname{range}\Pro$, because the lifts do not commute with $\Gfive$:
$\Ce^{-1}\Gfive y\neq\Gfive\Ce^{-1}y$, since $\Gfive\Ce\Gfive=\Ce\dg\neq\Ce$. For the same
reason a Petrov--Galerkin correction with restriction $\Pro\dg$ and prolongation $\Gfive\Pro$
is the Galerkin one: $\Gfive\Pro=\Pro\Sigma$ has the range of $\Pro$. A Petrov--Galerkin
correction that differs would need a second set of stored vectors.

\section{Open problems}
\label{sec:open}

\ifresults
The following questions remain open. Those the measurements answer are discussed where
they arise: the left form in Sec.~\ref{sec:basis}, $c\neq0$ in Secs.~\ref{sec:coarse}
and \ref{sec:herm}, the field of values in Secs.~\ref{sec:spectra} and
\ref{sec:fov}--\ref{sec:pseudo}.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Choosing the smoother interval.} The interval
$[\lambda_{\rm hi}/\kappa_s,\lambda_{\rm hi}]$ is set from an estimate of the
spectral radius and a ratio chosen per setup, and the wrong ratio makes the solve
stagnate (Sec.~\ref{sec:sensitivity}); the setup works with singular values of $\AR$,
which for a non-normal operator need not match its eigenvalues. How to choose the
interval from the operator, which part of the low end is left to the smoother after the
coarse correction, and whether an ellipse with complex foci should replace the real
interval, are open.

\item \textbf{Even-odd smoother and coarse space.} Boyle and
Yamaguchi~\cite{BY} found that a red-black smoother combined with the HDCR
coarse correction was not effective, and attributed it to the eigenvectors of
the red-black operator and of $\gamma_5R_5D$ not being aligned. In the present
method the smoother and the correction both approximate $\tilde A^{-1}$ and the
coarse space is built from low modes of $\AR$, so that particular mismatch is
absent by construction; but the coarse operators are Galerkin projections of
$D$ and $\Dpv$, not of $\tilde A$. Whether the even-odd outer system is
preferable to the full-lattice $A'$ for this method has not been established.

\item \textbf{A rigorous bound, and larger volumes.} The pseudospectral bound rests on
Lanczos estimates of $\sigma_{\min}$, which approach it from above, and on the
eigenvalues farthest from the centre found by Arnoldi (Sec.~\ref{sec:pseudo}). A
bound from an inclusion theorem, and how the outliers of setup~S and the
pseudospectra develop beyond $16^4$, are open. The latter is only a matter of memory:
on $24^4$ the field of values of $\AR$ fits in the 24~GB of the A30 but the Arnoldi
iteration does not. A GPU with more memory would answer it with the programs of
Sec.~\ref{sec:reproduce} unchanged; so would the CPU, with enough memory, once the
Arnoldi and pseudospectrum options are carried over to the CPU version of the program.

\item \textbf{How many test vectors.} Local coherence suggests that the number of test
vectors an aggregate needs does not grow with the volume, yet $16$ suffice on $12^4$ and
on $24^3\times48$ even $24$ do not (Sec.~\ref{sec:sensitivity}). Whether the $16$ lowest
modes of a larger lattice cover too narrow a part of the spectrum, or whether the low
modes vary in the fifth dimension more than one vector per aggregate can follow (Eick and
Mawhinney~\cite{EickMawhinney} find the fifth-dimensional profiles of low modes to differ
between lattices), is open; a test vector split in $s$ as it is now split in
chirality would keep $s$ and $\hat t$ in the range of $\Pro$, and Lüscher's coherence
test would measure what the coarse space captures.

\item \textbf{Solving with $D\dg$.} The dual cycle used for $\AR\dg$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:outer}) uses the basis built for $D$, and the dual correction is not
exact on it: for $r=\AR\dg y_j$ it misses $y_j$ by $4$--$90$ on $4^3\times8$ and by
$20$--$2\times10^3$ (S) and $10^2$--$2\times10^4$ (B) on $12^4$, more than it misses
random vectors (medians $12$, $75$, $830$ against $4.5$, $13$, $90$; exact coarse solves,
as in the remarks after Proposition~\ref{prop:exact}). On $4^3\times8$ GCR(2) needs about
the same iterations with it on $\AR\dg$ as with $\mathcal M$ on $\AR$ ($16$ against
$15$); on $12^4$, setup~S, $m=0.001$, it does not converge in $1000$ iterations where
$\mathcal M$ on $\AR$ takes $23$ (both coarse factors by BiCGStab to $10^{-2}$, double
precision, no levels below the coarse lattice). In setup~B this program converged with
neither cycle, unlike the solver of Sec.~\ref{sec:results}, so it gives no comparison
there. A basis for $D\dg$, from the low modes of $\AR\AR\dg$ or closed under $\Gfive$,
and how the dual cycle behaves in the solves of HMC, are open.
\end{enumerate}
\else
The following are questions of theory and algorithm, not findings.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Exactness for the left form.} Proposition~\ref{prop:exact} holds
for the right form. For $\AL$ (with or without $\alpha$) the Pauli--Villars lift
that would be needed differs from $\hat t$ by site-local factors (second remark
of Sec.~\ref{sec:basis}). Whether a basis can be made exact for both forms at
once, and what is lost by using the right-form basis for the left form, is open.

\item \textbf{Loss of $\Gfive$-Hermiticity for $c\neq0$.} By
Eq.~\eqref{eq:g5herm}, $\Gfive D\Gfive\neq D\dg$ at $O(c)$. MINRES on
$\Hc=\Dcpv\Sigma$, the absolute-value block preconditioner (built from the
Hermitian part only), the additive level preconditioner, and the $\Sigma$-adjoint
in place of the exact adjoint are all exact only for $c=0$. The exactly Hermitian
operator $\Gfive D_-^{-1}D$~\cite{BNO} involves a non-local inverse. How large
the non-Hermitian part is on the coarse space, and how to treat it, is open.

\item \textbf{Smoother interval for a non-normal operator.} The interval
$[\lambda_{\rm hi}/\kappa_s,\lambda_{\rm hi}]$ is set from an estimate of the
spectral radius, whereas the setup works with singular values (eigenvalues of
$\AR\dg\AR$). For a non-normal $\tilde A$ these need not agree. Which part of
the low end is left to the smoother after the coarse correction, and whether an
ellipse with complex foci should replace the real interval, are open.

\item \textbf{Even-odd smoother and coarse space.} Boyle and
Yamaguchi~\cite{BY} found that a red-black smoother combined with the HDCR
coarse correction was not effective, and attributed it to the eigenvectors of
the red-black operator and of $\gamma_5R_5D$ not being aligned. In the present
method the smoother and the correction both approximate $\tilde A^{-1}$ and the
coarse space is built from low modes of $\AR$, so that particular mismatch is
absent by construction; but the coarse operators are Galerkin projections of
$D$ and $\Dpv$, not of $\tilde A$. Whether the even-odd outer system is
preferable to the full-lattice $A'$ for this method has not been established.

\item \textbf{Field of values versus real parts of eigenvalues.}
Section~\ref{sec:spectra} shows $\Re\lambda>0$ for $A'$. The GCR bound~\cite{ESS}
needs a positive definite Hermitian part, i.e.\ a field of values in the right
half-plane, which is a stronger condition. Whether it holds for $A'$, $\AR$ and
$\AL$, and whether a shift of the kind used in Ref.~\cite{BoyleOare} is needed,
is not known.

\item \textbf{Outer solver with a varying preconditioner.} Because the coarse
solves are truncated, $\mathcal M$ is nonlinear and depends on the right-hand
side. FGMRES and flexible GCR allow this; BiCGStab does not, and its behavior
with $\mathcal M$ as preconditioner has no theoretical justification.

\item \textbf{Solving with $D\dg$.} The method is built for $D\psi=\eta$. A
solve with $D\dg$, as needed for the normal equations in HMC, requires the dual
correction (the two factors and the roles of the lifts exchanged), and its
basis and exactness have not been derived.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The notation of other references}
\label{sec:notation}
Table~\ref{tab:notation} relates Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Y}--\eqref{eq:Q} to Brower, Neff
and Orginos~\cite{BNO}, to Kanamori, Chen and Matsufuru~\cite{KCM2024} and to the
code of Bridge++~\cite{Bridge} (\texttt{Fopr\_Domainwall}, \texttt{Fopr\_Domainwall\_eo},
with Neff's $\alpha$ as \texttt{parameter\_alpha}). Eq.~\eqref{eq:Dslice} is the
tridiagonal matrix of Ref.~\cite{BNO}, Eq.~(2.20), and of Ref.~\cite{KCM2024}, Eq.~(1), with
$D_+=1+bW$ on the diagonal, $D_-\Pp$ below and $D_-\Pm$ above it, $-mD_-P_\pm$ in the corners,
and $s$ shifted by one. The operators $D$, $C$ and $D^{(\alpha)}$ are thus the same in all four;
the differences are names and signs:
\begin{itemize}
\item $D_\pm$ are those of Refs.~\cite{BNO,KCM2024}, $D_-=cW-1$. The hop $Y$ of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Y} carries a minus sign, so $D=D_+-D_-Y$ and $d_-=1-c(4-M_5)$ is minus the
site part of $D_-$. The comments of RutticeQCD on the five-dimensional source call $1-cW$
``$D_-$'', which is $-D_-$ (the source is that of the Bridge++ domain-wall test, sign
included).
\item The height $M_5>0$ here is $M_0$ in Ref.~\cite{KCM2024} and in Bridge++
(\texttt{domain\_wall\_height}); Ref.~\cite{BNO} writes $M_5$ for the Wilson mass,
$M_5^{\mathrm{BNO}}=-M_5$, Eq.~(2.13). The caption of Fig.~1 of Ref.~\cite{KCM2024}
gives $M_0=-1$ for the height $1$ of its text.
\item The $\alpha$ of Ref.~\cite{BNO}, $(b+c)/(b-c)$, is the scale factor of the
M\"obius kernel ($2$ at $b=1.5$, $c=0.5$), not Neff's $\alpha$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Q}.
\item $\Gamma_5=\gamma_5RD_-^{-1}$ in Ref.~\cite{BNO}, Eq.~(2.22); here $\Gfive=\gamma_5R$.
\end{itemize}
$D^{(\alpha)}$ here and the operator of Bridge++ with \texttt{parameter\_alpha} were
compared on a $12^4$ configuration with stout links, $L_s=8$, $m=0.01$: at $\alpha=0.6$
and $\alpha=1$ CG on the even-odd normal equation took the same number of iterations
for every column of a point source, and the pseudoscalar correlators agree to
$10^{-11}$. Bridge++ returns $\psi^{(\alpha)}$; $\psi=Q\psi^{(\alpha)}$.
