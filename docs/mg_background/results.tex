\subsection{Performance}
\label{sec:performance}

\subsubsection{Comparison with CGNE}
\label{sec:massscan}

We first benchmark the multigrid solver against mixed-precision even-odd CGNE as a function of the
quark mass and lattice volume. On the $24^3\times48$ ensembles at $m=0.001$, the multigrid solve
outperforms CGNE by a factor of $6.4$--$10.9$, and the setup overhead is amortized within a single
12-column point-source propagator for all $m\le0.01$.

\paragraph{The reference solver.} As our baseline we use mixed-precision CG on the normal equations
$\mathcal K\dg\mathcal K$ with $\mathcal K=\Ce^{-1}S$ (the even-odd preconditioned operator of
Bridge++), using the same value of Neff's $\alpha$ as the multigrid solver.
On the same
GPU ($24^3\times48$, configuration~1, setup~S, $\alpha=0.6$, at $m=0.1, 0.01, 0.001$), the
double-precision OpenACC even-odd CG of Bridge++~2.1.3 (stopping at $|r|^2/|D\dg\eta|^2<10^{-18}$

($184$--$189$ vs.\ $189$, $1336$--$1387$ vs.\ $1400$, and $3612$--$3618$ vs.\ $3616$), while taking
$1.56$ times as long per iteration at $m\le0.01$ and $1.84$ times at $m=0.1$.

(Fig.~\ref{fig:massscan}, Table~\ref{tab:massscan}). From $m=0.1$ to $m=0.001$ the iterations of
CGNE grow by $20$--$32$, those of the multigrid at $\alpha=0.6$ by $2.0$--$2.4$ \New (ranges over

$2.5$--$4.8$ right-hand sides at $m=0.001$. At $\alpha=0.6$ the multigrid solve, setup excluded, is
faster than CGNE for all $m\le0.05$ (and at $m=0.1$ in setup~B), while at $m=0.1$ in setup~S it is
slightly slower ($3.4$ vs.\ $3.2$\,s).

\subsubsection{Solves with $D\dg$}
\label{sec:daggerresults}
\paragraph{Single solves.} \New For the constant M\"obius coefficients used here,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:b5} reduces a solve with $D\dg$ to a solve with $D$ (Table~\ref{tab:b5}). The
preconditioner $\Gfive\mathcal M\Gfive$ of Sec.~\ref{sec:c4idea} also applies when $b_s$ and $c_s$
depend on $s$. With GCR(2) it needs $2$ to $7$ more iterations than $D$, and it stalls on the residual
$b-D\dg Dx_0$ of one normal-equation sweep on $12^4$ in setup~S (Table~\ref{tab:b5}); such residuals
occur as right-hand sides in HMC.

\New A larger Krylov space removes the stall. We repeat the $12^4$ setup-S test at $m=0.001$ with a
separate setup of $D$ and flexible GCR on $D\dg$ over the whole lattice. Here the residual
$b-D\dg Dx_0$ is formed from an initial solve $D\dg w_0=b$ using GCR(8) with a second cycle built
like $\mathcal M$ with $D$ and $\Dpv$ exchanged in Eq.~\eqref{eq:corrplain}. On that residual, GCR(2)
with $\Gfive\mathcal M\Gfive$ again stalls on all four configurations, while GCR(8) converges on all
four in $14$, $19$, $15$ and $12$ iterations.



\subsection{What each part contributes}
\label{sec:parts}

Beyond the preconditioned operator $A'$ itself, the performance reported in Sec.~\ref{sec:massscan}
depends on four choices: the coarse-grid correction, the lift $\hat t$ in the prolongation basis,
Tikhonov regularization of the Pauli--Villars coarse solve, and the setup parameters.

\subsubsection{Krylov on $\AL$ without the multigrid}
\label{sec:nomg}

To isolate the impact of the coarse-grid correction, we compare against Krylov solvers applied
directly to $\AL$ without multigrid preconditioning (Fig.~\ref{fig:massscan}). On $24^3\times48$,
unpreconditioned BiCGStab on $\AL$ reduces the iteration count relative to double-precision CGNE to
\New $0.40$--$0.50$ in setup~B and $0.53$--$0.99$ in setup~S; however, because
each BiCGStab iteration applies $\AL$ twice, it remains slower than mixed-precision CGNE at every
quark mass. Replacing BiCGStab with Chebyshev-preconditioned GCR(2) lowers the iteration count
further, yet increases the total solve time. Thus, the speed-up comes from the coarse-grid
correction.


\subsubsection{Choice of the prolongation basis}
\label{sec:basischoice}
Although the exactness relation~\eqref{eq:exact} requires both lifts $s$ and $\hat t$ of each $y$
to lie in $\operatorname{range}\Pro$, the left-preconditioned form used in practice does not
satisfy Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact} strictly (Sec.~\ref{sec:exactness}). To quantify the practical effect
of $\hat t$, Table~\ref{tab:basischoice} compares $\{s,\hat t\}$ against replacing $\hat t$ with
$Ds$ (which has zero support on the odd sites) and against dropping $\hat t$ altogether. Retaining
$\{s\}$ alone \New halves the number of test vectors $n_v$ and therefore halves
the number of columns in $\Pro$; for a fixed-dimension comparison, we also evaluate $\{s\}$
constructed from $32$ vectors $y$, which doubles the number of vectors processed by both the
Chebyshev filter and the bootstrap.

For a fixed set of vectors $y$, retaining both lifts consistently yields the lowest iteration count

\subsubsection{The regularized Pauli--Villars solve}
\label{sec:pvcoarse}

In setup~S, stout link smearing creates very small singular values in the Galerkin Pauli--Villars
coarse operator, reducing its smallest singular value by a factor of \New $9$ to
$106$ relative to the fine operator $\Dpv$, whereas without smearing both remain of the same order
(Table~\ref{tab:pvsv}). Left unregularized, these small singular values cause the outer solve to
slow down sharply or stagnate (\New Table~\ref{tab:tikhonov}). Introducing the
Tikhonov shift ($\tau=10^{-3}$) removes these failures. Across $40$ setups of setup~S on $12^4$
(ten random seeds per configuration), $39$ converge in $11$ to $21$ iterations and the remaining
one in $46$. \New Over the range $10^{-4}\le\tau\le3\times10^{-2}$, all tested
runs except two converge in $11$ to $23$ iterations (Table~\ref{tab:tikhonov}).

\paragraph{\New Automatic number of rounds.}
\label{sec:autorounds}
The rule of Sec.~\ref{sec:autoboot} does more rounds at small masses and fewer at large ones.
On the mass scan on $24^3\times48$ it does two rounds at $m\ge0.02$ and three at
$m\le0.002$. At $m=0.001$ the GCR(2) iterations are $18$--$22$ (S) and $16$--$18$ (B)\New{}. The
$L_s=8$ tables of Secs.~\ref{sec:performance}--\ref{sec:parts} use the automatic number of rounds.

The number of rounds is set by how far the lowest Ritz value has to fall. The filter leaves
$\theta_0\approx1.5\times10^{-2}$ (S) and $4\times10^{-2}$ (B) at every mass. While the vectors are
far from the lowest modes, a round lowers $\theta$ by a factor of $10$ to $150$. The bootstrap stops
near a value that falls with the mass, in setup~S from $\theta\approx2\times10^{-4}$ at $m=0.1$ to
$1$--$4\times10^{-7}$ at $m=0.001$. That is below three orders of magnitude under $\theta_0$ at
$m\ge0.02$, which two rounds cover, and five orders at $m\le0.002$, which take three.

\New The gain of a round need not fall from round to round, because the cycle improves with the
vectors. On $12^4$ S with blocks $6^4$ the second round gains $3.5$ and the third $280$. This is why the first two rounds run without a probe.

\subsection{Why the method converges}
\label{sec:why}

\New The field of values $W(X)$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:coarse}) contains the spectrum, and its leftmost point is the smallest eigenvalue
of the Hermitian part $(X+X\dg)/2$; GCR converges at every restart length when $W$ lies in the
right half-plane~\cite{ESS}.

For the spectral measurements in this section \New and in Appendix~\ref{sec:spectral}, we fix $\mathcal M$ as a linear operator by solving
both coarse systems to $|r|^2/|b|^2<10^{-20}$ in double precision (retaining the Tikhonov
regularization~\eqref{eq:tikhonov} on the Pauli--Villars factor) and constructing $\mathcal M\dg$
as its exact adjoint, verified so that $\langle z|\AR\mathcal M w\rangle$ and
$\langle\mathcal M\dg\AR\dg z|w\rangle$ agree to roughly $10^{-10}$. We denote the smoother
polynomial of Sec.~\ref{sec:smoother} by $\mathcal S=p(\AR)$, the exact coarse-grid
correction~\eqref{eq:corrplain} by $E$ ($Er=e$), and the full cycle of Algorithm~\ref{alg:cycle} by
$\mathcal M$. All spectral quantities are evaluated at $\alpha=1$, since $\AR$ is independent of
$\alpha$; in Table~\ref{tab:massscan}, setting $\alpha=1$ increases the mean iteration count by
only $0$--$3$ relative to $\alpha=0.6$, suggesting comparable spectral behavior.

On $12^4$ at $m=0.001$ (four configurations), the smoother alone is benign
($\|I-\AR\mathcal S\|\approx1$), whereas the coarse-grid correction $E$ strongly amplifies
directions orthogonal to the test subspace, with $\|I-\AR E\|$ reaching $2.2$--$3.1\times10^3$ (S)
and $1.7$--$2.0\times10^3$ (B). As in Ref.~\cite{EickMawhinney}, post-smoothing suppresses most of
this amplification, reducing $\|I-\AR\mathcal M\|$ to $6.8$--$10.4$ (S) and $3.7$--$4.2$ (B) with
a Chebyshev polynomial of degree $7$ or $11$.

\New Nevertheless, $W(\AR\mathcal M)$ still crosses into
the left half-plane on every configuration in both setups ($\min\Re W(\AR\mathcal M)\in[-2.85,-1.73]$
in setup~S and $[-0.86,-0.63]$ in setup~B; only at $M_5=1$ without smearing does it remain in the
right half-plane, $[0.23,0.33]$), so the ESS bound~\cite{ESS} does not apply. \New The spectrum and
the pseudospectra of $\AR\mathcal M$ bound only full GMRES with this fixed cycle; we define them and give them in
Appendix~\ref{sec:spectral}. Let $x$ be the unit vector at which $\Re\,x\dg\AR\mathcal Mx$ takes this minimum. For
truncated GCR(2) with inexact coarse solves, the method we use, the next paragraph evaluates the same
quantity on $x$ with the inexact cycle.

\paragraph{Truncated GCR and inexact coarse solves.}\label{sec:gcrk} We examine the sensitivity of outer GCR($k$) convergence to the truncation length $k$ and the
inner coarse-solve tolerance on $12^4$ ($m=0.001$, medians over the four configurations). With the
default loose coarse tolerance of $10^{-2}$, GCR(2) requires at most one more iteration in the
median than GCR(32), with $12$ vs.\ $11$ for both $\AL$ and $\AR$ in setup~S, and $13$ vs.\ $12$
($\AL$) or $15$ vs.\ $14$ ($\AR$) in setup~B. Tightening the coarse solves to $10^{-8}$ (so that
$\mathcal M$ approaches the fixed linear map) actually degrades convergence in setup~S on $\AL$,
increasing the median count to $20$ iterations for GCR(2), $16$ for GCR(8), and $15$ for
GCR($\ge16$).

This behavior reflects the action of inexact coarse solves on the directions amplified by $E$.
Evaluated at the vector $x$ at the leftmost point of $W(\AR\mathcal M)$, stopping the coarse solves
at $10^{-2}$ reduces $\|(I-\AR E)x\|$ by large factors compared with exact coarse solves, and in
every tested case restores $\Re\,x\dg\AR\mathcal Mx>0$ ($0.66$--$1.02$ across the eight
configurations of setups~S and~B). Because the Krylov coarse solves stop early, the
effective preconditioner $\mathcal M$ is no longer a fixed linear operator and varies from step to
step.

With a fixed cycle, truncated GCR(2) can reach a fixed point where the new search direction is
orthogonal to the current residual, causing subsequent iterations to stall. Under the parameters of
Table~\ref{tab:setups}, however, \New every solve with $D$ converged across all
volumes for both random and point-source right-hand sides (Tables~\ref{tab:massscan},
\ref{tab:pointsource}, and~\ref{tab:volume}).

\subsection{\New Shared-basis and split-basis coarsening across $L_s$ and volume}
\label{sec:ls16comp}

At $L_s=8$, both nine-point variants of Sec.~\ref{sec:correction} converge across all tested
volumes from $12^4$ to $24^3\times48$. At $L_s=16$ on $24^4$, the two variants fail on opposite
setups, so the shared basis covers setup~S while the split basis covers setup~B
(Table~\ref{tab:ls16comp}).

\paragraph{Spurious coarse directions and the ratio $r_i$.}
Let $\Dc=\Pro\dg A\Pro=\sum_i\sigma_i u_i v_i\dg$ be the singular value decomposition of the quark
coarse operator, with $\sigma_1\le\sigma_2\le\dots$, where $A=D$ for the shared basis and
$A=\Dsym$ (with $\Pro=\Pq$) for the split basis. For each unit right singular vector $v_i$, we
define the fine residual norm $\eta_i=\|A\Pro v_i\|$ and the dimensionless ratio
\begin{equation}
r_i=\frac{\|A\Pro v_i\|}{\sigma_i}=\frac{\eta_i}{\sigma_i}\ge1 .
\label{eq:ratio_r}
\end{equation}
Decomposing $A\Pro v_i=\Pro(\Pro\dg A\Pro v_i)+(1-\Pro\Pro\dg)A\Pro v_i=\sigma_i\Pro u_i+w_i^\perp$
with $\Pro\dg w_i^\perp=0$ gives $\|w_i^\perp\|=\sigma_i\sqrt{r_i^2-1}$. An exact coarse step
$e=\Pro\Dc^{-1}\Pro\dg h$ therefore cancels the coarse residual component $u_i\dg\Pro\dg h$ while
leaving a fine residual of norm $|u_i\dg\Pro\dg h|\,\sqrt{r_i^2-1}$ outside $\operatorname{range}\Pro$.
For the split basis, $\Dsym$ is $\Gfive$-Hermitian (Eq.~\eqref{eq:Dsymherm}) and $\Gfive\Pq=\Pq\sigma_3$,
so $\Dc=\sigma_3 H_c$ with the Hermitian indefinite matrix $H_c=\Pq\dg H\Pq$ and $H=\Gfive\Dsym$~\cite{BY}.
Each singular value satisfies $\sigma_i=|\theta_i|$, where $\theta_i$ is a Ritz value of $H$ on
$\operatorname{range}\Pq$. Expanding $\Pq v_i=\sum_a c_a|\lambda_a\rangle$ in fine eigenstates of
$H$ gives the convex combination~\cite{BrowerStag}
\begin{equation}
\theta_i=\sum_a |c_a|^2\lambda_a , \qquad
\|(H-\theta_i)\Pq v_i\|^2=\sum_a |c_a|^2(\lambda_a-\theta_i)^2=\theta_i^2(r_i^2-1) .
\label{eq:pollution}
\end{equation}
A large $r_i$ at small $|\theta_i|$ means that $\Pq v_i$ combines positive and negative fine modes
far from $\theta_i$, producing spectral pollution inside the gap of $H$~\cite{LevitinShargorodsky,BCHW}.

\paragraph{Wrong-wall ghosts.}
We first define the quantities used to tell true low modes from spurious coarse directions in the
split basis. Let $V_k$ be the span of the $k=16$ right singular vectors of $\Dc$ with the smallest
singular values, and let $w_1,\dots,w_k$ be the Ritz vectors of $H_c=\Pq\dg H\Pq$ on $V_k$, with
Ritz values $\theta_j$. For a unit coarse vector $w$ we write $\eta(w)=\|\Dsym\Pq w\|=\|H\Pq w\|$
($\Gfive$ is unitary), so $\eta(w)\ge|\theta|$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:pollution}. Let $w'$ be the
normalized projection of $\sigma_3w$ onto $V_k$, and define the pair ratio
\begin{equation}
\rho(w)=\frac{\eta(w')}{\eta(w)} .
\label{eq:pairratio}
\end{equation}

The ratio separates the two kinds of direction as follows. Let $\psi_0$ be an eigenvector of $H$,
$H\psi_0=\lambda\psi_0$ with small $|\lambda|$, localized on one wall, and let $\psi_0\approx\Pq c$.
Then $\eta(c)\approx\|H\psi_0\|=|\lambda|$ is small. Since $\Gfive\Pq=\Pq\sigma_3$, the reflection
$\phi=\Gfive\psi_0\approx\Pq\sigma_3c$ also lies in $\operatorname{range}\Pq$; $\Gfive=\gamma_5R$
keeps the four-dimensional chirality and moves $\phi$ to the opposite wall. Its residual is
$\eta(\sigma_3c)=\|H\Gfive\psi_0\|=\|\Dsym\Gfive\psi_0\|$, and its Ritz value is
$\phi\dg H\phi=\psi_0\dg\Dsym\phi$, a matrix element between states on the two walls. A direction
close to $\sigma_3c$ thus has a residual of order one (measured below), while its Ritz value can be
small, and its partner under $\sigma_3$ is $c$, so $\rho\ll1$ for it and $\rho\gg1$ for $c$. We call a Ritz vector with $\rho(w)>10$ a \emph{true mode} and one with
$\rho(w)<0.2$ a \emph{wrong-wall ghost}.

On $16^4$ configuration~4 in setup~S, three Ritz vectors are true modes at $L_s=8$, $10$, $12$,
$14$ and $20$, and two at $L_s=16$, with $\rho=24$--$214$ and $\eta=0.005$--$0.045$. The ghosts have
$\eta=1.06$--$1.12$ and $|\theta|=4.4\times10^{-4}$--$1.7\times10^{-2}$. On the full $H_c$ at
$L_s=8$, $\sigma_3$ maps the two lowest true modes onto the two ghosts with overlaps $0.98$ and
$0.91$. The true modes have $\#(\theta>0)-\#(\theta<0)=+3$, equal to $Q$ of this configuration
(Table~\ref{tab:configs}), at every $L_s$ except $16$, where it is $+2$. At $L_s=12$ a ghost lies at
$\theta=-4.4\times10^{-4}$, the direction of $r_{\max}=2402$ in Table~\ref{tab:ls16comp}, and
GCR(2) stalls. In setup~B on the same configuration, four Ritz vectors are true modes at every
$L_s$ ($\rho=13$--$63$, $\eta=0.024$--$0.12$), so their count $+4$ differs from $Q$. The ghosts
have $\eta=1.50$--$1.53$ and stay at $|\theta|\ge8\times10^{-3}$ for $L_s=8$--$20$. On
$24^4$ at $L_s=16$ with three bootstrap rounds, at most one Ritz vector per configuration has
$\rho>10$ (four configurations in setup~S, two in setup~B), so there the ratio does not identify
the true modes.

A single-switch ablation on $16^4$ configuration~4 tests the effect of including $\hat t$ in the
quark basis. Retaining $\Dsym$ and $\Pv$ of the split basis while adding the $\Gfive$-split
components $\frac12(1\pm\Gfive)B_5\hat t$ to $\Pq$ restores convergence in setup~S ($16$
iterations at $L_s=12$, $22$ at $L_s=16$), consistent with the opposite-wall weight expected for
$\hat t$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:exactness}), while causing setup~B at $L_s=16$ to stall ($300$ iterations,
compared with $20$ without $B_5\hat t$). In setup~B on $24^4$ at $L_s=16$ with matched $4^3\times2$
blocks (configuration~1, three bootstrap rounds), the shared basis stalls with
$r_{\mathrm{med}}=264$ and $r_{\max}=704$, whereas the split basis lowers $r_{\mathrm{med}}$ by
about half ($123$, $r_{\max}=316$) and converges in $28$ iterations.

\paragraph{Volume dependence of $r_i$ and $\eta_i$.}
At $L_s=8$, outer iterations grow slowly from $12^4$ to $24^3\times48$ ($32\times$ in
four-dimensional volume) for both adopted variants, and all $64$ measured low modes of each adopted
variant across the four $24^3\times48$ configurations stay below $r=100$
($r_{\mathrm{med}}=25.3$--$35.5$ for shared~S and $30.2$--$39.6$ for split~B, with
$\eta_{\mathrm{med}}=0.211$--$0.224$ across all four split~B configurations). At $L_s=16$ with
$4^4$ blocks ($12^4$, $16^4$, and $24^4$), the singular-vector residual $\eta_i=\|A\Pro v_i\|$
collapses onto a volume-independent plateau of $1.1$--$1.8$ for $\sigma_i\gtrsim10^{-2}$. To
separate true low modes from mixed singular directions in the split basis, we diagonalize the
$16\times16$ Gram matrix $V_k\dg\Pq\dg\Dsym\dg\Dsym\Pq V_k$ on the lowest $k=16$ singular subspace
$V_k$, giving unit eigenvectors $y_j$ with fine residual norms $\tilde\eta_j=\|\Dsym\Pq y_j\|$.
With three fixed bootstrap rounds at $L_s=16$, the smallest residual $\tilde\eta_1$ increases from
$16^4$ (configuration~4) to $24^4$ in both setups, from $0.0021$ to $0.010$--$0.048$ across the
four configurations in setup~S and from $0.017$ to $0.11$--$0.21$ (configurations~1 and~4) in
setup~B. With three bootstrap rounds on $24^4$ at $L_s=16$, split~B has no Ritz vector with
$\rho<0.2$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:pairratio}),
while its $r_i$ values reach $r_{\mathrm{med}}=117$--$132$ and $r_{\max}=299$--$416$. Raising the bootstrap from three to five rounds on
configurations~1 and~4 cuts the smallest three $\tilde\eta_j$ from $0.11$--$0.30$ to
$0.006$--$0.039$, showing that three rounds had not finished refining the lowest modes. Yet five
rounds cause GCR(2) to stall at $300$ iterations on both configurations, and six rounds stall
split~B on configuration~4. The automatic rule of Sec.~\ref{sec:autoboot} stops shared~S on
configuration~2 after two rounds and split~B on configuration~3 after four, and both stall. Of the
numbers of rounds tested on $24^4$ at $L_s=16$, three is the only one with which both adopted
variants converge on all four configurations.
