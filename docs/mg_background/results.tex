\paragraph{Numerical setup.} The quenched configurations use the plaquette action at $\beta=6.0$,
a lattice spacing of about $0.1$~fm; Table~\ref{tab:configs} lists the configurations. Results are means, medians or
ranges over the four configurations of each volume as the captions state; the solvers without the
multigrid and the pseudospectra use configuration~1. The two setups of the M\"obius operator
(Table~\ref{tab:setups}) share $(b,c)=(1.5,0.5)$, the choice of the JLQCD and the RBC/UKQCD
Collaborations, and $L_s=8$.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Setup B (bare links)} uses $M_5=1.8$, the value typical of simulations on unsmeared
links~\cite{Arthur2013}.
\item \emph{Setup S (stout links)} smears the links of the fermion operator (stout, $\rho=0.1$, three
steps, Sec.~\ref{sec:smearing}) and uses $M_5=1.0$, as in the JLQCD ensembles of
Ref.~\cite{JLQCDBpi}.
\end{itemize}
Both suppress the near-zero modes of $W=D_W(-M_5)$, by moving $M_5$ (B) or by smoothing the
field (S), and so give the multigrid low modes of different character; they need different
smoother intervals (Table~\ref{tab:setups}).\footnote{\textcolor{red}{New!!!} The multigrid also
works for Bori\c{c}i's operator, $(b,c)=(1,1)$, with a smoother interval chosen for it: on $24^3\times48$, in
setup~S a narrower one ($\kappa_s=3$) is needed and then gives the iteration count of the M\"obius
operator, and in setup~B the solve takes about four times the M\"obius iterations. Its residual
mass is not the reason; at $L_s=8$ it is of the same size as the M\"obius one in setup~S and
smaller in setup~B.} Timings are from one NVIDIA data-center GPU with
$24$\,GB of device memory; a second GPU was used for iteration counts and spectral quantities only.
Every setup uses the same random seed except where other seeds are stated
(Secs.~\ref{sec:pvcoarse} and~\ref{sec:sensitivity}).


\subsection{Performance}
\label{sec:performance}

\subsubsection{Comparison with CGNE}
\label{sec:massscan}

On $24^3\times48$ the multigrid is $6.6$--$11.1$ times faster than mixed-precision CGNE at
$m=0.001$, and its setup is recovered within one point-source propagator for $m\le0.01$.

\paragraph{The reference solver.} It is CG on $\mathcal K\dg\mathcal K$, $\mathcal K=\Ce^{-1}S$,
the even-odd operator of Bridge++, with Neff's $\alpha$ as for the multigrid, in mixed precision.
In double precision it runs at the memory bandwidth of the GPU: in the byte count of QUDA, which
counts every value a site reads, including those found in the cache, it moves $1.09$ times the
peak memory bandwidth. The even-odd CG of Bridge++~2.1.3 (OpenACC) on the same GPU
needs $3612$--$3618$ iterations for the 12 columns of a point source on configuration~1 at
$m=0.001$ in setup~S, against $3616$ here for a random right-hand side, and $1.5$ times as long
per iteration.
Boyle and Yamaguchi~\cite{BY} used CG on $S\dg S$, which here needs more iterations
(Appendix~\ref{sec:cgform}).

\paragraph{Mass dependence.} The multigrid removes most of the critical slowing down of CGNE
(Fig.~\ref{fig:massscan}, Table~\ref{tab:massscan}). From $m=0.1$ to $m=0.001$ the iterations of
CGNE grow by $20$--$32$, those of the multigrid by $2.0$--$2.4$. Its setup is paid back after
$2.5$--$4.6$ right-hand sides at $m=0.001$. The multigrid is faster at every mass. The multigrid needs three times the device memory of CGNE.

\paragraph{A point-source propagator.} At $m=0.001$ the multigrid takes $0.33$--$0.49$ of the wall
time of CGNE for a whole propagator, setup included (Table~\ref{tab:pointsource}). At $m=0.01$ it
takes $0.86$ (S) and $0.90$ (B) of that time for the 12 columns, setup included. The pseudoscalar correlators of the two solvers
agree to $1\times10^{-6}$ or better.

\subsubsection{Solves with $D\dg$}
\label{sec:daggerresults}
\paragraph{Single solves.} \textcolor{red}{New!!!} For the constant coefficients used here the
exact relation of Sec.~\ref{sec:b5} makes a solve with $D\dg$ a solve with $D$, with the iterations
of Table~\ref{tab:b5}. The tests below concern the preconditioner of Sec.~\ref{sec:c4idea}, the
route for coefficients that depend on $s$. One setup of $D$ also solves with $D\dg$: flexible GCR on $D\dg$ with
$\Gfive\mathcal M\Gfive$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:c4}, one cycle per step, needs two to four

random vector $b$ and the residual $r=b-D\dg Dx_0$ of one sweep of the normal equations, where
$D\dg w_0=b$ and $Dx_0=w_0$ are solved to $|r|^2/|b|^2<10^{-10}$ (the first by GCR(8) with the cycle
that has $D$ and $\Dpv$ exchanged, Sec.~\ref{sec:open}); HMC meets right-hand sides of the second kind. On $16^4$ on
the random right-hand side more inner cycles per step do not reduce the count, which suggests that
there the extra steps come from $N$; on the residual right-hand side two cycles per step save a few iterations. On $12^4$ in setup~S at $m=0.001$ the residual right-hand side is harder: it needs more kept
directions and more cycles per step, and GCR(8) with two cycles per step converges on all four
configurations (Table~\ref{tab:c4}).

The exact map of Eq.~\eqref{eq:g5exact} would give the null vectors of $D\dg$ and reduce $D\dg w=r$ to
a solve with $D$, but it needs $D_-^{-1}$, which does not converge (Sec.~\ref{sec:g5fail}).


\subsection{What each part contributes}
\label{sec:parts}

The speed-up of Sec.~\ref{sec:massscan} rests on four choices beyond the operator $A'$: the coarse
correction, the lift $\hat t$ in the basis, the regularized Pauli--Villars solve,
and the setup parameters.

\subsubsection{Krylov on $\AL$ without the multigrid}
\label{sec:nomg}

The speed-up comes from the coarse correction. Without it a Krylov method on $\AL$ does not beat
mixed-precision CGNE (Fig.~\ref{fig:massscan}). On $24^3\times48$ BiCGStab on $\AL$ alone needs fewer
iterations than double-precision CGNE, about $0.4$ of them, but each of its iterations applies $\AL$ twice,
and it is slower than mixed-precision CGNE at every mass. GCR(2) with the Chebyshev smoother as its
preconditioner needs fewer iterations than BiCGStab but more time.


\subsubsection{Choice of the prolongation basis}
\label{sec:basischoice}
The exactness~\eqref{eq:exact} needs both lifts $s$ and $\hat t$ of each $y$ in
$\operatorname{range}\Pro$, but the left form used in practice is not exact
(Sec.~\ref{sec:basis}). To see what $\hat t$ does there, we replace it by $Ds$, which
vanishes on the odd sites, or drop it (Table~\ref{tab:basischoice}). With $\{s\}$ alone $\Pro$
has half the columns; to compare at the same size we also take $32$ vectors $y$, which doubles
the vectors of the filter and of the bootstrap.

With the same vectors $y$, the two lifts give the fewest iterations on every volume. Without
$\hat t$, setup~S stagnates or slows down several times on some configurations, and setup~B needs
more iterations. Twice as many vectors $y$ with $\{s\}$ alone, the same number of columns, need fewer iterations on
$24^3\times48$ and within three of the two lifts on $12^4$ and $16^4$, but the filter and the
bootstrap then handle twice the vectors and the setup takes $1.2$--$1.7$ times as long.

\subsubsection{The regularized Pauli--Villars solve}
\label{sec:pvcoarse}

The regularized solve removes the slow solves of setup~S (Table~\ref{tab:noshift}). They come with
stout smearing, which makes the smallest singular value of the Galerkin Pauli--Villars factor one to
two orders of magnitude smaller than that of the fine $\Dpv$; without smearing the two are of the
same order (Table~\ref{tab:pvsv}). Without the shift the solve then needs several times the
iterations or stagnates. With the shift it converges in $11$ to $21$ iterations on every
configuration and setup seed but one, which needs $46$ with GCR(2) and $15$ with GCR(8), and in all
runs but two the count hardly depends on $\tau$ between $10^{-4}$ and $3\times10^{-2}$
(Table~\ref{tab:tikhonov}).

\subsubsection{Sensitivity to the parameters}
\label{sec:sensitivity}


The method is robust within a window of each parameter and fails outside it
(Fig.~\ref{fig:sensitivity}). The smoother interval must suit the
setup, the bootstrap is necessary, and more accurate coarse solves do not help.

Fewer iterations are not always less time (Table~\ref{tab:costs}). Smaller aggregates and more test

The number of test vectors that suffices grows from $12^4$ to $24^4$ (Table~\ref{tab:costs} and
Table~\ref{tab:tvscan}); in setup~B it does not grow further to $24^3\times48$, as local coherence of
the low modes leads one to expect once the box is large enough. In setup~B each
count gives about the same iterations on both volumes. Setup~S needs more vectors; with fewer than
$32$ it is slow or stagnates on both volumes.

\paragraph{\textcolor{red}{New!!!} Automatic number of rounds.}
\label{sec:autorounds}
The rule of Sec.~\ref{sec:autoboot} does more rounds at small masses and fewer at large ones:
on the mass scan on $24^3\times48$ it does two rounds at $m\ge0.02$ and three at
$m\le0.002$. At $m=0.001$ the GCR(2) iterations are $18$--$22$ (S) and $16$--$18$ (B), against up
to $44$ with two rounds. The tables use the automatic number of rounds.

\subsection{Why the method converges}
\label{sec:why}

We infer that the spectrum and the pseudospectra of the preconditioned operator govern the
convergence, since its field of values reaches into the left half-plane and the bound of
Eisenstat, Elman and Schultz~\cite{ESS} for GCR does not apply. The quantities of this section are
for $\alpha=1$; $\AR$ does not depend on $\alpha$. At $\alpha=1$ the solves of
Table~\ref{tab:massscan} need $1$--$4$ more iterations than at $\alpha=0.6$ in the mean over the
configurations. As usual for multigrid preconditioners of lattice Dirac operators, the
convergence of the truncated flexible outer method is established numerically.

The \emph{field of values}
$W(X)=\{z\dg Xz/z\dg z\}$ contains the spectrum, and its leftmost point is the smallest eigenvalue
of the Hermitian part $(X+X\dg)/2$; GCR converges at every restart length when $W$ lies in the
right half-plane~\cite{ESS}. The \emph{$\varepsilon$-pseudospectrum} is the set of $z$ where
$\sigma_{\min}(z-X)<\varepsilon$; for a normal $X$ it is the $\varepsilon$-neighborhood of the
spectrum, for a non-normal one it can be much larger, and it bounds full
GMRES~\cite{TrefethenEmbree}. The condition number of an eigenvalue, $\kappa=1/|y\dg x|$ for its
normalized right and left eigenvectors, is $1$ for a normal matrix and widens the pseudospectrum
around the eigenvalue.

For these quantities $\mathcal M$ is a fixed linear map: both coarse factors solved
to $|r|^2/|b|^2<10^{-20}$ in double precision, the Pauli--Villars factor regularized as in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:tikhonov}, and $\mathcal M\dg$ built as its adjoint, so that
$\langle z|\AR\mathcal M w\rangle$ and $\langle\mathcal M\dg\AR\dg z|w\rangle$ agree to about $10^{-10}$.

\subsubsection{Field of values}
\label{sec:fov}

The coarse correction pushes the field of values into the left half-plane (Table~\ref{tab:fov}).
The field of values of $\AR$ itself reaches only just below zero, a little further on larger volumes,
and the smoother is harmless, $\|I-\AR\mathcal S\|\approx1$. Without the shift the coarse correction $E$ is the identity
on the vectors it is built from (Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact}). With stout smearing it amplifies other
directions, like a strongly oblique projection, by three orders of magnitude, without smearing by
less than a hundred (both with the shift). The post-smoothing acts as a filter
and brings the whole cycle back to within a factor of ten of the identity. Eick and Mawhinney~\cite{EickMawhinney}
describe the same mechanism for a renormalization-group coarse lattice and need a Chebyshev filter
of high degree; here $\AR$ is close to the identity on the high modes, and the smoother suffices.

$W(\AR\mathcal M)$ crosses the origin on every configuration in both setups: with stout smearing at
$M_5=1$ (setup~S) and without it at $M_5=1.8$ (setup~B). Of the three cases we ran, only at $M_5=1$
without smearing is the
amplification far smaller and $W(\AR\mathcal M)$ in the right half-plane, where truncated GCR($k$) is
guaranteed to converge for every $k$ with a fixed cycle~\cite{ESS}.

\subsubsection{Spectrum}
\label{sec:spectrum}

Every eigenvalue lies at $\Re\lambda\ge0.72$, far from the origin, with moderate condition
numbers (Table~\ref{tab:spectrum}, Fig.~\ref{fig:spectrum}). A few eigenvalues lie farther from $1$ than the rest, isolated outliers (Table~\ref{tab:spectrum} gives those of configuration~1),
and all other eigenvalues lie in a disc about $1$ that excludes the origin. The left and right forms have nearly the same spectrum.

\subsubsection{Pseudospectra and a bound for GMRES}
\label{sec:pseudo}

On $12^4$ (configuration~1) the pseudospectra of $\AR\mathcal M$ bound full GMRES with a fixed
cycle: $\|r_k\|/\|r_0\|<10^{-9}$ within $55$ (S) and $57$ (B) iterations (Table~\ref{tab:pseudo}).
For a non-normal operator the convergence of GMRES is bounded by the $\varepsilon$-pseudospectrum, the
set where $\sigma_{\min}(z-X)<\varepsilon$~\cite{NRT,TrefethenEmbree}. If a contour $\Gamma$ of length $L$ encloses the
spectrum of $X$ but not the origin and $\sigma_{\min}(z-X)\ge\varepsilon$ on it, then
for any polynomial $p_k$ with $p_k(0)=1$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\|r_k\|}{\|r_0\|}\le\|p_k(X)\|\le\frac{L}{2\pi\varepsilon}\max_{z\in\Gamma}|p_k(z)| .
\label{eq:pseudobound}
\end{equation}
An isolated outlier $\lambda_o$ outside $\Gamma$ is removed by the factor
$(1-z/\lambda_o)$ of $p_k$: then $p_k(X)=p_k(X)(I-P_o)$ with $P_o$ the spectral
projector, $\|(z-X)^{-1}(I-P_o)\|\le\kappa_o/\varepsilon$, and with a circle
$|z-c|=r$ and $p_k(z)=(1-z/\lambda_o)(1-z/c)^{k-1}$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\|r_k\|}{\|r_0\|}\le\kappa_o\,\frac{r}{\varepsilon}\,
\frac{|\lambda_o-c|+r}{|\lambda_o|}\Big(\frac rc\Big)^{k-1}.
\label{eq:outlierbound}
\end{equation}

On $16^4$, configuration~1, setup~S has two outliers; the factor $\kappa_o$ is then replaced by
$\|I-P_1-P_2\|\le1+\kappa_1+\kappa_2$ and there are two factors of the form
$(|\lambda_o-c|+r)/|\lambda_o|$. On the dense circles of $12^4$ the spacing of the points is small
enough that, $\sigma_{\min}(z-X)$ being $1$-Lipschitz in $z$, the bound holds on the whole circle, to
the accuracy of the Lanczos estimates.

On the dense circles the bound gives convergence at a fixed rate, $r/c$ per iteration, with no stagnation; on the
contours with $16$ points it is an estimate. On $24^3\times48$, a larger lattice, in setup~S at $m=0.001$ and $\alpha=1$ on
configuration~1, the median factor per GCR step of $|r|$ is $0.42$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:history}),
below the rate $r/c=0.6$ of the bound on the dense circle of setup~S. The Lanczos estimates approach $\sigma_{\min}^2$ from above, the disc rests on the
eigenvalues farthest from $c$ found by Arnoldi, and the bound is for full GMRES with a fixed
$\mathcal M$.

\subsubsection{Truncated GCR and inexact coarse solves}
\label{sec:gcrk}

Truncated GCR does not need accurate coarse solves. With the usual coarse solves to $10^{-2}$,
GCR(2) needs in the median at most one iteration more than GCR(32) on $12^4$
(Table~\ref{tab:gcrk}). Coarse solves
to $10^{-8}$, where $\mathcal M$ is close to the fixed map, do not help: GCR(2) then needs $20$
iterations and GCR($\ge16$) $15$.

In the directions that $E$ amplifies, the solver's cycle, with coarse solves to $10^{-2}$, does
better than the fixed map. At the vector $x$ at the left
end of $W(\AR\mathcal M)$, coarse solves to $10^{-2}$ reduce $\|(I-\AR E)x\|$ by large factors relative to
exact coarse solves and in most cases make $\Re\,x\dg\AR\mathcal Mx$ positive. With Krylov coarse solves stopped
early the cycle is not a fixed linear map.

A truncated method has no guarantee when the field of values contains the origin~\cite{ESS,Embree}.
With a fixed cycle GCR(2) can reach a fixed point, where the new direction is orthogonal to the
residual and the next step repeats it. With the parameters of Table~\ref{tab:setups} no solve with
$D$ on random and point-source right-hand sides reached a plateau.

In setup~S configuration~2 of $24^3\times48$ needs $22$ iterations at $m=0.001$, the other three
$18$ (Table~\ref{tab:volume}).
