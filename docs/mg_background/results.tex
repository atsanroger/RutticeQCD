\paragraph{Numerical setup.} The gauge configurations are quenched, generated with the
plaquette gauge action at $\beta=6.0$, which corresponds to a lattice spacing of about $0.1$~fm. The volumes are $12^4$, $16^4$, $24^4$ and $24^3\times48$, with four configurations each
(Table~\ref{tab:configs}). Tables give means, medians or ranges over the four configurations as stated in their captions;
the field of values, the pseudospectra, the comparison of the two even-odd forms
(Appendix~\ref{sec:cgform}) and the solvers without the multigrid use configuration~1.
On them two setups of the M\"obius operator are used (Table~\ref{tab:setups}), both with
$(b,c)=(1.5,0.5)$, the choice of the JLQCD and the RBC/UKQCD Collaborations, and $L_s=8$.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Setup B (bare links).} The domain-wall height $M_5=1.8$ is the value typical of
practical simulations on unsmeared links, as in the RBC/UKQCD ensembles of
Ref.~\cite{Arthur2013}: on rough
fields the lattice artifact of the Wilson kernel shifts the best value of $M_5$ above~$1$.
\item \emph{Setup S (stout links).} Three steps of the stout smearing of
Sec.~\ref{sec:smearing} with $\rho=0.1$ are applied to the links of the fermion operator, and $M_5=1.0$ is used, as in the JLQCD
ensembles of Ref.~\cite{JLQCDBpi} (its supplemental material, which also gives $b+c=2$,
$b-c=1$); the smearing largely
suppresses the artifact that shifts the best value of $M_5$ away from $1$.
\end{itemize}
The quark mass ranges over $m=0.001$--$0.1$, and the Pauli--Villars mass is $1$. Timings are from one NVIDIA data-center GPU with $24$\,GB of device memory; a second GPU was used for iteration counts and spectral quantities only. The device reductions add
in an order that depends on the build and the GPU, so the iterations of otherwise equal runs can
differ by a few. The setup seed is $21$ in every run; Sec.~\ref{sec:gcrk} shows how the count depends on it.

\paragraph{What the two setups mean for the multigrid.} The residual breaking of chiral
symmetry at finite $L_s$ comes from the eigenvalues near zero of the M\"obius kernel
$H_M=\gamma_5(b+c)W\big(2+(b-c)W\big)^{-1}$~\cite{BNO}, that is from near-zero modes of
$W=D_W(-M_5)$, which on rough gauge fields come from small dislocations. The two setups keep
them small in two ways, smoothing the field (S) or moving $M_5$ (B), and so give the
multigrid low modes of different character. It shows in three measured places: the smoother
needs the interval $[\lambda_{\rm hi}/10,\lambda_{\rm hi}]$ in~S and
$[\lambda_{\rm hi}/30,\lambda_{\rm hi}]$ in~B, and with B's interval setup~S stagnates, with
S's setup~B needs nearly three times the iterations (Sec.~\ref{sec:sensitivity}); the field of
values of $\AR$ reaches less far below zero with smearing ($\Re W\ge-4.6\times10^{-3}$
against $-5.0\times10^{-2}$ on $12^4$, Sec.~\ref{sec:spectra}); and the test vectors of $\AR$
are much closer to null vectors of $\AR\dg$ in S than in B ($\|\AR\dg y\|^2/\|y\|^2\approx0.015$
against $0.27$ on $12^4$ and $16^4$, while $\|\AR y\|^2/\|y\|^2\approx0.003$ in both), which is why the dual
cycle works in~B but stalls in~S (Sec.~\ref{sec:daggerresults}, Table~\ref{tab:c4}).

\subsection{Performance}
\label{sec:performance}

\subsubsection{Comparison with CGNE}
\label{sec:massscan}

We compare the cost of the multigrid with that of the reference solver of
Sec.~\ref{sec:outer} on $24^3\times48$, first for one random right-hand side over seven masses
and then for whole point-source propagators. For one right-hand side and with the setup
excluded, Ref.~\cite{BY} found the Pauli--Villars multigrid an order of magnitude slower than
red-black CG (Sec.~\ref{sec:intro}). Measured the same way, the present method is the faster
solver at $m=0.001$ from $24^4$ on (Table~\ref{tab:volume}).

\paragraph{Which even-odd CG.} The reference solver is CG on $\mathcal K\dg\mathcal K$,
$\mathcal K=\Ce^{-1}S$, the even-odd operator of Bridge++, with Neff's $\alpha$ as for the
multigrid. Boyle and Yamaguchi~\cite{BY} used CG on $S\dg S$. For the M\"obius setups of this work
CG on $S\dg S$ needs $3$--$49\%$ more iterations at $m=0.001$ on $24^3\times48$
(Appendix~\ref{sec:cgform}), and the multigrid is compared with the faster $\mathcal K\dg\mathcal K$.

\paragraph{Mixed-precision CGNE.} For a fair comparison the reference solver also runs in mixed
precision, CG in single precision with reliable updates in double precision; it needs
$0.54$--$0.61$ times the time of double precision at the same final residuals
(Appendix~\ref{sec:cgform}).

\paragraph{Mass dependence.} The multigrid removes most of the critical slowing down of CGNE
(Table~\ref{tab:massscan}, Fig.~\ref{fig:massscan}). On the four configurations, from $m=0.1$ to $m=0.001$
the iterations of the mixed-precision CGNE grow by a factor of $20$--$27$ (S) and $18$--$21$ (B), those
of the multigrid by $1.6$--$4.1$ (S) and $3.1$--$3.7$ (B). At $m=0.001$ the multigrid solve is
$2.9$--$5.0$ (S) and $4.8$--$6.0$ (B) times faster than the mixed-precision CGNE, and its setup of
about $210$\,s is paid back after $3.5$--$5.2$ right-hand sides. The lowest factor, $2.9$, is
configuration~2 in setup~S (Sec.~\ref{sec:gcrk}). The setup is paid back within one point-source propagator (12 columns) for $m\le0.005$. For $m\ge0.05$ (S) the
mixed-precision CGNE is faster or level, and at $m=0.1$ (B) the two take nearly the same time. Against
double-precision CGNE the solve at $m=0.001$ is $3.9$--$9.4$ (S) and $9.0$--$11.4$ (B) times faster
(Table~\ref{tab:massscancfg}). The
multigrid needs three times the device memory of CGNE, which on a $24$\,GB GPU excludes
GCR($k$) with $k\ge8$.

\paragraph{A point-source propagator.} Whole propagators confirm the break-even estimates
(Table~\ref{tab:pointsource}). At $m=0.001$ the multigrid takes $0.40$--$0.56$ of the wall time of the
mixed-precision CGNE, setup included, and a column is $3.0$--$6.2$ times faster. At $m=0.01$ the
setup is not quite paid back within the 12 columns, as $N_{\rm rhs}\approx15$--$19$
(Table~\ref{tab:massscan}) predicts. A point source needs more iterations than a random
right-hand side, with either solver. The pseudoscalar correlators of the two agree to
$4\times10^{-8}$--$7\times10^{-7}$ at $t\le3$ and to $2\times10^{-6}$ at all $48$ times. The
multigrid stops on the residual of $D$ ($|D\psi-\eta|^2/|\eta|^2=1$--$2\times10^{-18}$), the CGNE
on that of its normal equation ($6\times10^{-17}$ to $3\times10^{-16}$). The device memory is
$21.8$\,GB (multigrid) and $9.1$\,GB (mixed-precision CGNE).

\paragraph{Neff's $\alpha$ in the two setups.} At $m=0.001$, $\alpha=0.6$ against $\alpha=1$ saves
CG on $\mathcal K\dg\mathcal K$ $35\%$ (S) and $25\%$ (B) of its iterations (Table~\ref{tab:cgform})
and the multigrid at most two (Table~\ref{tab:massscan}), as Sec.~\ref{sec:eo} expects. Over the
masses the multigrid saves one to two iterations at every mass in setup~B (from 9 to 7 at
$m=0.1$, from 26 to 24 at $m=0.001$, means over the four configurations), and in setup~S four at
$m=0.1$, about one at $m=0.05$ and none from $m=0.02$ down (32 for both at $m=0.001$). The smoother intervals of
Table~\ref{tab:setups} locate the difference. In~B the smoother covers
$[\lambda_{\rm hi}/30,\lambda_{\rm hi}]$, a wide band left to it at every mass, which $\alpha$
shortens. In~S it covers $[\lambda_{\rm hi}/10,\lambda_{\rm hi}]$ and the coarse correction takes
the rest; at light masses the low modes set the iterations, they are in the coarse space, and
$\alpha$ has little left to improve. The spectra of the two setups would test this interpretation.


\subsubsection{Solves with $D\dg$}
\label{sec:daggerresults}
\paragraph{Single solves.} Table~\ref{tab:c4} compares three ways to solve with $D\dg$ from one
setup of $D$. The right-hand sides are a random vector and the residual
$r=b-D\dg Dx_0$ after one sweep of the normal equations, solved to $10^{-10}$. The second kind
is the one that matters in HMC: its error lies mostly on the lowest modes.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The cycle of $\AR\dg$ on the same $\Pro$} (the ``dual cycle''): the factorized
correction~\eqref{eq:corr} with $D$ and $\Dpv$ exchanged.
\item \emph{Flexible GCR on $D\dg$ with $\Gfive\mathcal M\Gfive$}, Eq.~\eqref{eq:c4}, one cycle a
step.
\end{itemize}

The preconditioner $\Gfive\mathcal M\Gfive$ needs two to three iterations more than the solve
with $D$, in both setups and on both kinds of right-hand side, as the heuristic predicts. More
inner cycles per step do not reduce the count ($9$ iterations with one, two or four cycles on
$16^4$): the extra steps come from $N$, since a more exact $\mathcal M$ does not remove them. The dual cycle, in
contrast, stalls on the hard right-hand side in setup~S.

On $12^4$ in setup~S, $m=0.001$, the residual right-hand side stalls GCR(2) on all four
configurations, and GCR(8) needs $13$--$222$ iterations. On $16^4$, where configurations 2--4 have
$Q=+1$, $+1$ and $+3$, GCR(2) needs $9$--$10$ on all four. The stall follows the small smeared
lattice of Sec.~\ref{sec:gcrk}; the topological charge does not predict it. On the same right-hand side the
dual cycle does not converge on $12^4$ in setup~S.

\paragraph{What did not work.} We tried several other ways to solve with $D\dg$ from one setup.
None is as good as $\Gfive\mathcal M\Gfive$.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The exact map.} Eq.~\eqref{eq:g5exact} gives the null vectors of $D\dg$ as
$(D_-\dg)^{-1}\Gfive v$, and $D\dg w=r$ as a solve with $D$ followed by one with $D_-\dg$. Both
need $D_-^{-1}$, which does not converge (Sec.~\ref{sec:g5fail}).
\item \emph{Other prolongations for the dual cycle.} $\Pro$ from $y\cup\Gfive y$ converges
(8--11 iterations) but doubles $\Pro$ and quadruples the coarse operators. $\Pro$ from the left
singular vectors $\AR y$, with or without a bootstrap, or from a second setup of $\AR\dg$, needs
12--300 iterations and up to a full setup more.
\item \emph{Other coarse solvers in the dual cycle.} Its stagnation comes from the Pauli--Villars
factor, solved by BiCGStab: with GCR(4) there it converges, but each cycle is three times as
expensive. MINRES on the $\Gfive$-Hermitian form does not help, since that form is wrong at
order $c$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What these solves are for.} In HMC each force evaluation needs one solve with $D$ and one
with $D\dg$, and with $\Gfive\mathcal M\Gfive$ one setup serves both. The solves with $D\dg$ were
measured on $12^4$ and $16^4$ only, and the cost of the multigrid in HMC is left to future work
(Sec.~\ref{sec:open}).

\subsection{Why the method converges}
\label{sec:why}

\subsubsection{Field of values: the coarse correction amplifies}
\label{sec:fov}

The four parts of this subsection ask why the method converges, as it does in
Sec.~\ref{sec:massscan}, although the usual theory does not apply. Two notions from non-Hermitian linear algebra are used. The \emph{field
of values} $W(X)=\{z\dg Xz/z\dg z\}$ contains the spectrum, and its leftmost point is the
smallest eigenvalue of the Hermitian part $(X+X\dg)/2$; GCR is guaranteed to converge at
every restart length when $W$ lies in the right half-plane~\cite{ESS}. The
\emph{$\varepsilon$-pseudospectrum} is the set of $z$ where $\sigma_{\min}(z-X)<\varepsilon$,
i.e.\ where $\|(z-X)^{-1}\|>1/\varepsilon$; for a normal $X$ it is the
$\varepsilon$-neighborhood of the spectrum, for a non-normal one it can be much larger,
and it bounds the convergence of full GMRES~\cite{TrefethenEmbree}.

The condition number of an eigenvalue, $\kappa=1/|y\dg x|$ for its right and left
eigenvectors $x$ and $y$ normalized to $\|x\|=\|y\|=1$, is $1$ for a normal matrix; a large
$\kappa$ means that the eigenvalue moves by $\kappa\varepsilon$ under a perturbation of size
$\varepsilon$, which widens the pseudospectrum around it (Secs.~\ref{sec:spectrum},
\ref{sec:pseudo}).

The coarse correction amplifies some vectors by $10^3$--$10^4$, and
the field of values of the preconditioned operator reaches into the left half-plane
(this subsection), so the bound of Eisenstat, Elman and Schultz~\cite{ESS} for GCR is
lost. Secs.~\ref{sec:spectrum}, \ref{sec:pseudo} and \ref{sec:gcrk} give what remains: the
eigenvalues, the pseudospectra and the truncated GCR used in practice.

The Hermitian part of $\AR\mathcal M$ was computed with $\mathcal M$ made a fixed
linear map: both coarse factors solved to $|r|^2/|b|^2<10^{-20}$ in double precision
with the exact adjoints, and $\mathcal M\dg$ built with the same $\Pro$ and the
same smoother interval, so that $\langle z|\AR\mathcal M w\rangle$ and
$\langle\mathcal M\dg\AR\dg z|w\rangle$ agree to $10^{-10}$. The cycle was also
taken apart: the smoother alone, $\mathcal S=p(\AR)$, the coarse correction alone, $E$
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:corr}, and the cycle $\mathcal M$ (Table~\ref{tab:fov}).

The coarse correction is what pushes the field of values into the left half-plane. On
$4^3\times8$ the field of values of $\AR\mathcal M$ lies in the right half-plane, whereas on $12^4$ and $16^4$ it does not. Table~\ref{tab:fov} shows where the change comes from.
\begin{itemize}
\item The field of values of $\AR$ itself hardly depends on the volume. From $12^4$ to $24^4$ its
upper edge moves by less than $1\%$, and its lower edge goes from $-4.6\times10^{-3}$ to
$-1.3\times10^{-2}$ (S) and stays at $-5\times10^{-2}$ (B).
\item The smoother is harmless: $\|I-\AR\mathcal S\|\approx1$.
\item The coarse correction amplifies: $\|I-\AR E\|$ is $10^3$--$10^4$, against $18.5$ on
$4^3\times8$. $\AR E$ is the identity on the vectors the correction is built from
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact}), but it amplifies other directions by three to four orders of
magnitude, like a strongly oblique projection.
\end{itemize}
Eick and Mawhinney~\cite{EickMawhinney} describe the same mechanism for a renormalization-group
coarse lattice. The coarse inverse is large on the low modes of the coarse operator. The
interpolated correction is not exactly a low mode of the fine operator, and the part that misses
comes back amplified. In their case the fine operator is $D\dg D$, whose spectrum reaches $520$
against $0.05$ for its lowest modes; it supplies the four orders of magnitude, and they need a
Chebyshev filter of degree $150$--$300$. In our case $\AR$ is close to the identity on the high
modes, because the Pauli--Villars operator divides them out, and its field of values ends at $2$ (S)
and $4.5$ (B). The large factor is the coarse inverse itself. The post-smoothing, a Chebyshev
polynomial of degree $7$--$11$ on the upper part of the spectrum, acts as the filter, and the
whole cycle keeps $\|I-\AR\mathcal M\|$ at $55$ (S) and $5.6$ (B).

So the GCR bound of Ref.~\cite{ESS} does not apply. The field of values is also not what decides
the iteration count. If we replace $E$ by the Galerkin correction of $\AR$ itself,
$\Pro(\Pro\dg\AR\Pro)^{-1}\Pro\dg$ (built densely), the field of values of setup~B moves into the
right half-plane, $[0.121,1.99]$, and $\|I-\AR\mathcal M\|$ drops to $1.84$ (setup~S:
$[-3.62,13.1]$, $16.8$). Yet GCR(8) to $10^{-16}$ then needs more iterations: $16$ against $13$
(B) and $12$ against $10$ (S).

\subsubsection{Pseudospectra and a bound for GMRES}
\label{sec:pseudo}

For a non-normal operator the eigenvalues alone do not bound the convergence of
GMRES; the $\varepsilon$-pseudospectrum, the set where $\sigma_{\min}(z-X)<\varepsilon$,
does~\cite{NRT,TrefethenEmbree}. If a contour $\Gamma$ of length $L$ encloses the
spectrum of $X$ but not the origin and $\sigma_{\min}(z-X)\ge\varepsilon$ on it, then
for any polynomial $p_k$ with $p_k(0)=1$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\|r_k\|}{\|r_0\|}\le\|p_k(X)\|\le\frac{L}{2\pi\varepsilon}\max_{z\in\Gamma}|p_k(z)| .
\label{eq:pseudobound}
\end{equation}
An isolated outlier $\lambda_o$ outside $\Gamma$ is removed by the factor
$(1-z/\lambda_o)$ of $p_k$: then $p_k(X)=p_k(X)(I-P_o)$ with $P_o$ the spectral
projector, $\|(z-X)^{-1}(I-P_o)\|\le\kappa_o/\varepsilon$, and with a circle
$|z-c|=r$ and $p_k(z)=(1-z/\lambda_o)(1-z/c)^{k-1}$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\|r_k\|}{\|r_0\|}\le\kappa_o\,\frac{r}{\varepsilon}\,
\frac{|\lambda_o-c|+r}{|\lambda_o|}\Big(\frac rc\Big)^{k-1}.
\label{eq:outlierbound}
\end{equation}

The bound guarantees convergence at a fixed rate, $r/c$ per iteration, with no stagnation. It
is pessimistic by a factor of $3$--$5$ in iterations: GCR needs $10$--$17$ with exact coarse
solves (Sec.~\ref{sec:gcrk}). Its rate, however, is the one we measure on $24^3\times48$ on configurations 1 and 3. There
the median factor per GCR step of $|r|^2$ is $0.23$--$0.34$ (Table~\ref{tab:history}), that is
$0.48$--$0.58$ in $|r|$, against $r/c=0.5$--$0.6$ for setup~S. Configuration~2 in setup~S converges
more slowly, at a steady $0.56$ per step in $|r|^2$. Setup~B does not get worse from
$12^4$ to $16^4$ ($\min\sigma$ grows from $0.098$ to $0.13$). In setup~S the outliers of configuration~1 grow, but
their condition numbers fall and $\varepsilon$ stays the same.

\paragraph{What is not yet rigorous.} On the two dense circles of $12^4$ the spacing of the
points is below $\varepsilon$, and since $\sigma_{\min}(z-X)$ is $1$-Lipschitz in $z$ the bound
holds on the whole circle, $\sigma_{\min}\ge0.0164$ (S) and $\ge0.0676$ (B), with $54$ and $58$
guaranteed iterations. The circles of $16^4$ have only $16$ points. The Lanczos estimates approach
$\sigma_{\min}^2$ from above, the disc rests on the eigenvalues farthest from $c$ found by Arnoldi,
and the bound is for full GMRES with a fixed $\mathcal M$ (Secs.~\ref{sec:gcrk}, \ref{sec:open}).

Truncated GCR does not need accurate coarse solves, and with them it can fail. With the usual
coarse solves to $10^{-2}$, GCR(2) needs one to five iterations more than the full method on
$12^4$ (Table~\ref{tab:gcrk}). With accurate coarse solves, where $\mathcal M$ is close to the
fixed map of Secs.~\ref{sec:fov}--\ref{sec:pseudo}, GCR(2) stagnates there in setup~S on
all four configurations ($|r|^2\approx3\times10^{-8}$ after 1000 iterations on configuration~1),
while GCR(8) converges in $17$--$26$ on three of them and GCR($\ge16$) in $15$--$17$ on all four. The pseudospectra guarantee the full method; a truncated method has no
guarantee when the field of values contains the origin~\cite{ESS,Embree}. On $16^4$ and $24^4$
GCR(2) converges with the same accurate solves (Table~\ref{tab:stall}).

Inexact coarse solves do not keep GCR(2) converging on $12^4$ by leaving out the directions in
which $E$ amplifies. Take the vector $x$ at the left end of the field of values of $\AR\mathcal M$
(exact coarse solves, $12^4$). With coarse solves to $10^{-2}$ the cycle gives
$\Re\,x\dg\AR\mathcal Mx=-25.2$ (S) and $-1.43$ (B), against $-24.3$ and $-1.49$ with exact
ones, and $\|(I-\AR E)x\|=1.16\times10^3$ and $1.03\times10^2$, against $1.13\times10^3$ and
$1.12\times10^2$. With tolerances $10^{-8}$ and $10^{-4}$ they agree with the exact solves to within
$1\%$. What the tolerance changes is that the cycle is no longer a fixed linear map: with Krylov
coarse solves stopped early, it depends on its input.

\paragraph{How GCR(2) stagnates.} The stagnation is a fixed point of GCR(2) with a fixed
preconditioner. On $12^4$ in setup~S, with three right-hand sides, $|r|^2$ falls for about $15$
steps and then does not change in any digit for the next $480$, at $2\times10^{-11}$,
$6\times10^{-8}$ and $5\times10^{-10}$. Meanwhile
$|\langle r|\AL\mathcal Mr\rangle|/(\|r\|\,\|\AL\mathcal Mr\|)$ falls from about $0.8$ to
$10^{-10}$--$10^{-9}$. The new direction is then orthogonal to the residual, and since
$\mathcal M$ is fixed the next step repeats it. A field of values that contains the origin admits
such a residual. Any variation of the cycle leaves the fixed point: on configuration~1 of $12^4$ the exact
cycle and two fixed perturbations of it stagnate in all $15$ runs, and random perturbations, down to $2\%$
of the cycle, converge in all $75$ (Table~\ref{tab:stall}). On the four quenched $16^4$
configurations, on the dynamical one and on $24^4$ the exact cycle does not reach a fixed point in
the runs made. A fixed perturbation
can still produce one there ($\delta\Gfive\mathcal Mr$ on $24^4$), so whether a fixed cycle
stagnates depends on the cycle and on the lattice. Of the four $12^4$ configurations, 1 and 4 reach it and 2 and 3 do
not, so the fixed point depends on the configuration; which property of it decides is open. It is
neither the topological charge (configurations 3 and 4 both have $Q=+2$, and $24^4$ has $Q=+4$ and
converges) nor the odd extent of the $3^4$ coarse lattice ($3^4$ aggregates give a $4^4$ coarse
lattice and stagnate as well).

\paragraph{Dependence on the setup seed.} On $12^4$ with stout smearing about one setup in eight
makes GCR(2) slow. Without smearing, in setup~B, and on $16^4$ none of the setups tested is; on
$24^3\times48$ one configuration is slow with every seed. The setup filters random starting vectors; their seed ($21$ in all other runs) changes the coarse
space but not the configuration, the operator or the right-hand side, and the solution agrees to the
stopping tolerance.
Table~\ref{tab:seeds} repeats the solve at $m=0.001$ with many seeds. On $12^4$ it uses four
configurations of Table~\ref{tab:configs}. In
setup~S, $19$ of $160$ setups need more than $30$ iterations and $13$ of them more than $200$ or do not
converge in $2000$. The rate per configuration, $7$, $6$, $4$ and $2$ of $40$ (configurations 1, 3, 2, 4), does
not distinguish the configurations at these counts. The same configurations with $M_5=1$ and no
smearing give $0$ of $40$, and setup~B gives $0$ of $80$. On $16^4$ setup~S gives $0$ of $60$ on
three quenched configurations and $0$ of $20$ on the dynamical one, and on $24^3\times48$ seeds 1--5
need $18$--$28$ iterations on configurations 3 and 4. Configuration~2 of $24^3\times48$ needs
$53$, $36$ and $45$ iterations with seeds 21, 7 and 99, against $18$--$28$ on the other three; that
excess is shared by all three seeds and so belongs to the configuration.

A slow setup gives a cycle of the usual quality, and GCR(2) fails to use it. With the same cycles
GCR(16) needs $11$--$14$ iterations in all $40$ setups tested. In the slow runs $|r|^2$ falls normally
for a few steps and then stays nearly constant, for up to $300$ steps at
$|r|^2/|b|^2\approx7\times10^{-10}$ on configuration~1 with seed 2, before it falls again; this is the behavior
near a fixed point of Table~\ref{tab:stall}, reached here with inexact coarse solves. The slowness
belongs to the setup: five of the six slow setups of the first ten seeds are slow for the point
source as well ($44$ to more than $500$ iterations). At $L_s=12$ and $16$ the same occurs, in $7$ of
$20$ setups on configurations 2 and 4. Why smearing on the small lattice produces these setups is
open.

GCR(8) and $48$ test vectors remove the slow setups on $12^4$ but do not fit $24^3\times48$ in
$24$\,GB: each kept direction costs two fine vectors ($1$\,GB there; GCR(4) peaks at $22.1$ against
$20.2$\,GB), and the setup with $48$ test vectors runs out of device memory. Coarse solves for the
quark factor to $10^{-4}$ cost no memory and leave $2$ of $160$ setups slow. On $24^3\times48$ they
make each iteration $22$--$40\%$ more expensive: configuration~2 drops to $43$, $33$ and $33$
iterations at about the same solve time, and the other three configurations take $9$--$34\%$ longer. A slow setup shows itself in the first solve as a plateau of $|r|^2$; the setup can then be repeated
with another seed on the same configuration, at the cost of one more setup. We have not automated
this.

\paragraph{What this establishes.} As usual for multigrid preconditioners of lattice Dirac
operators, the convergence of the truncated flexible outer method is established numerically.
GCR(2) with coarse solves stopped at $10^{-2}$ converged without plateaus in every run of this
section on $16^4$ and $24^3\times48$, mostly at the rate the pseudospectral bound of
Sec.~\ref{sec:pseudo} gives for the full method; on $12^4$ with smearing about one setup in eight
reaches a plateau (Table~\ref{tab:seeds}). No guarantee is available for a truncated method whose preconditioner varies from step to
step. Accurate coarse solves do not make the solver faster. On $16^4$ the exact cycle takes
$24$--$36$ iterations on the four configurations, as many as BiCGStab on the exact adjoint stopped
at $10^{-2}$ ($37$--$47$ on configuration~1). The solver's cycle takes $11$--$13$. It differs from the exact one in two ways, the
$\Sigma$-adjoint $\Sigma\Dcpv\Sigma$ in place of $(\Dcpv)\dg$ and MINRES stopped at $10^{-2}$. The
first is not the whole difference: at $c=0$, where the two adjoints coincide, MINRES still needs
$13$ iterations against $25$ with BiCGStab on $12^4$ (medians, Table~\ref{tab:minres}). The cycle of
the solver converged in every run of Table~\ref{tab:stall}.
