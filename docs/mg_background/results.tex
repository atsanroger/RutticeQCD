\paragraph{Numerical setup.} The quenched configurations use the plaquette action at $\beta=6.0$,
a lattice spacing of about $0.1$~fm (Table~\ref{tab:configs}). Results are means, medians or
ranges over the four configurations of each volume as the captions state; the solvers without the
multigrid and the pseudospectra use configuration~1. The two setups of the M\"obius operator
(Table~\ref{tab:setups}) share $(b,c)=(1.5,0.5)$, the choice of the JLQCD and the RBC/UKQCD
Collaborations, and $L_s=8$.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Setup B (bare links)} uses $M_5=1.8$, the value typical of simulations on unsmeared
links~\cite{Arthur2013}.
\item \emph{Setup S (stout links)} smears the links of the fermion operator (stout, $\rho=0.1$, three
steps, Sec.~\ref{sec:smearing}) and uses $M_5=1.0$, as in the JLQCD ensembles of
Ref.~\cite{JLQCDBpi}.
\end{itemize}
Both keep the near-zero modes of $W=D_W(-M_5)$ small, by moving $M_5$ (B) or by smoothing the
field (S), and so give the multigrid low modes of different character; they need different
smoother intervals (Table~\ref{tab:setups}). Timings are from one NVIDIA data-center GPU with
$24$\,GB of device memory; a second GPU was used for iteration counts and spectral quantities only.
The device reductions add in an order that depends on the build and the
GPU, so the iterations of otherwise equal runs can differ by a few. The setup seed is $21$ except
in Sec.~\ref{sec:pvcoarse}.

\subsection{Performance}
\label{sec:performance}

\subsubsection{Comparison with CGNE}
\label{sec:massscan}

On $24^3\times48$ the multigrid is $2.9$--$6.0$ times faster than mixed-precision CGNE at
$m=0.001$, and its setup is recovered within one point-source propagator for $m\le0.005$. We
compare first for one random right-hand side over seven masses, then for whole point-source
propagators.

\paragraph{The reference solver.} It is CG on $\mathcal K\dg\mathcal K$, $\mathcal K=\Ce^{-1}S$,
the even-odd operator of Bridge++, with Neff's $\alpha$ as for the multigrid, in mixed precision.
Boyle and Yamaguchi~\cite{BY} used CG on $S\dg S$, which here needs $21\%$ (B) to $49\%$ (S) more
iterations (Appendix~\ref{sec:cgform}).

\paragraph{Mass dependence.} The multigrid removes most of the critical slowing down of CGNE
(Fig.~\ref{fig:massscan}, Table~\ref{tab:massscan}). From $m=0.1$ to $m=0.001$ the iterations of
CGNE grow about twentyfold, those of the multigrid by $1.6$--$4.1$. Its setup of about $210$\,s is
paid back after $3.5$--$5.2$ right-hand sides at $m=0.001$. For $m\ge0.05$ in setup~S, CGNE is
faster or level. The multigrid needs three times the device memory of CGNE.

\paragraph{A point-source propagator.} At $m=0.001$ the multigrid takes $0.40$--$0.56$ of the wall
time of CGNE for a whole propagator, setup included (Table~\ref{tab:pointsource}). At $m=0.01$ the
setup is not quite paid back within the 12 columns. The pseudoscalar correlators of the two solvers
agree to $1\times10^{-6}$ or better.

\subsubsection{Solves with $D\dg$}
\label{sec:daggerresults}
\paragraph{Single solves.} Table~\ref{tab:c4} compares three ways to solve with $D\dg$ from one
setup of $D$. The right-hand sides are a random vector and the residual
$r=b-D\dg Dx_0$ after one sweep of the normal equations, solved to $10^{-10}$. The second kind
is the one that matters in HMC: its error lies mostly on the lowest modes.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The cycle of $\AR\dg$ on the same $\Pro$} (the ``dual cycle''): the factorized
correction~\eqref{eq:corr} with $D$ and $\Dpv$ exchanged.
\item \emph{Flexible GCR on $D\dg$ with $\Gfive\mathcal M\Gfive$}, Eq.~\eqref{eq:c4}, one cycle a
step.
\end{itemize}

On $16^4$ the preconditioner $\Gfive\mathcal M\Gfive$ needs two to three iterations more than the solve
with $D$, in both setups and on both kinds of right-hand side, as the heuristic predicts. In
setup~S more inner cycles per step do not reduce the count, so the extra steps come from $N$ and
not from the inexact $\mathcal M$. The dual cycle stalls on the residual right-hand side in setup~S
on $12^4$ and on three of the four $16^4$ configurations.

On $12^4$ in setup~S, $m=0.001$, the residual right-hand side stalls GCR(2) on all four
configurations, and GCR(8) needs $13$--$222$ iterations. On $16^4$, where configurations 2--4 have
$Q=+1$, $+1$ and $+3$, GCR(2) needs $9$--$10$ on all four. The stall follows the small smeared
lattice of Sec.~\ref{sec:gcrk}, which we infer from the coincidence only: no $12^4$ run without smearing
was made for $D\dg$. The topological charge does not predict it. On the same right-hand side the
dual cycle does not converge on $12^4$ in setup~S.

\paragraph{What did not work.} None of the other ways we tried to solve with $D\dg$ from one setup
is as good as $\Gfive\mathcal M\Gfive$. The exact map of Eq.~\eqref{eq:g5exact} needs $D_-^{-1}$,
which does not converge (Sec.~\ref{sec:g5fail}). For the dual cycle, $\Pro$ from $y\cup\Gfive y$
converges but quadruples the coarse operators, other prolongations need up to $300$ iterations, and
GCR(4) in place of BiCGStab for its Pauli--Villars factor converges at three times the cost per
cycle.

\paragraph{What these solves are for.} In HMC each force evaluation needs one solve with $D$ and one
with $D\dg$, and with $\Gfive\mathcal M\Gfive$ one setup serves both. The cost in HMC is left to
future work (Sec.~\ref{sec:open}).


\subsection{What each part contributes}
\label{sec:parts}

The speed-up of Sec.~\ref{sec:massscan} rests on four choices beyond the operator $A'$: the coarse
correction, the lift $\hat t$ in the basis, the regularized Pauli--Villars solve,
and the setup parameters. Each part below isolates one of them.

\subsubsection{Krylov on $\AL$ without the multigrid}
\label{sec:nomg}

The speed-up comes from the coarse correction: without it a Krylov method on $\AL$ does not beat
CGNE (Table~\ref{tab:nomg}). BiCGStab on $\AL$ alone needs about as many iterations as
double-precision CGNE, but each of its iterations applies $\AL$ twice, and at $m=0.001$ in setup~S
it takes $1.7$ times the time of double-precision CGNE. The Chebyshev smoother as a preconditioner cuts its iterations but
raises the time.


\subsubsection{Choice of the prolongation basis}
\label{sec:basischoice}
The exactness~\eqref{eq:exact} needs both lifts $s$ and $\hat t$ of each $y$ in
$\operatorname{range}\Pro$, but the left form used in practice is not exact
(Sec.~\ref{sec:basis}). To see what $\hat t$ does there, we replace it by $Ds$, which
vanishes on the odd sites, or drop it (Table~\ref{tab:basischoice}). With $\{s\}$ alone $\Pro$
has half the columns; to compare at the same size we also take $32$ vectors $y$, which doubles
the vectors of the filter and of the bootstrap.


With the same vectors $y$, the two lifts give the fewest iterations on every volume. In setup~S,
replacing $\hat t$ by $Ds$ or dropping it stagnates on three of the four $12^4$ and $16^4$
configurations and costs five times the iterations on $24^3\times48$; in setup~B it costs $1.2$ to
$2.2$ times the iterations. Twice as many vectors $y$ with $\{s\}$ alone take the place of $\hat t$ on
$24^3\times48$ but not on $12^4$, at $1.6$--$1.8$ times the setup time and twice the host memory; a
point-source propagator is still faster with the two lifts in both setups.

\subsubsection{The regularized Pauli--Villars solve}
\label{sec:pvcoarse}

With the regularized solve no slow setups remain on $12^4$: all $40$ setups of setup~S need
$10$--$17$ iterations, against $70$ to more than $2000$ for the slow ones without it
(Table~\ref{tab:tikhonov}). The cause is the Galerkin factor. With stout smearing its smallest
singular value lies one to two orders of magnitude below that of the fine $\Dpv$; without smearing
the two agree (Table~\ref{tab:pvsv}).

\subsubsection{Sensitivity to the parameters}
\label{sec:sensitivity}


The method is robust within a window of each parameter and fails outside it
(Fig.~\ref{fig:sensitivity}, Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity}). The smoother interval must suit the
setup, the bootstrap is necessary, and more accurate coarse solves do not help.

Fewer iterations are not always less time (Table~\ref{tab:costs}). $2^4$ aggregates need $8$--$9$
iterations, but the larger coarse lattice makes the solve and the setup two to three times as long
as with $4^4$; $48$ test vectors likewise save iterations and lose in setup and time per iteration.
On $24^3\times48$, $16$ test vectors do not suffice: the solve needs hundreds of iterations or does
not converge in $2000$.

Whyte, Stathopoulos and Romero~\cite{WhyteSVD} obtain, for Wilson fermions, the effect of more test
vectors at the coarse size of fewer by truncating them with a singular value decomposition on each
aggregate. Here the truncated basis would no longer contain $s$ and $\hat t$ exactly; whether the
gain outweighs that loss is open.

\subsection{Why the method converges}
\label{sec:why}

\subsubsection{Field of values: the coarse correction amplifies}
\label{sec:fov}

The method converges as in Sec.~\ref{sec:massscan} although the usual theory does not apply. The
four parts of this subsection ask which property of the preconditioned operator sets the convergence. Two notions from non-Hermitian linear algebra are used. The \emph{field
of values} $W(X)=\{z\dg Xz/z\dg z\}$ contains the spectrum, and its leftmost point is the
smallest eigenvalue of the Hermitian part $(X+X\dg)/2$; GCR is guaranteed to converge at
every restart length when $W$ lies in the right half-plane~\cite{ESS}. The
\emph{$\varepsilon$-pseudospectrum} is the set of $z$ where $\sigma_{\min}(z-X)<\varepsilon$,
i.e.\ where $\|(z-X)^{-1}\|>1/\varepsilon$; for a normal $X$ it is the
$\varepsilon$-neighborhood of the spectrum, for a non-normal one it can be much larger,
and it bounds the convergence of full GMRES~\cite{TrefethenEmbree}.

The condition number of an eigenvalue, $\kappa=1/|y\dg x|$ for its right and left
eigenvectors $x$ and $y$ normalized to $\|x\|=\|y\|=1$, is $1$ for a normal matrix; a large
$\kappa$ means that the eigenvalue moves by $\kappa\varepsilon$ under a perturbation of size
$\varepsilon$, which widens the pseudospectrum around it (Secs.~\ref{sec:spectrum},
\ref{sec:pseudo}).

The coarse correction amplifies some vectors by $10^3$--$10^5$, and
the field of values of the preconditioned operator reaches into the left half-plane
(this subsection), so the bound of Eisenstat, Elman and Schultz~\cite{ESS} for GCR is
lost. Secs.~\ref{sec:spectrum}, \ref{sec:pseudo} and \ref{sec:gcrk} give what remains: the
eigenvalues, the pseudospectra and the truncated GCR used in practice.

The Hermitian part of $\AR\mathcal M$ was computed with $\mathcal M$ made a fixed
linear map: both coarse factors solved to $|r|^2/|b|^2<10^{-20}$ in double precision
with the exact adjoints, and $\mathcal M\dg$ built with the same $\Pro$ and the
same smoother interval, so that $\langle z|\AR\mathcal M w\rangle$ and
$\langle\mathcal M\dg\AR\dg z|w\rangle$ agree to $10^{-10}$. The cycle was also
taken apart: the smoother alone, $\mathcal S=p(\AR)$, the coarse correction alone, $E$
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:corr}, and the cycle $\mathcal M$ (Table~\ref{tab:fov}).


The coarse correction is what pushes the field of values into the left half-plane. On
$4^3\times8$ the field of values of $\AR\mathcal M$ lies in the right half-plane, whereas on $12^4$ and $16^4$ it does not. Table~\ref{tab:fov} shows where the change comes from.
\begin{itemize}
\item The field of values of $\AR$ itself hardly depends on the volume. From $12^4$ to $24^4$ its
upper edge moves by less than $1\%$, and its lower edge goes from $-4.6\times10^{-3}$ to
$-1.3\times10^{-2}$ (S) and stays at $-5\times10^{-2}$ (B).
\item The smoother is harmless: $\|I-\AR\mathcal S\|\approx1$.
\item The coarse correction amplifies: $\|I-\AR E\|$ is $10^3$--$10^5$ (up to $3\times10^5$ on the four
$12^4$ configurations), against $18.5$ on $4^3\times8$. $\AR E$ is the identity on the vectors the correction is built from
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact}), but it amplifies other directions by three to four orders of
magnitude, like a strongly oblique projection.
\end{itemize}
Eick and Mawhinney~\cite{EickMawhinney} describe the same mechanism for a renormalization-group
coarse lattice. The coarse inverse is large on the low modes of the coarse operator. The
interpolated correction is not exactly a low mode of the fine operator, and the part that misses
comes back amplified. In their case the fine operator is $D\dg D$, whose spectrum reaches $520$
against $0.05$ for its lowest modes; it supplies the four orders of magnitude, and they need a
Chebyshev filter of degree $150$--$300$. In our case $\AR$ is close to the identity on the high
modes, because the Pauli--Villars operator divides them out, and its field of values ends at $2$ (S)
and $4.5$ (B). The large factor is the coarse inverse itself. The post-smoothing, a Chebyshev
polynomial of degree $7$--$11$ on the upper part of the spectrum, acts as the filter, and the
whole cycle keeps $\|I-\AR\mathcal M\|$ at $55$ (S) and $5.6$ (B) on configuration~1, and at $15$--$722$
over the four configurations in~S.

So the GCR bound of Ref.~\cite{ESS} does not apply. The field of values is also not what decides
the iteration count. If we replace $E$ by the Galerkin correction of $\AR$ itself,
$\Pro(\Pro\dg\AR\Pro)^{-1}\Pro\dg$ (built densely), the field of values of setup~B moves into the
right half-plane, $[0.121,1.99]$, and $\|I-\AR\mathcal M\|$ drops to $1.84$ (setup~S:
$[-3.62,13.1]$, $16.8$). Yet GCR(8) to $10^{-16}$ then needs more iterations: $16$ against $13$
(B) and $12$ against $10$ (S).

\subsubsection{Spectrum: far from the origin}
\label{sec:spectrum}


The spectrum, unlike the field of values, stays far from the origin. Every eigenvalue lies at
$\Re\lambda\ge0.69$, with moderate condition numbers (Table~\ref{tab:spectrum}, Fig.~\ref{fig:spectrum}). On
configuration~1 setup~S has one ($12^4$) or two ($16^4$) isolated outliers of large modulus, and all
other eigenvalues lie within $0.43$--$0.47$ of $1$; on configurations 2--4 of $16^4$ the eigenvalue
farthest from $1$ lies at $|\lambda|=2.7$--$4.6$. The left and right forms have the same spectrum to
the precision shown. At $m=0.005$ on $16^4$ the lowest real parts are $0.802$ (S) and $0.790$
(B).

\subsubsection{Pseudospectra and a bound for GMRES}
\label{sec:pseudo}

For a non-normal operator the eigenvalues alone do not bound the convergence of
GMRES; the $\varepsilon$-pseudospectrum, the set where $\sigma_{\min}(z-X)<\varepsilon$,
does~\cite{NRT,TrefethenEmbree}. If a contour $\Gamma$ of length $L$ encloses the
spectrum of $X$ but not the origin and $\sigma_{\min}(z-X)\ge\varepsilon$ on it, then
for any polynomial $p_k$ with $p_k(0)=1$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\|r_k\|}{\|r_0\|}\le\|p_k(X)\|\le\frac{L}{2\pi\varepsilon}\max_{z\in\Gamma}|p_k(z)| .
\label{eq:pseudobound}
\end{equation}
An isolated outlier $\lambda_o$ outside $\Gamma$ is removed by the factor
$(1-z/\lambda_o)$ of $p_k$: then $p_k(X)=p_k(X)(I-P_o)$ with $P_o$ the spectral
projector, $\|(z-X)^{-1}(I-P_o)\|\le\kappa_o/\varepsilon$, and with a circle
$|z-c|=r$ and $p_k(z)=(1-z/\lambda_o)(1-z/c)^{k-1}$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\|r_k\|}{\|r_0\|}\le\kappa_o\,\frac{r}{\varepsilon}\,
\frac{|\lambda_o-c|+r}{|\lambda_o|}\Big(\frac rc\Big)^{k-1}.
\label{eq:outlierbound}
\end{equation}

On $16^4$ setup~S has two outliers; the factor $\kappa_o$ is then replaced by
$\|I-P_1-P_2\|\le1+\kappa_1+\kappa_2$ and there are two factors of the
form $(|\lambda_o-c|+r)/|\lambda_o|$. On the real axis between the origin and the
spectrum, $\sigma_{\min}(z-\AR\mathcal M)\ge0.024$ (S) and $\ge0.031$ (B) on
$12^4$ ($z=-0.5\ldots0.7$), and $0.107$ (S), $0.271$ (B) at the origin.

The bound guarantees convergence at a fixed rate, $r/c$ per iteration, with no stagnation. It
is pessimistic by a factor of $3$--$5$ in iterations: GCR needs $10$--$17$ with exact coarse
solves (Sec.~\ref{sec:gcrk}). Its rate, however, is the one we measure on $24^3\times48$ on configurations 1 and 3. There
the median factor per GCR step of $|r|^2$ is $0.23$--$0.34$ (Table~\ref{tab:history}), that is
$0.48$--$0.58$ in $|r|$, against $r/c=0.5$--$0.6$ for setup~S. Configuration~2 in setup~S converges
more slowly, at a steady $0.56$ per step in $|r|^2$. Setup~B does not get worse from
$12^4$ to $16^4$ ($\min\sigma$ grows from $0.098$ to $0.13$). In setup~S the outliers of configuration~1 grow, but
their condition numbers fall and $\varepsilon$ stays the same.

\paragraph{What is not yet rigorous.} On the two dense circles of $12^4$ the spacing of the
points is below $\varepsilon$, and since $\sigma_{\min}(z-X)$ is $1$-Lipschitz in $z$ the bound
holds on the whole circle, $\sigma_{\min}\ge0.0164$ (S) and $\ge0.0676$ (B), with $54$ and $58$
guaranteed iterations. The circles of $16^4$ have only $16$ points. The Lanczos estimates approach
$\sigma_{\min}^2$ from above, the disc rests on the eigenvalues farthest from $c$ found by Arnoldi,
and the bound is for full GMRES with a fixed $\mathcal M$ (Secs.~\ref{sec:gcrk}, \ref{sec:open}).

\subsubsection{Truncated GCR: inexact coarse solves}
\label{sec:gcrk}


Truncated GCR does not need accurate coarse solves, and with them it can fail. With the usual
coarse solves to $10^{-2}$, GCR(2) needs one to five iterations more than the full method on
$12^4$ (Table~\ref{tab:gcrk}). With coarse solves to $10^{-8}$, where $\mathcal M$ is close to the
fixed map of Secs.~\ref{sec:fov}--\ref{sec:pseudo}, GCR(2) stagnates in setup~S on
all four configurations ($|r|^2\approx3\times10^{-8}$ after 1000 iterations on configuration~1),
while GCR(8) converges in $17$--$26$ on three of them and GCR($\ge16$) in $15$--$17$ on all four. The pseudospectra guarantee the full method; a truncated method has no
guarantee when the field of values contains the origin~\cite{ESS,Embree}. With the exact cycle (double precision, $10^{-20}$) GCR(2) stagnates only on configurations 1 and~4
of $12^4$; on $16^4$ and $24^4$ it converges (Table~\ref{tab:stall}).

Inexact coarse solves do not keep GCR(2) converging on $12^4$ by leaving out the directions in
which $E$ amplifies. Take the vector $x$ at the left end of the field of values of $\AR\mathcal M$
(exact coarse solves, $12^4$). With coarse solves to $10^{-2}$ the cycle gives
$\Re\,x\dg\AR\mathcal Mx=-25.2$ (S) and $-1.43$ (B), against $-24.3$ and $-1.49$ with exact
ones, and $\|(I-\AR E)x\|=1.16\times10^3$ and $1.03\times10^2$, against $1.13\times10^3$ and
$1.12\times10^2$. With tolerances $10^{-8}$ and $10^{-4}$ they agree with the exact solves to within
$1\%$. What the tolerance changes is that the cycle is no longer a fixed linear map: with Krylov
coarse solves stopped early, it depends on its input.

\paragraph{How GCR(2) stagnates.} GCR(2) with a fixed preconditioner can reach a fixed point. On
$12^4$ in setup~S, $|r|^2$ falls for about $15$ steps and then does not change in any digit for the
next $480$. Meanwhile
$|\langle r|\AL\mathcal Mr\rangle|/(\|r\|\,\|\AL\mathcal Mr\|)$ falls from about $0.8$ to
$10^{-10}$--$10^{-9}$. The new direction is then orthogonal to the residual, and since
$\mathcal M$ is fixed the next step repeats it. A field of values that contains the origin admits
such a residual. Any variation of the cycle leaves the fixed point: on configuration~1 of $12^4$ the exact
cycle and two fixed perturbations of it stagnate in all $15$ runs, and random perturbations, down to $2\%$
of the cycle, converge in all $75$ (Table~\ref{tab:stall}). On the four quenched $16^4$
configurations, on the dynamical one and on $24^4$ the exact cycle does not reach a fixed point in
the runs made. A fixed perturbation
can still produce one there ($\delta\Gfive\mathcal Mr$ on $24^4$), so whether a fixed cycle
stagnates depends on the cycle and on the lattice. Of the four $12^4$ configurations, 1 and 4 reach it and 2 and 3 do
not, so the fixed point depends on the configuration; which property of it decides is open. It is
neither the topological charge (configurations 3 and 4 both have $Q=+2$, and $24^4$ has $Q=+4$ and
converges) nor the odd extent of the $3^4$ coarse lattice ($3^4$ aggregates give a $4^4$ coarse
lattice and stagnate as well).

\paragraph{Dependence on the setup seed.} On $12^4$ with stout smearing about one setup in eight
makes GCR(2) slow. The seed of the random starting vectors that the setup filters ($21$ in all other
runs) changes the coarse space but not the configuration, the operator or the right-hand side, and
the solution agrees to the stopping tolerance. Table~\ref{tab:seeds} repeats the solve at
$m=0.001$ with many seeds, on the four configurations of Table~\ref{tab:configs} for $12^4$. In
setup~S, $19$ of $160$ setups need more than $30$ iterations and $13$ of them more than $200$ or do not
converge in $2000$. This is $2$ to $7$ per configuration, which does not distinguish the configurations at these
counts. No setup is slow without smearing at $M_5=1$, in setup~B, on $16^4$ (quenched and
dynamical), or on $24^3\times48$ configurations 3 and 4, where five seeds need $18$--$28$ iterations. Configuration~2 of $24^3\times48$ needs
$36$--$53$ iterations with all three seeds, against $18$--$28$ on the other three, so that
excess belongs to the configuration and not to the seed.

A slow setup gives a cycle of the usual quality, and GCR(2) fails to use it. With the same cycles
GCR(16) needs $11$--$14$ iterations in all $40$ setups tested. In the slow runs $|r|^2$ falls normally
for a few steps and then stays nearly constant, for up to $300$ steps at
$|r|^2/|b|^2\approx7\times10^{-10}$ on configuration~1, before it falls again; this is the behavior
near a fixed point of Table~\ref{tab:stall}, reached here with inexact coarse solves. The slowness
belongs to the setup: five of the six slow setups of the first ten seeds are slow for the point
source as well ($44$ to more than $500$ iterations). At $L_s=12$ and $16$ the same occurs, in $7$ of
$20$ setups on configurations 2 and 4.

Smearing moves the field of values of the cycle across the origin on $12^4$. With exact coarse solves
and stout smearing, $\Re W(\AR\mathcal M)$ reaches $-6.3$ to $-324$ on the four configurations;
with $M_5=1$ and no smearing it lies in $[0.23,2.62]$ on all four (Table~\ref{tab:fov}). The
field of values of $\AR$ itself is the same in both cases. The difference is the coarse
correction: $\|I-\AR E\|$ is $4.7\times10^3$--$2.8\times10^5$ with smearing and $64$--$84$
without. With the origin outside the field of values, truncated GCR($k$) converges for every
$k$~\cite{ESS}, which matches the absence of slow setups without smearing. The converse does not
hold: $16^4$ in setup~S also reaches $-15.6$ and has no slow setups, and among the $12^4$
configurations the depth does not predict which exact cycles stagnate (configuration~2 reaches
$-21.9$ and converges). Why smearing makes the coarse correction this large on the small lattice is
open.

GCR(8) and $48$ test vectors remove the slow setups on $12^4$ but do not fit $24^3\times48$ in
$24$\,GB: each kept direction costs two fine vectors ($1$\,GB there; GCR(4) peaks at $22.1$ against
$20.2$\,GB), and the setup with $48$ test vectors runs out of device memory. Coarse solves for the
quark factor to $10^{-4}$ cost no memory and leave $2$ of $160$ setups slow. On $24^3\times48$ they
make each iteration $22$--$40\%$ more expensive: configuration~2 drops to $33$--$43$
iterations at about the same solve time, and the other three configurations take $9$--$34\%$ longer. A slow setup shows itself in the first solve as a plateau of $|r|^2$; the setup can then be repeated
with another seed on the same configuration, at the cost of one more setup. We have not automated
this.

On $24^3\times48$ no run shows a plateau (Table~\ref{tab:history}, Fig.~\ref{fig:history}); the first step reduces $|r|^2$ by

\paragraph{What this establishes.} As usual for multigrid preconditioners of lattice Dirac
operators, the convergence of the truncated flexible outer method is established numerically.
GCR(2) with coarse solves stopped at $10^{-2}$ converged without plateaus in every run of this
section on $16^4$ and $24^3\times48$, mostly at the rate the pseudospectral bound of
Sec.~\ref{sec:pseudo} gives for the full method; on $12^4$ with smearing about one setup in eight
reaches a plateau (Table~\ref{tab:seeds}). No guarantee is available for a truncated method whose preconditioner varies from step to
step. Accurate coarse solves do not make the solver faster. On $16^4$ the exact cycle takes
$24$--$36$ iterations on the four configurations, fewer than BiCGStab on the exact adjoint stopped
at $10^{-2}$ ($37$--$47$ against $33$--$36$ on configuration~1). The solver's cycle, with the
regularized Pauli--Villars solve, takes $10$--$11$. The cycle of
the solver converged in every run of Table~\ref{tab:stall}.

\paragraph{In short.} The convergence is set by the spectrum and the pseudospectra, not by the field of
values. The spectrum of $\AR\mathcal M$ stays at $\Re\lambda\ge0.69$ with moderate condition numbers,
and the pseudospectra give full GMRES a guaranteed rate of $0.5$--$0.6$ per iteration, close to the
$0.48$--$0.58$ measured for GCR(2) on $24^3\times48$. The field of values reaches into the left half-plane
because the coarse correction amplifies, and that loses the guarantee for truncated GCR. With the exact or the solver's
cycle, GCR(2) stagnated only on $12^4$ with smearing. On configuration~1 the amplification is $1.9\times10^4$ on $12^4$ and $1.5\times10^4$ on $16^4$
(Table~\ref{tab:fov}), and neither it nor the depth of the field of values alone decides which setups stagnate.
